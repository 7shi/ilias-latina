### 959 (*Iliad* 22.274–276)

> 959 quam praeterlapsam uitauit callidus Hector.
> (which clever Hector dodged as it flew past.)

Achilles’ powerful throw fails to end the duel. Hector, Troy’s defender, survives this attack through quick perception and movement rather than strength alone. After the crowded, exhausting struggle of the preceding lines, this brief evasion gives him a momentary reprieve: Achilles’ force has not yet overcome his opponent’s resourcefulness.

The Latin compresses Homer’s account into a single swift action. In the *Iliad*, Hector sees the spear coming and crouches; the bronze weapon flies overhead and sticks in the earth (*Iliad* 22.274–276). The Latin leaves out both the precise movement and the spear’s landing, but its description of Hector as “clever” draws attention to the alertness behind his escape. Where Homer calls him “glorious,” the Latin chooses a quality immediately relevant to his survival. Homer then begins to describe the goddess Athena retrieving Achilles’ spear; this verse keeps the focus entirely on Hector’s successful dodge.
