### 348 (*Iliad* 4.135–136)

> 348 incidit et tunicam ferro squamisque rigentem
> (struck, and the tunic stiff with iron scales...)

The arrow reaches Menelaus’s side, where armour stands between its point and his flesh. The “tunic” is a protective garment reinforced with overlapping metal scales, not simply a layer of clothing. The sentence remains unfinished, carrying the account of the arrow’s progress into the next verse.

Homer describes the missile passing through two successive defences: Menelaus’s elaborately worked belt and his richly fashioned breastplate (*Iliad* 4.135–136). The Latin condenses these details into the image of a tunic stiff with iron scales. Its emphasis falls less on the armour’s craftsmanship than on the hard material that must resist the shot.
