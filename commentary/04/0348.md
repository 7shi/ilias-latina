### 348 (*Iliad* 4.135–136)

> 348 incidit et tunicam ferro squamisque rigentem
> (fell, and the tunic stiff with iron and scales)

The arrow falls on Menelaus’s side, where armor stands between its point and his flesh. The “tunic” stiff with iron and scales is a protective garment of overlapping metal scales, not simply a layer of clothing. The sentence remains unfinished, carrying the account of the arrow’s progress into the next verse.

Homer describes the missile passing through two successive defences: Menelaus’s elaborately worked belt and his richly fashioned breastplate (*Iliad* 4.135–136). The Latin condenses these details into the image of a tunic stiff with iron and scales. Its emphasis falls less on the armor’s craftsmanship than on the hard material that must resist the shot.
