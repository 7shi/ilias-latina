While the chiefs contend, the almighty ruler of Olympus holds a divine council. Pandarus breaks the treaty by shooting at Menelaus: his arrow penetrates the iron-scaled tunic and wounds him. Menelaus, called Atrides, withdraws groaning to the camp, where the young Podalirius, trained in his father’s healing art, treats him with Paeonian herbs. He then returns victorious to the fighting. Agamemnon’s anger arms the brave Pelasgians, and shared grief drives the whole army into battle.

The fighting becomes general, with heavy losses among both Trojans and Danaans. Mavors, the war god, resounds everywhere amid showers of missiles. Antilochus kills Thalysiades with his sword. Telamonian Aiax strikes down Anthemion’s son, who is pressing the Greeks, piercing his breast. Antiphus then hurls his spear at Aeacides—the designation here for Aiax—but misses and strikes Leucon in the groin; Leucon dies biting the grass. “Tireless Atrides,” stirred by his friend’s death, kills Democoonta with a massive spear through the temples and then draws his sword as his opponent falls backwards. Pirous Imbrasides has meanwhile killed Amarynciden with a stone. As Pirous prepares to strip the dead youth, Thoas casts a spear through his shoulders and breast, leaving him face-down, bleeding and twitching on his own weapons.

The book ends with battle still raging across the blood-soaked Dardanian plains. The opposing armies are variously named Trojans, Danaans, Greeks, Pelasgians, Troiani, and Achiui. Neither side has secured victory: courage and advantage shift between Troiani and Achiui as both continue striving to prevail.
