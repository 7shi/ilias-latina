### 184 (*Iliad* 2.565, 2.564)

> 184 Euryalus Sthenelusque duces et fortis in armis
> (the leaders Euryalus and Sthenelus, and, brave in battle,)

The catalogue turns from northern Thessaly to the forces of Argos and its surrounding territory in the Peloponnese, southern Greece. Euryalus and Sthenelus share command with Diomedes, whose name is held over while the unfinished phrase “brave in battle” prepares his introduction.

Homer presents these commanders in the opposite order: Diomedes comes first, followed by Sthenelus and then Euryalus, described as a “godlike man” (*Iliad* 2.565). The Latin retains all three leaders but compresses their introduction, omitting Sthenelus’ parentage and Euryalus’ individual praise. Its emphasis instead falls on the martial prowess of Diomedes, who will become one of the most formidable Greek fighters in the battles ahead.
