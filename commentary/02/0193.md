### 193–194 (*Iliad* 2.678, 2.680)

> 193 Thessalici iuuenes Phidippus et Antiphus ibant
> (The Thessalian youths Phidippus and Antiphus went forth)
>
> 194 altaque ter denis pulsarunt aequora proris
> (and struck the deep seas with thirty prows.)

The catalogue continues with Phidippus and Antiphus, two brothers who jointly command another Greek contingent. Homer identifies them as sons of Thessalus and grandsons of Hercules. The description “Thessalian” may recall their father’s name: in Homer, their followers come from islands in the southeastern Aegean, including Cos, rather than from mainland Thessaly.

The Latin keeps both commanders and their thirty ships, but leaves out Homer’s list of islands and the brothers’ ancestry. It also turns a statement about the fleet’s strength into a picture of its voyage. Where Homer says that “thirty hollow ships followed them in order” (*Iliad* 2.680), the Latin shows their prows striking through the sea. Like the preceding description of Achilles crossing his mother’s waters, this gives movement to the succession of names and numbers.
