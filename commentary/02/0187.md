### 187–188 (*Iliad* 2.512, 2.516)

> 187 Ascalaphusque potens et Ialmenus, acer uterque,
> (And mighty Ascalaphus and Ialmenus, both valiant,)
>
> 188 ter denas ualido complerunt remige naues
> (filled thirty ships with strong oarsmen.)

Ascalaphus and Ialmenus are brothers who lead the men of Aspledon and Orchomenus, cities in Boeotia in central Greece. Homer introduces them as sons of Ares, the god of war (*Iliad* 2.512). The Latin leaves their homeland and divine parentage unmentioned, presenting them simply as two formidable commanders joining the expedition against Troy.

Their thirty ships preserve Homer’s count, but again the Latin turns a description of the fleet into an act of preparation: where Homer says that thirty “hollow ships” followed them (*Iliad* 2.516), the brothers here fill their vessels with strong oarsmen. This continues the emphasis on manpower and vigorous rowing in the preceding account of the Argive fleet. The order has changed, however: in Homer these brothers appear much earlier in the catalogue, before the Argive leaders.
