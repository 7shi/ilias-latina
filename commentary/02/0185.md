### 185–186 (*Iliad* 2.567–568)

> 185 Tydides ualido pulsarunt remige pontum:
> (Tydides beat the sea with powerful strokes of the oar:)
>
> 186 bis quadragenas onerarunt milite puppes;
> (they loaded eighty ships with soldiers;)

Tydides is Diomedes, named here as the son of Tydeus. His name completes the list of Argive leaders begun in the preceding verse: Euryalus, Sthenelus, and Diomedes, “brave in battle,” together bring their forces to Troy.

The Latin preserves Homer’s total of eighty ships but changes the emphasis. Homer explicitly makes Diomedes commander over the whole contingent and says that eighty black ships followed the leaders (*Iliad* 2.567–568). Here the three leaders act together, without that distinction of rank. The ships’ color disappears, while the vigorous rowing and the loading of soldiers turn a brief statement of the fleet’s size into a scene of military activity.
