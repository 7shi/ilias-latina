### 240 (*Iliad* 2.830, 2.837, 2.842)

> 240 Amphiusque et Adrastus et Asius atque Pylaeus.
> (And Amphius and Adrastus and Asius and Pylaeus.)

The catalogue continues with four more leaders among Troy’s allies. Amphius and Adrastus are brothers, sons of Merops, who command troops from towns near Troy. In Homer, their father is a seer who warns them against going to war, but they disregard him and march toward their deaths (*Iliad* 2.831–834). The Latin preserves only their names, reversing Homer’s order and omitting both Amphius’ linen armor and the warning that shadows the brothers’ departure.

Asius, the son of Hyrtacus, leads troops from around the Hellespont, the narrow strait north of Troy. Pylaeus shares command of the Pelasgians from Larissa with Hippothous in Homer; here he appears alone. By gathering leaders from three separate contingents into a single line, the Latin keeps the sense of Troy’s many allies while stripping away most of their geography, family histories, and individual distinctions.
