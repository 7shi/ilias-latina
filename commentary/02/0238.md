### 238 (*Iliad* 2.826)

> 238 nec non et proles generosa Lycaonis ibat
> (and the noble son of Lycaon was also marching;)

The muster continues with another warrior identified by his father: this is Pandarus, a renowned archer fighting on the Trojan side. In Homer, he commands the Trojans from Zeleia, a town near Mount Ida, whose people drink from the river Aesepus (*Iliad* 2.824–827). The Latin leaves out that local setting and his role as commander, presenting him instead as another noble figure marching among Troy’s defenders. “Noble” preserves the praise conveyed by Homer’s “glorious son of Lycaon” (*Iliad* 2.826), while giving it a stronger suggestion of distinguished birth.
