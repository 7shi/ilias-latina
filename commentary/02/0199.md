### 199 (*Iliad* 2.756)

> 199 Ast Prothous Magnes Tenthredone natus et una
> (But Prothous, a Magnesian, son of Tenthredon, and with him)

The catalogue turns from Eumelus and Ajax to Prothous, another commander bringing troops to Troy. “Magnesian” identifies him as a man of Magnesia, a district of Thessaly in northern Greece. Its people, the Magnetes, form his contingent.

Homer introduces him directly as their leader: “Prothous, son of Tenthredon, commanded the Magnetes” (*Iliad* 2.756). The Latin preserves his name, his father, and his people, while leaving the account of the force accompanying him to continue in the next verse.
