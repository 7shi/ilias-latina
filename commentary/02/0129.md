### 129 (*Iliad* 2.35)

> 129 Dixit, et has repetit per quas modo uenerat auras.
> (He spoke, and returns through the same air by which he had just come.)

His message delivered, Somnus, the god of sleep, retraces his flight from heaven, leaving Agamemnon to act on Jupiter’s command. Homer marks the messenger’s departure just as briefly: “Having spoken thus, he went away” (*Iliad* 2.35). The Latin adds the image of a return along the same airy route, neatly closing the journey begun when Somnus flew down into the king’s chamber. The divine visit is over; its consequences for the Greek army are still to unfold.
