### 205 (*Iliad* 2.557)

> 205 quem sequitur totidem ratibus Telamonius Aiax,
> (whom Ajax, son of Telamon, follows with as many ships,)

Ajax follows Ulysses in the catalogue with an equal force of twelve ships. His father’s name, Telamon, distinguishes him from the other Ajax among the Greek commanders, the son of Oileus. This is the warrior often called “the greater Ajax,” renowned for his size, strength, and steadfastness in battle.

Homer introduces his contingent with the words “Ajax led twelve ships from Salamis” (*Iliad* 2.557), an island near Athens. The Latin leaves out his homeland and identifies him instead by his parentage. It also places him immediately after Ulysses, though he appears earlier in Homer’s catalogue, linking the two contingents through their matching numbers of ships.
