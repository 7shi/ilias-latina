### 195 (*Iliad* 2.671)

> 195 et tribus assumptis ratibus secat aequora Teucer
> (and, having taken three ships, Teucer cuts through the seas,)

The catalogue continues with another glimpse of the fleet in motion, but the commander’s name differs from Homer’s. In the corresponding passage, Homer says that “Nireus brought three well-built ships from Syme” (*Iliad* 2.671), an island in the southeastern Aegean. The Latin keeps the three ships but names Teucer instead and leaves out their island home.

Teucer is a Greek archer and the half-brother of Ajax, son of Telamon, whose contingent comes from Salamis. Homer does not give Teucer a separate command in the catalogue: Ajax leads twelve ships, a number the Latin will also retain when it reaches him. Here, then, the three-ship contingent belongs to a different hero from the one named in Homer, while Nireus himself disappears from the abridged account.
