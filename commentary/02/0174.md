### 174 (*Iliad* 2.586–587)

> 174 et bis tricenis Menelai nauibus ardor
> (and with sixty ships fiery Menelaus)

Agamemnon is followed by his brother Menelaus, king of Sparta, whose wife Helen had been taken to Troy by the Trojan prince Paris. Her recovery was the cause of the Greek expedition. The brothers thus stand together in the catalogue, though Menelaus brings a smaller fleet: sixty ships against Agamemnon’s hundred.

The Latin preserves Homer’s ship count but compresses his description of the commander and his troops. Homer explicitly identifies Menelaus as Agamemnon’s brother, calls him “good at the war cry,” and says that his men armed themselves separately (*Iliad* 2.586–587). Here those details give way to the single image of “fiery Menelaus”: his ardour stands for the man himself, making him appear as an eager warrior alongside the supreme commander.
