### 206–207 (*Iliad* 2.748)

> 206 egregia uirtute potens; simul horrida Guneus
> (mighty in outstanding courage; meanwhile to grim war Guneus)
>
> 207 ire bis undenis temptabat in arma carinis.
> (sought to go with twenty-two ships.)

The opening praise completes the description of Ajax, son of Telamon, from the preceding passage, emphasizing the courage that makes him one of the Greeks’ foremost warriors. The catalogue then moves to Guneus, another Greek commander bringing his contingent to the war against Troy.

Homer introduces Guneus as leading twenty-two ships from Cyphus, in northern Greece (*Iliad* 2.748). The Latin preserves his name and the size of his fleet but omits his homeland, keeping the catalogue focused on commanders and ships rather than geography. Its reference to “grim war” also gives this brief entry a darker colouring: these ships are carrying men toward the fighting at Troy.
