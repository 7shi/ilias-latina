### 213–214 (*Iliad* 2.622–623, 2.618–619)

> 213 et clara uirtute Polyxenus atque Diores,
> (and Polyxenus and Diores, renowned for their valor,)
>
> 214 hi bis uicenas onerarunt milite puppes.
> (these men loaded forty ships with soldiers.)

Polyxenus and Diores complete the group of four commanders from Elis, joining Amphimachus and Thalpius named just before them. “These men” therefore means all four leaders, whose ships carry the Epeians to war at Troy.

Homer divides this force into four contingents, each with its own commander and ten ships. The Latin gathers them into a single company and gives the combined total of forty ships, preserving the scale of their contribution while simplifying its organization. It also replaces Homer’s individual descriptions—Diores is the mighty son of Amarynceus, while Polyxenus is “godlike”—with a shared reputation for valor. As in the preceding line, the emphasis falls less on ancestry and individual distinction than on the warriors’ readiness for battle.
