### 210–211 (*Iliad* 2.552, 2.556, 2.685)

> 210 et totidem puppes clara de gente Menestheus
> (and as many ships from a renowned people Menestheus)
>
> 211 duxit Athenaeus, quot uiribus ambit Achilles;
> (the Athenian led as Achilles commands in his force;)

The catalogue moves from Crete to Athens, whose contingent is led by Menestheus. His “renowned people” are the Athenians. Their fleet numbers fifty ships: the comparison is with Achilles’ force, mentioned earlier in the catalogue, not with the eighty Cretan ships immediately preceding this passage.

The Latin brings together information from separate parts of Homer’s catalogue. Homer identifies Menestheus as the son of Peteos and states that “fifty black ships followed him” (*Iliad* 2.552, 2.556); elsewhere, Achilles is likewise named as commander of fifty ships (*Iliad* 2.685). The Latin drops Menestheus’ parentage and replaces the explicit number with a comparison between the two commanders. This links the Athenian contingent to an already familiar force while keeping the list moving briskly onward.
