### 144–146 (*Iliad* 2.278–284, 2.299–302)

> 144 Tandem sollertis prudentia Nestoris aeuo
> (At last the wisdom of resourceful Nestor, gained through age,)
>
> 145 compressam miti sedauit pectore turbam
> (with a gentle spirit subdued and calmed the crowd,)
>
> 146 admonuitque duces dictis responsa recordans
> (and admonished the leaders with his words, recalling the prophecies)

Nestor, an elderly Greek king and trusted counsellor, restores order after the anger and near-violence of the assembly. Where Ulysses used a rebuke and a blow, Nestor brings the gentleness and judgement of long experience. The Latin makes his “wisdom” the acting force: it is this quality, rather than another display of authority, that quiets the crowd. His intervention turns the gathering away from its immediate quarrels and towards remembered grounds for continuing the war.

The retelling gives Nestor a task performed by Odysseus, called Ulysses in the Latin, in Homer’s account. There Odysseus stands with the sceptre while Athena, the goddess supporting the Greeks, takes the likeness of a herald and orders silence so that everyone can hear him. He then appeals for endurance: “Hold firm, friends, and stay a while, so that we may learn whether Calchas prophesies truly or not” (*Iliad* 2.299–300). Calchas is the Greek army’s diviner, whose interpretation of an earlier omen offers a reason not to abandon the expedition. The Latin condenses the divine intervention and the speaker’s preparations into Nestor’s calming presence. His recollection of the prophecies now prepares the way for that earlier omen to be retold.
