### 242 (*Iliad* 2.856)

> 242 magnanimique duces Odiusque et Epistrophus ingens
> (and the great-hearted leaders, Odius and mighty Epistrophus,)

The procession of Troy’s allies continues with Odius and Epistrophus. They command the Halizones, a people whose homeland is not securely identified. Homer introduces them simply as their people’s leaders: “Odius and Epistrophus led the Halizones” (*Iliad* 2.856). The Latin again drops the name of the people but adds heroic praise, calling both commanders “great-hearted” and Epistrophus “mighty.” Coming after Amphimachus and Nastes, whom Homer names later, they show how the shortened catalogue rearranges the allied forces into a flowing succession of distinguished warriors.
