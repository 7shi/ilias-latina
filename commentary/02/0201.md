### 201 (*Iliad* 2.627)

> 201 Dulichiusque Meges, animisque insignis et armis,
> (and Meges of Dulichium, distinguished in courage and arms,)

Meges joins the catalogue as another Greek commander. “Dulichius” identifies him with Dulichium, whose warriors he leads along with those of the Echinades, islands off the western coast of Greece. The Latin reduces this contingent’s fuller description to its leader’s name, his homeland, and his qualities as a warrior.

Homer calls Meges “a match for Ares,” the god of war (*Iliad* 2.627). The Latin preserves the force of that praise without the divine comparison: Meges stands out both for his courage and for his prowess in battle. Like the preceding entry for Elephenor, this brief notice keeps the catalogue moving while giving each commander a distinct place among the assembled Greeks.
