### 182–183 (*Iliad* 2.740, 2.745, 2.747)

> 182 et bis uicenas Polypoetes atque Leonteus
> (and forty Polypoetes and Leonteus)
>
> 183 instruxere rates ornatas milite forti.
> (fitted out as ships, manned by brave warriors.)

Another forty ships join the catalogue, matching the fleet of Schedius and Epistrophus. Polypoetes and Leonteus lead the Lapiths, a people of northern Thessaly in mainland Greece. They are joint commanders in Homer too: after introducing Polypoetes, Homer adds that he commanded “not alone: with him was Leonteus, a scion of Ares,” the god of war (*Iliad* 2.745).

The Latin preserves both leaders and Homer’s forty ships but leaves out the towns they represent and their distinguished ancestry. Polypoetes is the son of Pirithous, a legendary Lapith king, while Leonteus is the son of Coronus, another Lapith leader. Here their names alone identify the contingent. Homer’s praise of the commanders’ warlike qualities gives way to a collective tribute to the brave warriors aboard their ships, keeping the catalogue moving while emphasizing the strength of the gathering Greek army.
