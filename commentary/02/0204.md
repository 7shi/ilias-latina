### 204 (*Iliad* 2.631, 2.637)

> 204 et bis sex Ithaci naues sollertia duxit,
> (and the Ithacan’s resourcefulness led twelve ships,)

After the contingents of forty ships comes the smaller fleet of the Ithacan: Ulysses, whom Homer calls Odysseus, king of Ithaca, an island off the western coast of Greece. The Latin introduces him through his defining quality, resourcefulness. By making that quality itself lead the ships, the poet gives a brief but distinctive portrait of a commander whose strength lies especially in intelligence and ingenuity.

Homer names Odysseus as the leader of the “great-hearted Cephallenians” (*Iliad* 2.631), the people of Ithaca and the surrounding territories, and assigns him twelve ships with red-painted bows (*Iliad* 2.637). The Latin preserves the number but leaves out the description of the ships and the extent of his kingdom, concentrating instead on the character of their leader.
