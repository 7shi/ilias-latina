### 190 (*Iliad* 2.736–737)

> 190 instruxit puppes totidemque Euhaemone natus,
> (fit out as ships, and as many the son of Euhaemon.)

The son of Euhaemon is Eurypylus, a Greek commander whose followers came from Thessaly in northern Greece. “As many” carries forward the number assigned to Ajax in the preceding line: Eurypylus too commands forty.

The Latin preserves Homer’s parentage and ship count but leaves Eurypylus himself unnamed. Homer calls him the “glorious son of Euaemon” and describes forty black ships following him (*Iliad* 2.736–737). Here that brief picture becomes another act of preparation, as the catalogue continues to assemble the Greek fleet for the expedition against Troy.
