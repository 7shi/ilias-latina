### 154 (*Iliad* 2.333–335)

> 154 Assensere omnes, laudatur Nestoris aetas
> (All agreed, Nestor’s wisdom in old age is praised,)

The army accepts Nestor’s reminder that victory at Troy was foretold for the tenth year. Praise of his age is praise of the experience and sound judgment of this elderly Greek leader: his recollection has given the weary troops a reason to persevere.

In Homer, the response is much more resounding: the Greeks shout their approval so loudly that the ships echo with the noise, and their praise is for Odysseus’ speech (*Iliad* 2.333–335). The Latin condenses that clamorous scene into unanimous agreement. Having given Odysseus’ recollection of the omen to Nestor, it also transfers the credit to him, emphasizing the authority of his years rather than the power of the speech itself.
