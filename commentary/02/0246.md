### 246 (*Iliad* 2.858)

> 246 Arsinooque sati Chromiusque atque Ennomus, ambo
> (and Chromius and Ennomus, sons of Arsinous, both)

The gathering of Troy’s allies continues with Chromius and Ennomus. In Homer, these two command the Mysians, a people of northwestern Asia Minor, in what is now Turkey. As with the preceding commanders, the Latin names the leaders without identifying their people, but here it adds a family connection: both are sons of Arsinous, a father not named in Homer’s account. Homer also distinguishes Ennomus as an interpreter of bird omens—a diviner who seeks signs of the gods’ intentions in the movements and cries of birds.
