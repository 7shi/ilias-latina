### 212 (*Iliad* 2.620–621)

> 212 Amphimachusque ferox et Thalpius, Elide nati,
> (and fierce Amphimachus and Thalpius, born in Elis,)

The catalogue turns from Athens to Elis, a region in the western Peloponnese. Amphimachus and Thalpius are two of the commanders of its people, whom Homer calls the Epeians. Like the other leaders in the catalogue, they have brought their forces to join the expedition against Troy.

Homer names the two men together and identifies Amphimachus as the son of Cteatus and Thalpius as the son of Eurytus (*Iliad* 2.620–621). The Latin keeps the pairing but replaces their family connections with their shared birthplace. It also adds “fierce” to characterize Amphimachus, giving a brief impression of martial spirit where Homer supplies ancestry.
