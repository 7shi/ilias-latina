### 787 (*Iliad* 14.463–465)

> 787 Archelochumque Antenoriden Telamonius Aiax,
> (and Aiax, son of Telamon, strikes Archelochus, son of Antenor,)

The Greeks answer the Trojan attack: after Polydamas strikes Prothoenor, Aiax strikes Archelochus. Aiax is one of the Greeks’ strongest defenders; his opponent is a son of Antenor, a leading Trojan elder. The Latin links the blows closely, carrying over “strikes” from the preceding verse and giving each side a victim.

In Homer, the connection is more pointed. Aiax throws his spear at Polydamas to avenge Prothoenor, but Polydamas dodges, and Archelochus receives the fatal blow instead. The spear strikes where his head joins his neck, and the poet declares that the gods have willed his destruction (*Iliad* 14.463–465). The Latin retains the warrior and his parentage but compresses this fatal substitution into another swift battlefield encounter.
