### 782 (*Iliad* 14.421–432, 14.437)

> 782 Concurrit Troiana manus iuuenemque uomentem
> (The Trojan force rushes up, and the young man, vomiting …)

The Trojans rally around the fallen Hector, the “young man” struck down by Aiax. Their leading defender has suddenly become helpless and needs their protection. The sentence continues into the next verse, leaving him here in the grip of a violent physical reaction to the blow.

The Latin compresses a much fuller rescue scene. In Homer, the Greeks charge forward shouting and hurling spears, hoping to drag Hector away. Five leading warriors, supported by the other Trojans, stand over him and shield him from attack. His companions then lift him out of the fighting and carry him to his chariot, which takes him toward Troy as he groans heavily (*Iliad* 14.421–432). Here the rescuers appear collectively as the “Trojan force,” without individual names or the detailed struggle to protect him. The mention of vomiting also brings forward a detail from later in Homer’s account: after being carried away, Hector rises onto his knees and vomits dark blood (*Iliad* 14.437). The compressed telling places the Trojans’ urgent response directly beside the bodily suffering of their champion.
