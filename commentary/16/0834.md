### 834 (*Iliad* 16.820–821)

> 834 irruit et iuuenem nudato pectore ferro
> (rushes upon, and the youth, his breast laid bare, with the sword)

Hector rushes upon Patroclus, the young warrior whose borrowed armour has now failed to protect him. The exposure of the “counterfeit Achilles” immediately becomes an opportunity for a killing blow. What had seemed a confrontation with the Greeks’ greatest fighter is now an attack on his defenceless companion.

The Latin shortens the sequence of Patroclus’ defeat. In Homer, after Apollo disarms him, the Trojan Euphorbus wounds him from behind with a spear. Patroclus then retreats towards his companions, and Hector advances to drive another spear through his lower belly (*Iliad* 16.820–821). Here Euphorbus’ intervention disappears: the revelation of Patroclus’ identity leads directly to Hector’s assault. The sword striking his exposed breast replaces Homer’s spear thrust, keeping attention on the fatal loss of his armour.
