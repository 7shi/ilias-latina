Unable to bear the slaughter of his own men any longer, Patroclus swiftly puts on Achilles’ armour and rushes into battle. His deceptive appearance terrifies the Trojans, who had just been routing the Danaans and shouting defiantly. They flee, and he pursues them, disrupts their ranks, and strikes men down throughout the host. He lays Sarpedon low with a grievous wound and continues raging through the battle in the guise of “fearsome Achilles.”

“Fiery Hector,” described as huge in his mighty armour, rallies his courage and confronts him. Believing that he faces Achilles, Hector challenges him as “bravest Achilles” and boasts that he will learn the strength of an avenging Trojan right hand and of “valiant Hector.” He declares that even the protection of Mauors, also called Mars, cannot save his opponent from death. Patroclus remains silent and scorns the threats, maintaining his impersonation of Achilles.

Hector, here called “Dardanides,” throws his spear first, but Patroclus parries it and returns a spear-cast of his own. Hector’s shield deflects it into the ground. They then draw their swords and fight at close quarters until “Trojan Apollo” exposes the false Achilles and strips Patroclus bare. Hector, now called “mighty in battle,” recognizes that his opponent is fighting in another man’s armour, rushes upon him, and pierces the youth’s exposed breast with his sword. The book ends with Hector victorious over Patroclus, stripping him of Achilles’ armour, called “Vulcan’s armour.”
