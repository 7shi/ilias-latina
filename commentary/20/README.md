Venus and Apollo, protector of the Phrygian people, stir up the river Xanthus against the Danaans, intending its waters to overwhelm Achilles, called Aeacides, who is fighting savagely with his fearsome right hand. Xanthus swells and surges around him in a vast whirl, hindering his movement with torrential waves. Achilles struggles bodily against the river, breaking through its currents and thrusting back the waves with his shoulders and broad chest. Watching from afar, Iuno sustains him with fire when he begins to yield to the rushing water. The holy gods also fight among themselves.

Achilles then renews his assault on the Trojans, called the Phrygian ranks and the Troes. Described as the fearsome Aeacides, he drives them before him with great slaughter, his ardor for war restored. No opposing force shakes him and his fierce heart does not tire; success only increases his strength. The book ends with the Trojans trembling in fear and almost despairing of safety: they flee within their walls and secure the gates with a timber bar, leaving Achilles triumphant outside.
