Venus and Apollo, protector of the Phrygian people, force the waves of Xanthus to rise against the Danaans, so that they may overwhelm Achilles, called Aeacides, who is stirring up savage battles with his fearsome right hand. The river spreads abroad with all its waters and rolls headlong in a vast whirl, hampering him with torrential waves and slowing his steps. Achilles fights the savage waters with his whole body, breaking through the opposing currents and driving back the waves with his shoulders and broad chest. Provident Juno, from afar, sustains him with fire when he begins to yield to the rushing water, and the holy powers of the gods fight among themselves.

Achilles then renews his assault on the Trojans, called the Phrygian companies. The bristling Aeacides, his ardor for war renewed, drives them before him with huge slaughter and throws their deadly lines into turmoil. No force moves him and his savage heart does not tire of fighting; success only increases his strength. The book ends with the Trojans, stricken with trembling fear and their safety almost spent, fleeing for refuge within their walls and making the gates fast with timber set against them, leaving Achilles outside.
