### 920 (*Iliad* 21.328)

> 920 propellit fluctus. Quem longe prouida Iuno
> (he drives back the waves. Him, from afar, watchful Iuno …)

Achilles drives the waves back with his shoulders and chest, completing the image begun in the preceding passage. His strength remains formidable, but the struggle has not ended: pushing water aside is not the same as overcoming the river. The narrative now turns from his bodily effort to a goddess watching his danger.

Iuno is the Roman name for Hera, queen of the gods and a supporter of the Greeks against Troy. In Homer, “Hera cried out loudly, greatly fearing for Achilles” (*Iliad* 21.328). The Latin replaces that immediate expression of alarm with the description of Iuno as watchful: she observes from a distance and recognizes the danger. The verse breaks off before telling us what she does, but the change of focus prepares a divine response to a force Achilles cannot master by strength alone.
