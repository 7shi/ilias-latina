### 915–919 (*Iliad* 21.240–242, 21.268–271)

> 915 exspatiatur aquis et uasto gurgite praeceps
> (the river spreads out in its waters and, headlong in a vast whirl,)
>
> 916 uoluitur atque uirum torrentibus impedit undis
> (rolls on and hampers the man with torrential waves)
>
> 917 praetardatque gradus. Ille omni corpore saeuas
> (and slows his steps. With his whole body he fights the savage)
>
> 918 contra pugnat aquas aduersaque flumina rumpit
> (waters head-on and breaks through the opposing currents,)
>
> 919 et modo disiectos umeris modo pectore uasto
> (and [waves] scattered now by his shoulders, now by his broad chest …)

The opening continues the description of Xanthus, the river set against Achilles by Venus and Apollo. Its spreading waters turn the battlefield itself into an adversary: the ground on which Achilles needs to stand and move is overwhelmed by the flood. The man who has been pursuing and killing the Trojans is now impeded at every step. Yet he resists with his whole body, forcing his way against the current. His shoulders and broad chest take the place of the “fearsome right hand” emphasized just before: this is a struggle of bodily strength against a force that cannot be defeated with a weapon.

The Latin brings together two moments from Homer’s longer account. In the first, a wave towers around Achilles, and the current strikes his shield so hard that he cannot keep his footing (*Iliad* 21.240–242). Later, the flood beats down upon his shoulders while he leaps upward in distress; beneath him, it weakens his knees and washes the sand from under his feet (*Iliad* 21.268–271). The retelling condenses these distinct pressures into a swift exchange: the river checks Achilles’ movement, and Achilles fights back.

That compression gives particular prominence to his resistance. Where Homer describes how the water repeatedly unbalances him, the Latin stresses his effort to break through it. This does not yet amount to escape or victory. The final line remains unfinished, carrying the image of waves scattered against his powerful body into the next part of the struggle.
