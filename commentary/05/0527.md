### 527 (*Iliad* 5.669–670)

> 527 Sarpedon fraudisque subit commentor Vlixes
> (Sarpedon; and Vlixes, contriver of deceit, comes forward.)

Sarpedon’s name completes the preceding line: he is the wounded Trojan ally leaving the battle. Attention now shifts to Vlixes, the Greek leader known in Homer as Odysseus, whose resourcefulness and cunning are among his defining qualities.

In Homer, Odysseus notices Sarpedon being carried away; his endurance is emphasized, and his heart surges with fury (*Iliad* 5.669–670). The Latin replaces this glimpse of his response with a brief entrance and a pointed description: “contriver of deceit.” The phrase presents deception almost as his craft. It does not describe a trick he is performing here, but introduces him through his established reputation for cunning rather than through the anger and determination emphasized by Homer.
