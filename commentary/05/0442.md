### 442 (*Iliad* 5.136)

> 442 in mediasque acies animosi more leonis
> (and into the midst of the ranks, like a spirited lion,)

Diomedes’ anger carries him back into the thick of battle. The lion comparison presents him as a predator among the enemy: Pandarus’ arrow has not stopped him but made him more dangerous.

Homer introduces the comparison by saying that “three times as much fighting fury seized him” (*Iliad* 5.136). The Latin keeps the lion’s ferocity but omits this explicit measure of Diomedes’ renewed strength. Following directly from his blazing anger, the unfinished movement into the ranks sustains the momentum of his return to combat.
