### 528 (*Iliad* 5.677–678)

> 528 et septem iuuenum fortissima corpora fundit.
> (and lays low the bodies of seven very brave young men.)

Vlixes now strikes down seven Lycians, warriors from Sarpedon’s contingent fighting on the Trojan side. The wounded Sarpedon has escaped him, but his followers bear the force of his attack. Homer names each of the seven men killed by Odysseus, the Greek name for Vlixes (*Iliad* 5.677–678). The Latin compresses that list into a single collective description, preserving the number of victims while emphasizing their courage and physical strength. Their individual identities disappear, and the focus falls instead on the scale of Vlixes’ victory: the “contriver of deceit” is also a formidable warrior in open combat.
