### 445 (*Iliad* 5.148)

> 445 inde premit Polyidon Abantaque cuspide forti
> (then he presses hard on Polyidus and Abas with his mighty spear)

Diomedes presses hard on two more Trojan warriors, Polyidus and Abas. The succession of names sustains the pace of his assault: scarcely has one pair fallen before he attacks another. Homer says that Diomedes “left those men and went after Abas and Polyeidus” (*Iliad* 5.148). The Latin reverses their order and makes him press them hard with his spear.

These men are brothers, sons of Eurydamas, an interpreter of dreams. Homer briefly recalls their father and his inability to foresee his sons’ fate (*Iliad* 5.149–151). Here, only their names interrupt Diomedes’ advance; their family story gives way to the relentless movement of battle.
