### 445 (*Iliad* 5.148)

> 445 inde premit Polyidon Abantaque cuspide forti
> (Next he overpowers Polyidus and Abas with his mighty spear.)

Diomedes presses on against two more Trojan warriors, Polyidus and Abas. The succession of names sustains the pace of his assault: scarcely has one pair fallen before he attacks another. Homer says that Diomedes “left those men and went after Abas and Polyeidus” (*Iliad* 5.148). The Latin reverses their order and turns the pursuit into an act of overpowering them, adding the spear as his weapon.

These men are brothers, sons of Eurydamas, an interpreter of dreams. Homer briefly recalls their father and his inability to foresee his sons’ fate (*Iliad* 5.149–151). Here, only their names interrupt Diomedes’ advance; their family story gives way to the relentless movement of battle.
