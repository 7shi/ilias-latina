### 526 (*Iliad* 5.663–664)

> 526 Saucius egreditur medio certamine belli
> (Wounded, he withdraws from the midst of the battle.)

The wounded survivor is Sarpedon, son of Jupiter and a leader of the forces allied with Troy. He has killed Tlepolemus, but has not escaped their encounter unharmed: in Homer, Tlepolemus’ spear strikes him in the thigh. The two sons of famous fathers thus meet different fates—one dies, while the other leaves the fighting wounded.

The Latin compresses Sarpedon’s removal into a single line. Homer describes his companions carrying him from the battlefield, with the long spear still weighing him down (*Iliad* 5.663–664). Here neither the companions nor the embedded weapon appears; the emphasis falls simply on his withdrawal from combat.
