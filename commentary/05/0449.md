### 449–453 (*Iliad* 5.290, 5.294–296, 5.291–293)

> 449 Tu quoque Tydidae prostratus, Pandare, dextra
> (You too, Pandarus, laid low by Tydides’ right hand,)
>
> 450 occidis, infelix, accepto uulnere tristi,
> (fall dead, unfortunate man, having received a grievous wound)
>
> 451 dextera qua naris fronti coniungitur imae;
> (where the right nostril joins the lowest part of the forehead;)
>
> 452 dissipat et cerebrum galeae cum parte reuulsum
> (and scatters the brain, torn out with part of the helmet,)
>
> 453 ossaque confossa spargit Tydideus ensis.
> (and strews the pierced bones, the sword of Tydides.)

Diomedes’ onslaught continues with the death of Pandarus, a Trojan ally and skilled archer who had earlier wounded him. “Tydides” means “son of Tydeus,” another name for Diomedes. The direct address—“You too, Pandarus”—draws this opponent into the growing succession of the dead, while “unfortunate man” briefly gives the account a pitying voice.

In Homer, the fatal attack follows an exchange of challenges, and Diomedes throws a spear whose course Athena directs (*Iliad* 5.290). The Latin omits both the dialogue and the goddess’s intervention, concentrating attention on the warrior’s own destructive force. It also announces Pandarus’ death before describing the wound. Homer instead follows the weapon’s course before showing Pandarus falling from his chariot, his shining armor clattering around him and his horses recoiling (*Iliad* 5.294–296). Those surrounding movements and sounds disappear here.

The wound itself changes substantially. Homer’s spear strikes beside the eye, pierces the teeth, cuts through the tongue and emerges beneath the chin (*Iliad* 5.291–293). The Latin places the wound where the right nostril meets the lower forehead and replaces the spear with a sword. Brain, helmet and bones are violently torn apart and scattered. This is not simply a shortened account of Homer’s injury, but a different, more explosive picture of bodily destruction, sustaining the impression that Diomedes’ attack cannot be resisted.
