### 472 (*Iliad* 5.344–345)

> 472 Dardanium Aenean seruat Troianus Apollo
> (Trojan Apollo protects Dardanian Aeneas.)

While Venus seeks comfort from her mother, the story returns to the wounded son she has left behind. Aeneas, whom she had tried to carry out of danger, now receives the protection of Apollo. “Dardanian” identifies him with the Trojan people, whose ancestry reaches back to Dardanus; “Trojan” describes Apollo’s allegiance, not his origin. The god stands on Troy’s side.

The Latin reduces the rescue to its essentials: the endangered warrior and his divine protector. In Homer, Apollo takes Aeneas into his hands and conceals him in a dark cloud, shielding him from the Greek attackers (*Iliad* 5.344–346). This happens immediately after Venus is wounded, before her flight to Olympus and her appeal to her mother. By placing the rescue here, the Latin finishes following Venus away from battle before returning to Aeneas. Her withdrawal has not left him without divine help: Apollo takes over the protection that Diomedes forced her to abandon.
