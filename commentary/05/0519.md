### 519 (*Iliad* 5.576–579)

> 519 Paphlagonum ductor Menelai concidit armis,
> (the leader of the Paphlagonians fell beneath Menelaus’ weapons,)

After Aeneas’ killing of Crethon and Orsilochus, the fighting turns in the Greeks’ favor: Menelaus strikes down a leader on the Trojan side. The Paphlagonians are Trojan allies from the Black Sea coast of northern Asia Minor. Their commander, unnamed in the Latin, is Pylaemenes.

Homer describes Pylaemenes as “a match for Ares,” the god of war, and calls his followers “high-hearted shield-bearers” (*Iliad* 5.576–577). He then gives the precise action: Menelaus catches the standing warrior with a spear thrust at the collarbone (*Iliad* 5.578–579). The Latin compresses this into a single statement of defeat, omitting the commander’s name, his warlike stature, and the location of the wound. What remains is the rapid exchange of losses between the armies: the deaths of two Greeks are followed by the fall of a Trojan ally.
