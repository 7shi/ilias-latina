### 794 (*Iliad* 15.279–280)

> 794 Aduolat interea Danaum metus impiger Hector:
> (Meanwhile tireless Hector, the terror of the Danai, rushes up:)

The panic of the retreat now finds its personal focus in Hector, the Trojans’ leading warrior. The Danai are the Greeks, whose defences have just become obstacles in their flight. Hector’s swift approach keeps the pressure on them: he is not merely another fighter advancing, but the man whose presence inspires fear throughout their army.

In Homer, the Greeks see Hector moving along the ranks and are terrified; “all their hearts sank to their feet” (*Iliad* 15.279–280). The Latin compresses that collective reaction into the title “terror of the Danai.” It also gives Hector’s movement greater urgency: where Homer describes him advancing through the ranks, here he rushes into the scene. After the confused movements of the fugitives, his tireless energy presents a sharp contrast between the retreating Greeks and the warrior pressing forward against them.
