### 795 (*Iliad* 15.343–345)

> 795 confugiunt iterum ad classes Agamemnonis alae
> (once more Agamemnon’s squadrons flee for refuge to the fleet)

Hector’s arrival drives the Greeks back toward their ships, drawn up on the shore near Troy. Agamemnon is the commander of the Greek expedition, and his name here stands for the army as a whole. The ships offer a last place of refuge, but they are also the Greeks’ means of returning home: their retreat brings the fighting dangerously close to what they must preserve.

In Homer, while the Trojans strip the armor from the fallen, the Greeks collide with the ditch and its stakes, scatter in different directions, and are forced back through their defensive wall (*Iliad* 15.343–345). The Latin leaves out the stripping of the dead and the physical obstacles, reducing the confused retreat to one urgent movement toward the fleet. Following immediately upon Hector’s appearance, the flight reads as the direct effect of his advance. “Once more” sustains the sense of an army repeatedly driven back, unable to regain its footing.
