The Troes press their attack more fiercely, driving the Achaeans back in fear. The defenders leap over the rampart and embankment-protected walls, while others fall into the ditches. Tireless Hector, called “the terror of the Danai,” rushes forward, and Agamemnon’s troops retreat again to their ships. From there they resist, and the battle continues directly before the fleet.

Warlike Hector rages, calling for fire and demanding that the entire fleet be burned. As he prepares to carry out the attack, Aiax opposes him with mighty strength. Standing at the very stern of a ship, he holds off the savage flames with his shield and alone defends a thousand ships. The Greek forces are called Achaeans, Danai, and Agamemnon’s troops; their opponents are called Troes and Phryges. The book ends with the Danai hurling sturdy-pointed spears from the ships while the Phryges throw blazing torches from every direction. Sweat streams down the fighters’ mighty limbs, and the struggle to defend or burn the fleet remains unresolved.
