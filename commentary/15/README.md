The Trojans rise up more fiercely to the war with the Achaeans; the verse that followed is lost. Men driven by fear leap over the rampart and the walls fenced with an embankment, while others roll into the ditches. Tireless Hector, called “the terror of the Danaans,” flies up, and Agamemnon’s squadrons flee again for refuge to the fleet. From there they drive the enemy back, and the battle is fought before the ships.

Warlike Hector rages, calling for fire and making ready to burn the entire fleet. Ajax opposes him with mighty strength. Standing at the stern, he holds off the savage flames with his shield and alone defends a thousand ships. The Greeks are called Achaeans, Danaans, and Agamemnon’s squadrons; their opponents Trojans and Phrygians. The book ends with the Danaans hurling sturdy-pointed spears from one side while the Phrygians throw blazing torches from every direction on the other. Sweat streams down the fighters’ mighty limbs, and the struggle to defend or burn the fleet remains unresolved.
