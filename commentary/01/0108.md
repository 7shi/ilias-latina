### 108 (*Iliad* 1.605)

> 108 Interea sol emenso decedit Olympo
> (Meanwhile the sun sets, its journey across the sky complete,)

Evening closes the scene among the gods. “Olympus,” just named as the seat of Jupiter’s authority, here means the sky through which the sun has travelled. The setting sun gives a quiet conclusion to the divine quarrel and its resolution, though the consequences of Jupiter’s promise to honor Achilles still await the Greeks.

In Homer, sunset comes before the gods depart to their separate homes: “when the bright light of the sun had sunk” (*Iliad* 1.605). The Latin has already described Jupiter dismissing the assembly, then uses “meanwhile” to place the sunset alongside that dispersal. It also gives the sun a completed journey across heaven, where Homer simply marks the disappearance of its light.
