### 97 (*Iliad* 1.532)

> 97 litus adit patrium gratasque sororibus undas.
> (reaches her native shore and the waters dear to her sisters.)

Thetis completes her descent from heaven, returning to the sea after securing Jupiter’s promise to take care of her son’s grievance. Her sisters are the Nereids, sea goddesses who, like her, are daughters of the sea god Nereus. The “native shore” and the waters dear to them present the sea as her family’s home; no particular coastline is named.

In Homer, Thetis “leapt into the deep sea from shining Olympus” (*Iliad* 1.532). The Latin softens that abrupt movement into the gliding descent begun in the preceding verse and adds the welcoming associations of home and sisterhood. Although Jupiter has told her to comfort Achilles, she is not shown rejoining him here: the passage closes with her return to her own divine surroundings.
