### 67 (*Iliad* 1.478–487)

> 67 atque iterum ad classes Danaum sua uela retorsit.
> (and turned his sails back toward the fleets of the Danai.)

With Chryseis safely home, Ulixes turns back toward the Greek forces outside Troy. “Danai” is another name for the Greeks, whose ships are drawn up along the shore beside their encampment. This single line closes the account of his mission, carrying him back to the setting where the quarrel between Agamemnon and Achilles remains unresolved.

Homer makes the return voyage a vivid scene: the crew raise the mast and spread the white sails, the wind fills the canvas, and a dark wave roars around the ship’s stem as it speeds forward. Once ashore, they haul the vessel onto the sand, support it with props, and disperse to their quarters. The Latin compresses all this into the turning of the sails toward home base. It also omits a significant sign of reconciliation: in Homer, Apollo himself sends the favorable wind (*Iliad* 1.479). The god who brought the plague now helps the men who have returned his priest’s daughter.
