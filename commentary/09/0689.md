### 689–690 (*Iliad* 9.165–172)

> 689 legatos mittunt dextramque hortantur Achillis,
> (they send envoys and appeal to Achilles’ right hand,)
>
> 690 ut ferat auxilium miseris. Thetideius heros
> (that he may bring aid to them in their misery. The hero, son of Thetis,)

Nestor’s advice turns the leaders’ lamentation into action: they send an embassy to Achilles, whose withdrawal from battle after his quarrel with Agamemnon has deprived the Greeks of their greatest fighter. Their appeal acknowledges how badly they need him. The “right hand” can suggest both the hand offered in friendship or as a pledge of support and the hand that exercises a warrior’s strength. The image thus brings reconciliation and military help together: the Greeks need Achilles to stand beside them again.

Homer gives Nestor’s instructions in detail. He names Phoenix, an older companion of Achilles, to lead the embassy, followed by the warriors Ajax and Odysseus, with two heralds accompanying them. He then calls for water to wash their hands and for reverent silence before a prayer that Zeus may show mercy (*Iliad* 9.165–172). The Latin reduces these preparations to the dispatch of unnamed envoys and the urgent purpose of their mission. Its emphasis falls not on ceremony or the choice of persuasive speakers, but on the suffering that Achilles is asked to relieve.

The unfinished phrase “The hero, son of Thetis” turns attention toward Achilles himself. Thetis is his mother, a sea-goddess; this description introduces the man whose response will determine whether the embassy succeeds.
