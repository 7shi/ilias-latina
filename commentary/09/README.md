Overwhelmed by the crisis facing them, the leaders of the Danai neglect food and bodily care and lament their plight. At Nestor’s urging, they send envoys to Achilles, appealing to his right hand—that is, his prowess in battle—to bring them aid. Achilles, called “the hero, son of Thetis,” refuses to heed their prayers or accept any of the king’s gifts. Even the offer to return his beloved Briseis, untouched in body, fails to move him. The king is not named in this passage but is Agamemnon; the envoys are likewise unnamed. The Greeks are called both “Danai” and “Pelasgi.” The book ends with the envoys bringing back Achilles’ fruitless reply, after which the Greeks restore themselves with food and gentle sleep. Achilles remains unwilling to help them.
