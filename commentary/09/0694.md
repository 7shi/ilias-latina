### 694 (*Iliad* 9.669–692)

> 694 Irrita legati referunt responsa Pelasgis
> (The envoys bring back a fruitless reply to the Pelasgi.)

The embassy returns without securing Achilles’ help. “Pelasgi” here means the Greeks, the same army called the Danai in the preceding passage. The reply is “fruitless” because it offers them no relief: their appeal and Agamemnon’s promised gifts have failed to persuade Achilles to rejoin the fighting.

The Latin reduces a substantial reporting scene in Homer to a single line. In the *Iliad*, the Greek leaders rise to welcome the returning envoys with golden cups, and Agamemnon immediately asks whether Achilles will defend the ships or remains angry. Odysseus answers that Achilles rejects both Agamemnon and his gifts, leaving the king to devise his own means of saving the army (*Iliad* 9.669–681). He also reports Achilles’ threat to sail home at dawn and his advice that the other Greeks should abandon the war. The Latin omits these particulars and the exchange between the leaders, retaining only the embassy’s failure. What was previously Achilles’ personal refusal now becomes unwelcome news for the army awaiting his answer.
