In the supplied closing lines, Telamonian Aiax—Aiax, son of Telamon—defends a fallen warrior’s body, covering it with the shield he holds before him. The dead man is not named in this excerpt. The Trojans, called “Priam’s young men,” rejoice, while the Greeks, called the “Danai,” mourn their wounds. The book thus ends with Aiax guarding the body and a contrast between Trojan jubilation and Greek suffering; no recovery or removal of the body is described here.
