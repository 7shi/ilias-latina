### 741–742 (*Iliad* 11.1)

> 741 Lux exorta uiros in pristina bella remisit,
> (Daybreak sent the men back to the fighting,)
>
> 742 instaurantque animos recreato milite pugnae
> (and, with their troops refreshed, they renew their eagerness for battle.)

After the night expedition of Diomedes and Ulixes, the story returns to the armies and their continuing struggle before Troy. The previous episode ended with the two raiders resting; here renewed strength and determination prepare the soldiers for another day’s fighting. This is a resumption, not a new conflict: the night’s successes have not ended the war.

Homer opens this day with Dawn rising “from her bed beside noble Tithonus” (*Iliad* 11.1). Dawn is a goddess, and Tithonus her husband. The Latin removes that divine household scene and makes daybreak itself the signal for men to return to war. It then adds a direct statement of the troops’ recovered fighting spirit. In two brief lines, the focus shifts from the secrecy of the night raid to the collective effort of battle.
