### 754–755 (*Iliad* 11.284–285)

> 754 Hector tunc pugnae subit acri concitus ira
> (Hector then enters the battle, stirred by fierce anger,)
>
> 755 Priamides et percussos agit undique Graios;
> (Priamides, and drives the battered Greeks before him on every side;)

The initiative now passes from Agamemnon to Hector, the Trojans’ foremost warrior. “Priamides” identifies Hector as the son of Priam, king of Troy. After Agamemnon’s furious advance, Hector takes control of the fighting and presses the Greeks back.

The Latin makes this reversal abrupt. In Homer, Agamemnon continues fighting until his wound’s pain becomes unbearable and forces him to leave the battlefield. Hector sees him withdrawing and shouts to the Trojans and their Lycian allies (*Iliad* 11.284–285). His ensuing speech urges them to seize their opportunity now that the Greeks’ strongest fighter has gone. The Latin omits both Agamemnon’s withdrawal and Hector’s rallying cry, moving directly from the wounded commander’s vengeance to the Trojan counterattack. Fierce anger supplies Hector’s immediate impulse, and the Greeks, so recently advancing behind Agamemnon, are now driven before him.
