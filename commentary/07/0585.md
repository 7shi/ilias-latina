### 585 (*Iliad* 7.229–230)

> 585 procedunt; aberat nam Troum terror Achilles
> (They step forward; for Achilles, terror of the Trojans, was absent.)

The nine champions step forward as possible opponents for Hector, but the greatest Greek fighter is missing. Achilles has withdrawn from battle after Agamemnon, the Greek commander, took away Briseis, a captive woman awarded to him as a prize. He remains beside his ships, refusing to fight. “Terror of the Trojans” recalls the power the Greeks have lost through this quarrel: even among so many willing champions, his absence matters.

In Homer, this explanation comes later, when Ajax, chosen to face Hector, declares that the Greeks still have warriors capable of fighting him without Achilles. He acknowledges that Achilles “lies among the curved, seagoing ships, nursing his anger against Agamemnon, shepherd of the people” (*Iliad* 7.229–230). The Latin moves the reminder into the narrator’s account of the volunteers. It omits the explanation of Achilles’ anger here, but preserves the contrast between the men ready to fight and their formidable comrade who holds back.
