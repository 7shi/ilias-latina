### 605–607 (*Iliad* 7.254, 7.261)

> 605 Ille ictum celeri praeuidit callidus astu
> (With quick-witted cunning he anticipated the blow)
>
> 606 tergaque summisit ferrumque umbone repellit.
> (and lowered his back, and drives off the steel with the boss.)
>
> 607 Sed leuis extremas clipei perlabitur oras
> (But, light, along the outer rims of the shield slips)

Hector sees Ajax’s sword coming toward his exposed neck and responds with both an evasive movement and his shield. He ducks beneath the blow while using the boss—the raised central part of the shield—to turn the weapon aside. His quick judgment answers Ajax’s furious attack. Yet the defense does not bring the danger to an end: the blade slips along the shield’s edge, and the passage breaks off before its further course becomes clear.

The Latin combines moments from two exchanges in Homer’s duel. Earlier, Hector “leaned aside and escaped black death” (*Iliad* 7.254); later, Ajax’s spear passed through Hector’s shield and “staggered him as he pressed forward” (*Iliad* 7.261). Here those movements become one continuous action with a sword. The Latin preserves Hector’s narrow escape and the force of Ajax’s attack, but makes the shield’s deflection of the blade part of a more closely described defense.
