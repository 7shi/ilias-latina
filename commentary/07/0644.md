### 644–645 (*Iliad* 7.413)

> 644 Paruit is monitis iterumque ad castra reuersus
> (He obeyed the orders and, having turned back again to the camp)
>
> 645 Troiae contemptum duro se reddit ab hoste.
> (of Troy, returns, scorned by the harsh enemy.)

Idaeus, the Trojan herald, obeys the Greek leader’s command to leave. He returns to his own side with the peace proposal rejected. The description of him as “scorned” makes the rebuff personal as well as political: the envoy himself has been treated with contempt by an unyielding enemy.

Homer marks the same transition simply: “Idaeus went back towards sacred Ilios” (*Iliad* 7.413), Ilios being another name for Troy. The Latin preserves the herald’s return but adds the hostile reception that now defines his mission’s failure. In Homer, although the peace offer has failed, Idaeus returns with agreement on a pause to burn the dead. Here that limited success is absent. Coming immediately after his expulsion from the Greek camp, these lines close the embassy on a note of rejection rather than cooperation.
