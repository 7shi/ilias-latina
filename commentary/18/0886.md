### 886 (*Iliad* 18.550–551)

> 886 maturasque metit robustus messor aristas
> (and a sturdy reaper cuts the ripe ears of grain,)

The shield moves from ploughing to harvest, showing the reward of agricultural labour. A single sturdy reaper stands for the workers who gather the ripened crop, continuing the picture of a community sustained by the land.

Homer places this harvest on “a king’s estate,” where labourers reap “with sharp sickles in their hands” (*Iliad* 18.550–551). The Latin leaves out the royal setting and the tools, condensing the group of harvesters into one representative figure. It adds an emphasis on the reaper’s strength and the grain’s ripeness, presenting the harvest as a brief, vivid image of fruitful work.
