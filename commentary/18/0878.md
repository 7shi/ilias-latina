### 878–879 (*Iliad* 18.503–508)

> 878 certantes populi; sedet illic aequus utrisque
> (the people in dispute; there sits, impartial to both sides,)
>
> 879 iudex et litem discernit fronte serena.
> (a judge, who settles the case with a calm expression.)

The description of civic life on Achilles’ shield now gives justice a human face. The townsfolk bring their disputes before a judge who favours neither plaintiff nor defendant. His calm expression contrasts with their contention: the shield depicts not a world without conflict, but a community with a peaceful means of resolving it.

Homer presents a more crowded and ceremonial scene. Heralds restrain the people while elders sit on polished stones in a sacred circle. Holding the heralds’ staffs as signs of their authority to speak, the elders deliver judgments in turn (*Iliad* 18.503–506). Two talents of gold lie before them, to be awarded to whoever gives the soundest judgment (*Iliad* 18.507–508). The Latin leaves out these arrangements and the gold, replacing the group of elders with a single impartial judge. It also adds his serene expression. The result is a simpler, more concentrated picture: public justice rests visibly in a composed figure who can distinguish between opposing claims without sharing the disputants’ agitation.
