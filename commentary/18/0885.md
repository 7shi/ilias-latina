### 885 (*Iliad* 18.541–542)

> 885 Rura colunt alii, sulcant grauia arua iuuenci
> (Others till the countryside, young oxen furrow the heavy fields,)

From music and dancing, the shield turns to the work that sustains the community. The figures shown here cultivate the land, while young oxen draw the ploughs through fertile soil. This brief scene adds agricultural labour to the peaceful activities represented on the shield.

Homer introduces “a broad, soft field of rich soil, thrice ploughed,” with many ploughmen at work (*Iliad* 18.541–542). The Latin preserves the fertile ground and its cultivation but omits the field’s breadth and repeated ploughing. Rather than pausing over the landscape, it concentrates on the shared labour of people and animals, reducing the opening of Homer’s ploughing scene to a single verse.
