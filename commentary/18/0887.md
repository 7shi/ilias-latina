### 887 (*Iliad* 18.561–562)

> 887 et gaudet pressis immundus uinitor uuis;
> (and the grimy vine-grower rejoices over the pressed grapes;)

From the grain harvest, the shield’s scenes of rural life turn to the grape crop. The vine-grower’s delight continues the picture of labour rewarded: the grapes have been pressed for wine, and his dirty appearance marks his involvement in the work.

Homer describes “a beautiful golden vineyard, heavily laden with grapes,” with dark clusters hanging upon it (*Iliad* 18.561–562). The Latin passes over the vineyard’s rich colours and the fruit still on the vines, moving directly to the pressing of the harvested grapes. As with the sturdy reaper in the preceding scene, one worker becomes the focus of the picture. Here his joy makes the abundance of the harvest a source of human satisfaction, rather than simply a beautiful feature of the shield.
