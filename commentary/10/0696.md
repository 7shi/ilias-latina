### 696 (*Iliad* 10.251–252)

> 696 Alterius tenebrae tarde labentibus astris
> (The darkness of the night’s second part, as the stars slowly glided on)

The story continues on the same night as the unsuccessful appeal to Achilles, not on a new night. After the Greeks have taken food and rested, this glimpse of the moving stars prepares the transition to further action under cover of darkness. The second part of the night is passing; the continuation will place events in its final third. Achilles remains unwilling to help, so the Greeks must look elsewhere for a response to their danger.

In Homer, this indication of time belongs to Diomedes’ speech to Odysseus as the two warriors prepare to venture out on a night reconnaissance: “Let us go: the night is drawing to an end, dawn is near, and the stars have moved onward” (*Iliad* 10.251–252). The Latin turns that urgent call to departure into a brief description of the night, using the stars to mark the passage of time.
