### 254 (*Iliad* 3.30–31)

> 254 armatum aduerso Menelaum ex agmine cernit
> (sees Menelaus in arms among the opposing ranks,)

Paris now catches sight of the man he has most directly wronged: Menelaus, Helen’s husband. The conflict between the two armies sharpens into a personal confrontation. The man just named as Troy’s ruin faces the armed husband whose marriage he has broken.

In Homer, Paris sees Menelaus appear among the foremost fighters, and “his heart was struck with terror” (*Iliad* 3.30–31). The Latin keeps the decisive moment of recognition, emphasizing that Menelaus is armed and belongs to the battle line opposite Paris. For the moment, it leaves Paris’s response unstated: attention rests on the dangerous opponent he has suddenly recognized.
