### 313 (*Iliad* 3.377–378)

> 313 in medios mittit proceres rursumque recurrit
> (throws it among the chiefs and rushes back again.)

Menelaus throws Paris’s empty helmet among the Greek leaders and returns to the attack. The splendid armour is not enough: his anger remains directed at the man who has escaped him. Homer describes Menelaus swinging the helmet before throwing it among the Greeks, where his companions pick it up (*Iliad* 3.377–378). The Latin leaves out both the swing and the companions’ action, moving directly from the discarded helmet to Menelaus’s renewed charge. The pace keeps attention on his determination to continue the fight despite the goddess’s intervention.
