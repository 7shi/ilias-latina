### 252 (*Iliad* 3.15)

> 252 Iamque duae stabant acies fulgentibus armis,
> (And now the two battle lines stood with gleaming arms,)

The Greeks and the Trojans, whose leaders and forces filled the close of the previous book, now confront one another on the plain outside Troy. The long preparations have brought them face to face, but combat has not yet begun.

Homer marks this moment with movement: “When they were now close, advancing against one another” (*Iliad* 3.15). The Latin instead presents a poised, stationary scene, adding the gleam of weapons and armour. In a single line, the separate armies become two opposing battle lines, ready for the encounter.
