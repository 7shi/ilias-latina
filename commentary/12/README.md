The Trojans press forward as the exhausted Greeks retreat into their camp and secure its gates with massive bars. Warlike Hector breaks through a gate with a rock, forcing apart its iron-bound timbers. The Trojans pour through the entrances, kill the Greeks resisting at the threshold, and drive defenders from the rampart. Others call for ladders and throw firebrands, their success giving them fresh strength. The Greeks defend the walls and lofty towers with stones, while the Trojans advance under a roof of interlocked shields, climb the approaches, and press against the gates. Finally, panic overtakes all the Greeks, who abandon the camp and board their ships.

Hector, called “warlike,” is the only individual named. The Trojans are called Troes and Phryges, and at the close “the young Trojan warriors”; the Greeks are called Pelasgi, Grai, and Danai. The book ends with the Greeks aboard their ships and the young Trojans pressing the attack, hurling volleys of missiles as shouts resound through the sky.
