The Trojans press forward as the exhausted Greeks retreat into their camp and secure its walls on every side with massive bars. Warlike Hector breaks through the gates with a rock, loosening their iron-bound timbers. The Trojans burst through the entrances, lay low the Greeks resisting at the threshold, and dislodge the defenders from the rampart. Others call for ladders and throw firebrands, their success giving them fresh strength. The Greeks fight from the walls and towers; stones fly, while the Trojans come up under a tortoise of shields, climb the approaches, and press against the gates. Finally, panic overtakes all the Greeks, who flee the camp and climb aboard their ships.

Hector, called “warlike,” is the only individual named. The Trojans are called Trojans and Phrygians, and at the close “the Trojan youth”; the Greeks are called Pelasgians, Greeks, and Danaans. The book ends with the Greeks aboard their ships and the Trojan youth pressing the attack, hurling volleys of missiles as shouts resound through the sky.
