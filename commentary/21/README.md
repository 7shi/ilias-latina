Hector, the one man in whom all Troy’s remaining hope of safety lies, goes to meet and fight mighty Achilles, held back neither by fear of death nor by his father’s prayers. When he sees Achilles from afar in heavenly armor, Tritonia Pallas suddenly appears before his eyes. Terrified, Hector flees around the walls of his own city, whose gates are shut, and Achilles pursues him.

Hector is described as Troy’s last hope and as ill-fated; Achilles is called “mighty” and “the Nereian hero,” recalling his mother, the Nereid Thetis, daughter of Nereus. The goddess who appears to Hector is named “Tritonia Pallas,” that is, Minerva. The chase is compared to a dream in which one person pursues and another flees, but their very efforts seem to slow their steps. The book ends with the pursuit still underway, without rest or resolution, as fear on every side rouses their anger.
