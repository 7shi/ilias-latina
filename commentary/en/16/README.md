No longer able to bear the slaughter of his own men, Patroclus, suddenly fortified with Achilles’ armor, rushes forth and terrifies the Trojans with a false semblance. Those who had just been throwing the Danaans into confusion flee in terror, and he presses upon them, throws their lines into disorder and lays men low throughout the host. He fells Sarpedon with a huge wound and wages battle under the semblance of “dread Achilles.”

Seeing him confound the allied companies with slaughter, fiery Hector rouses his courage and, huge in his vast armor, comes to meet him. He challenges him as “bravest Achilles” and boasts that he will learn what an avenging Trojan right hand can do and how strong “the bravest Hector” is in war: even if Mavors, the war god also called Mars, protects him, this right hand will destroy him. Patroclus remains silent and scorns the threats, so as to be taken for the true Achilles.

Hector, here called “Dardanides,” hurls his spear first; Patroclus catches it with a swift stroke and pays back in turn, but what he sends back is struck off by a shield and comes to rest on the ground. They then draw their swords and fight at close quarters until “Trojan Apollo” lays open the features of the counterfeit Achilles and strips him bare. Hector, “greatest in war,” having caught him fighting in false armor, rushes upon him and runs the youth through his bared breast with the sword. The book ends with Hector victorious, stripping off the armor of Vulcan.
