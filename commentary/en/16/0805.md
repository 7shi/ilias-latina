### 805 (*Iliad* 16.2–3)

> 805 Non ualet ulterius cladem spectare suorum
> (No longer can he bear to watch the slaughter of his own men,)

The man who can no longer endure the sight is Patroclus, the close companion of Achilles. With the Trojans threatening to burn the Greek fleet, the story turns from the desperate fighting at the ships to the distress of someone watching his comrades suffer. Achilles, the Greeks’ strongest warrior, has withdrawn from battle after a quarrel with their commander, Agamemnon; Patroclus has remained with him.

Homer introduces Patroclus standing beside Achilles, “shedding hot tears like a dark-watered spring” (*Iliad* 16.2–3). The Latin leaves out both his approach to Achilles and the image of flowing water, expressing his grief instead as an inability to watch any longer. This compressed opening ties his distress directly to the slaughter just described: the men defending the ships are his own comrades, and their suffering has become unbearable.
