### 779 (*Iliad* 14.388–391)

> 779 Hector ubique ferus uiolento pectore saeuit,
> (Hector, fierce, rages everywhere with violent heart,)

Hector, the foremost warrior defending Troy, drives the Trojan resistance as the battle continues. His fury answers the renewed strength that Neptune, the sea god, has given the Greeks defending their ships. Neither side has yet secured victory.

The Latin compresses a wider view of the battlefield into this single fierce portrait. In the corresponding passage of the *Iliad*, Hector draws up the Trojans, while Poseidon, the god called Neptune in the Latin, supports the Greeks. Together these opposing champions bring the conflict to a terrible pitch (*Iliad* 14.388–391). The Latin leaves out that balanced picture and the practical work of arranging the troops. Instead, Hector seems to be everywhere at once, and his furious spirit embodies the continuing violence of the battle.
