### 786 (*Iliad* 14.449–451)

> 786 Polydamas ualido Prothoenora percutit ictu,
> (Polydamas strikes Prothoenor with a powerful blow,)

The general slaughter now comes into focus through a single encounter. Polydamas, a Trojan commander, strikes Prothoenor, a Greek warrior: despite Hector’s removal from the battlefield, the Trojans remain dangerous opponents. In Homer, Polydamas comes to a comrade’s aid and drives his heavy spear through Prothoenor’s right shoulder (*Iliad* 14.449–451). The Latin preserves the two combatants and the force of the attack, but omits their fathers’ names, the weapon, and the precise location of the wound. What was a more closely described act of support becomes one swift, violent stroke in the continuing battle.
