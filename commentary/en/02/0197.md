### 197–198 (*Iliad* 2.713–714, 2.557)

> 197 Eumelus sequitur, minus una naue profectus
> (Eumelus follows, having set out with one ship fewer)
>
> 198 quam duxit Telamone satus Salaminius Aiax.
> (than Ajax of Salamis, son of Telamon, led.)

Eumelus follows the preceding commanders with eleven ships. The Latin gives his contribution indirectly, comparing it with the twelve commanded by Ajax. The catalogue thus links its leaders together rather than simply listing each man and his fleet.

In Homer, Eumelus leads the contingent from Pherae, a city in Thessaly in northern Greece, and is introduced as the son of Admetus: “The beloved son of Admetus commanded their eleven ships, Eumelus” (*Iliad* 2.713–714). The Latin leaves out his homeland and parentage, preserving only his name and the size of his force.

This Ajax is the son of Telamon and ruler of Salamis, an island near Athens. Homer’s brief entry says that “Ajax brought twelve ships from Salamis” (*Iliad* 2.557). Although these two commanders appear far apart in Homer’s catalogue, the Latin brings them together through the comparison of their fleets, retaining Ajax’s island origin and naming his father.
