### 217 (*Iliad* 2.718–719)

> 217 et septem Poeante satus tulit arma carinis,
> (and the son of Poeas brought arms in seven ships,)

The son of Poeas is Philoctetes, a Greek warrior renowned for his skill with the bow. His seven ships add another small contingent to the gathering fleet. Where Homer names him directly and singles out his archery, the Latin identifies him through his father and uses the more general phrase “brought arms.” It also omits Homer’s detail that each ship carried fifty rowers.

As with Protesilaus in the preceding passage, this brief entry leaves out the commander’s absence from the fighting. Homer goes on to explain that Philoctetes had been left on the island of Lemnos, suffering from a painful snakebite, and that Medon commanded his men at Troy. His followers still missed him. The Latin preserves the original leader and the number of ships but passes over the suffering and change of command that give Homer’s catalogue its personal dimension.
