### 191–192 (*Iliad* 2.685)

> 191 quos iuxta Graium murus comitatur Achilles
> (Beside them comes Achilles, the bulwark of the Greeks,)
>
> 192 cum quinquaginta materna per aequora uectus.
> (having sailed with fifty ships across his mother’s seas.)

Achilles now joins the catalogue beside the commanders just named. He is the Greeks’ greatest warrior, and calling him their “bulwark”—literally, their protecting wall—presents his strength as a defense for the whole army. His mother is Thetis, a sea goddess: “his mother’s seas” gives his voyage a personal connection with the divine world, rather than naming a particular stretch of water.

Homer’s corresponding line states simply that Achilles “was commander of fifty ships” (*Iliad* 2.685). The Latin keeps his name and the size of his fleet, but adds both the image of the warrior as the army’s protection and the reminder of his divine parentage. These touches distinguish Achilles within the rapid succession of leaders and ship counts.
