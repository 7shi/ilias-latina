### 243 (*Iliad* 2.846, 2.848)

> 243 Euphemusque ferox clarusque aetate Pyraechmes,
> (and fierce Euphemus and Pyraechmes, famed for his years,)

Two more commanders join the roll of Troy’s allies. Euphemus leads the Cicones, a Thracian people whom Homer describes as spearmen (*Iliad* 2.846). Pyraechmes leads the Paeonians, from the northern Aegean hinterland, whom Homer distinguishes by their curved bows (*Iliad* 2.848). The Latin again omits the peoples’ names, concentrating attention on their leaders. It also replaces Homer’s descriptions of the troops’ weapons with personal distinctions absent from these Greek lines: Euphemus’ fierceness and Pyraechmes’ renown for his years. The catalogue thus continues as a compact procession of notable warriors rather than a detailed account of the forces they command.
