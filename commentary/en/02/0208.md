### 208–209 (*Iliad* 2.645, 2.650–652)

> 208 Idomeneus et Meriones, Cretaeus uterque,
> (Idomeneus and Meriones, both Cretans,)
>
> 209 bis quadragenis muniti nauibus ibant;
> (went forth with a force of eighty ships;)

The catalogue now turns to the contingent from Crete, the large island south of mainland Greece. Its leaders are Idomeneus, the island’s king, and Meriones, his companion in arms. Their eighty ships make this a substantial contribution to the Greek expedition against Troy.

The Latin preserves both commanders and the size of their fleet, but reduces Homer’s account of their homeland to “both Cretans,” omitting his list of Cretan towns. It also leaves out the descriptions that distinguish the two warriors: Homer calls Idomeneus “famed for his spear” and Meriones “equal to Enyalius, slayer of men,” comparing him to a god of war (*Iliad* 2.650–651). Here the emphasis falls instead on their shared origin and the naval force supporting them.
