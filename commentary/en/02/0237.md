### 237 (*Iliad* 2.822–823)

> 237 Archelochusque Acamasque ferox Antenore creti;
> (and Archelochus and fierce Acamas, sons of Antenor;)

Two more Trojan leaders join the muster: Archelochus and Acamas, sons of Antenor, a respected elder and counsellor at Troy. Like the princes named before them, they are identified by their family, placing them among the leading households defending the city.

In Homer, these brothers are specifically Aeneas’s companions in command of the Dardanians: Aeneas does not lead alone, but has beside him Antenor’s two sons, both “well versed in every kind of fighting” (*Iliad* 2.822–823). The Latin keeps their names and parentage but leaves their shared command unstated, drawing them into the larger succession of Trojan warriors. Homer’s praise of both brothers’ experience in battle becomes the single epithet “fierce,” attached to Acamas.
