### 245 (*Iliad* 2.840, 2.844)

> 245 Hippothous uenere Acamasque et Pirous una,
> (Hippothous came, and Acamas and Pirous together,)

Hippothous completes the phrase “skilled in arms” from the preceding verse, as more commanders join Troy’s allied forces. In Homer, he leads the Pelasgians, a people described as fighting with spears, alongside Pylaeus, whom the Latin poem has already named. The abridgment thus separates two leaders who share a command in the fuller account.

Acamas and Pirous lead the Thracians, whose homeland lies north of the Aegean Sea, on its European side. The Latin keeps these two commanders together but again omits the people they lead. What Homer presents as a survey of allied contingents becomes a swift gathering of named warriors, with Hippothous receiving a brief mark of distinction for his skill in battle.
