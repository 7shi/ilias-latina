### 202–203 (*Iliad* 2.638, 2.545, 2.630, 2.644, 2.759)

> 202 Aetola de gente Thoas Andraemone natus,
> (Thoas of the Aetolian people, son of Andraemon,)
>
> 203 hi quadragenas omnes duxere carinas;
> (all these led forty ships each;)

Thoas completes this group of Greek commanders. He leads the Aetolians, a people of western mainland Greece, north of the Gulf of Corinth. His introduction closely follows Homer’s: “Thoas, son of Andraemon, led the Aetolians” (*Iliad* 2.638). The Latin keeps his people and parentage but omits Homer’s list of their towns and the account of the earlier ruling family whose absence has left Thoas in command.

The closing line gathers the preceding leaders into a single statement of naval strength: each brought forty ships, not forty between them. Homer repeatedly gives this number as he finishes describing individual contingents; the Latin saves space by stating their shared total once. After the brief portraits of the commanders, this collective reckoning brings the focus back to the forces they have assembled for the expedition against Troy.
