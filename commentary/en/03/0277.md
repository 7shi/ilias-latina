### 277 (*Iliad* 3.76, 3.111)

> 277 Dicta refert Hector: placuit sententia Grais.
> (Hector relays the words: the proposal pleased the Greeks.)

Hector passes on Paris’s proposal to the Greeks besieging Troy. Paris has agreed to fight Menelaus for Helen, with the understanding that she will follow the victor and that peace will result. The Greeks’ approval turns that agreement between the brothers into a possible settlement between the opposing sides.

The Latin compresses a substantial exchange in Homer into a single line. In the *Iliad*, Hector first “rejoiced greatly when he heard the words” (*Iliad* 3.76); he then brings the armies to a halt and announces the proposed duel. Menelaus responds by calling for sacrifices and solemn oaths to secure the agreement. Only after this do “both Achaeans and Trojans rejoice” (*Iliad* 3.111), hoping for an end to the war. Here Hector’s personal delight and the Trojans’ answering relief disappear. The emphasis falls instead on his role as messenger and on the Greeks’ acceptance: the proposal has crossed the battle line.
