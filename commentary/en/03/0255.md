### 255 (*Iliad* 3.32–35)

> 255 seque uelut uiso perterritus angue recepit
> (and, like a man terrified at the sight of a snake, drew back)

The sight of Menelaus makes Paris recoil. Having taken Helen, Menelaus’s wife, he now shrinks from facing the husband he has wronged. The snake comparison makes his response seem immediate and instinctive: recognition of danger gives way at once to retreat.

Homer describes Paris withdrawing among his companions to escape death, then compares him to someone who encounters a snake in a mountain valley, steps backward with trembling limbs, and turns pale (*Iliad* 3.32–35). The Latin compresses this whole sequence into a single line. It leaves out the mountain setting, the trembling and the pallor, but preserves the essential connection between sudden fright and backward movement. The armed opponent of the preceding line has become, in Paris’s eyes, something to flee.
