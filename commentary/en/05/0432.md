### 432 (*Iliad* 5.59, 5.65–66)

> 432 Meriones Phereclum librata percutit hasta
> (Meriones strikes Phereclus with a poised spear,)

The killing continues as **Meriones**, a Cretan warrior and companion of Idomeneus, strikes **Phereclus**, who fights on the Trojan side. Unlike the preceding death, this encounter preserves Homer’s pairing of killer and victim.

In Homer, Phereclus is a skilled craftsman who built the ships in which Paris sailed to Greece—the voyage that brought Helen to Troy and set the war in motion. His handiwork thus helped bring about the conflict in which he dies. The Latin leaves out that background, as well as the pursuit and the precise wound: Homer describes Meriones catching him as he flees and striking him in the right buttock (*Iliad* 5.65–66). Here the encounter becomes a single spear-strike, keeping the story moving rapidly through the Greek warriors’ victories.
