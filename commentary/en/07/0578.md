### 578 (*Iliad* 7.67, 7.73–75)

> 578 Graiorumque duces inuictis prouocat armis.
> (and challenges the leaders of the Greeks with his unconquered arms.)

Having stepped forward between the armies, Hector challenges the Greek leaders to send a champion against him in single combat. The challenge is addressed to them collectively, but he means to fight one opponent, not the whole group. “Unconquered arms” gives his appearance an air of formidable confidence: the Trojan defender presents himself as a warrior whom no challenger has yet overcome.

The Latin reduces Hector’s public speech to a single decisive action. In Homer, he first calls on both the Trojans and the Greeks to listen (*Iliad* 7.67), then invites whichever of the leading Greeks has the courage to come forward and face him (*Iliad* 7.73–75). The abridgment preserves the challenge and the prominence of the men addressed, but replaces Hector’s own words with the narrator’s compact description. Following his advance into the midst, this leaves the action poised for the Greeks’ response: someone must now decide whether to meet him.
