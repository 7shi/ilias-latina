### 671 (*Iliad* 8.266, 8.269–270)

> 671 Teucer agit spargitque leues in terga sagittas.
> (Teucer drives, and scatters light arrows into their backs.)

The sentence begun in the preceding verse now names Teucer, the Greek archer sheltered by his half-brother Ajax. His targets are the Phrygians, that is the Trojans, named there. The arrows striking their backs suggest that they are giving way before him, extending the Greek recovery begun by Diomedes’ charge.

Homer introduces Teucer as the ninth Greek warrior to enter the renewed fighting, drawing his bow. He then describes a repeated sequence: Teucer looks out from behind Ajax’s shield, shoots a man in the crowd, and leaves him dead where he falls (*Iliad* 8.266–270). The Latin condenses these carefully aimed, individually fatal shots into a shower of arrows. It leaves the deaths to the next verse and here emphasizes Teucer’s pressure on the retreating enemy.
