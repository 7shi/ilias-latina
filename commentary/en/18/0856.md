### 856 (*Iliad* 18.146–148, 18.369)

> 856 fluctibus auxilium Vulcani protinus orat.
> (the waves, at once begs for the help of Vulcan.)

Thetis leaves the sea to seek the armor her son needs. Vulcan, the Roman name for the god whom Homer calls Hephaestus, is the divine smith: his skill can supply Achilles with weapons worthy of his strength. The appeal passes swiftly from son to mother and from mother to craftsman, turning Achilles’ desire for revenge into preparations for his return to battle.

The Latin compresses a much longer movement in Homer. Thetis first sets out toward Olympus, the home of the gods, “to bring glorious armor to her dear son” (*Iliad* 18.146–148); only after the poem returns to events on the battlefield does she reach the smith’s house (*Iliad* 18.369). Here departure, journey and appeal are drawn together into a single continuous action. The word “at once” sustains the urgency of Achilles’ request: Thetis wastes no time in seeking help for him.
