Telamonian Ajax—Ajax, son of Telamon—defends the body of the dead Patroclus, covering it with the shield he holds before it. The Trojans, called “the youth of Priam,” exult with joy, while the Danaans mourn their wounds. The book thus ends with Ajax guarding the body and a contrast between Trojan jubilation and Greek suffering; no recovery or removal of the body is described here.
