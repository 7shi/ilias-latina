### 563 (*Iliad* 6.230, 6.235–236)

> 563 commutant clipeos inimicaque proelia linquunt.
> (they exchange shields and abandon their hostile combat.)

The exchange completes the reconciliation between Glaucus and Diomedes. Their shields, moments before part of their equipment for fighting one another, now become tokens of the inherited friendship between their families. They abandon this encounter, not their places on opposing sides of the war.

The Latin gives a straightforward ending to a more complicated moment in Homer. There Diomedes proposes exchanging armor so that the other warriors will recognize their bond of guest-friendship (*Iliad* 6.230–231). But the narrator adds that Zeus has taken away Glaucus’ good sense: he exchanges golden armor for Diomedes’ bronze, “the worth of a hundred oxen for the worth of nine” (*Iliad* 6.234–236). The oxen express the enormous difference in value between the gifts.

Here the exchange of armor becomes an exchange specifically of shields, and nothing distinguishes their materials or worth. The unequal bargain and the divine interference disappear. After Diomedes’ threatening speech in the preceding passage, this balanced, reciprocal action gives the episode a peaceful close: the two warriors publicly confirm a relationship that overrides their immediate hostility.
