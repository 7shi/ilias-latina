### 1013 (*Iliad* 23.882–883)

> 1013 Merionesque arcu. Tandem certamine misso
> (and Meriones with the bow. At last, with the contest ended,)

Meriones is a Cretan warrior and companion of Idomeneus, the leader of the Cretan contingent. His victory in archery completes the Latin’s rapid account of the funeral games for Patroclus. Like Polypoetes’ victory with the discus, it is reduced to the winner’s name and the event.

In Homer, the archers aim at a dove tied by the foot to a ship’s mast. Teucer cuts the cord but misses the bird, which flies free; Meriones then strikes it in flight. The lines corresponding to this verse describe the distribution of the prizes: Meriones takes all ten double-headed axes, while Teucer carries the ten single-headed axes back to the ships. The Latin omits both the difficult shot and the prizes. Its closing phrase begins the transition away from the games, leaving out Homer’s final event, the spear contest, in which Achilles awards Agamemnon the chief prize without requiring him to compete.
