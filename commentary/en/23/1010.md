### 1010 (*Iliad* 23.725–736)

> 1010 cuius decepit uires Laertius astu;
> (whose strength Laertes’ son foiled by cunning;)

The strength is that of Ajax, whose defeat has just been announced; Laertes’ son is Ulysses. The line explains the result as a victory of cunning over physical power, drawing on a particular moment in Homer’s wrestling match. When Ajax lifts Ulysses, Ulysses strikes him behind the knee, bringing him down and falling onto his chest. Yet this successful trick does not settle the Homeric contest. Ulysses then struggles to lift Ajax, and both tumble into the dust. Before they can grapple again, Achilles stops them and declares both winners. The Latin keeps the memorable contrast between strength and resourcefulness but turns one successful maneuver into the explanation for a decisive victory.
