### 695 (*Iliad* 9.712–713)

> 695 et dapibus curant animos lenique sopore.
> (and they refresh their spirits with food and gentle sleep.)

After the failure of the embassy, the Greeks turn to food and rest. These offer a temporary easing of their distress, not a solution: Achilles still refuses to fight, and the army must face the next day without his promised support. The quiet ending sets aside the unsuccessful negotiations and closes the night’s events.

In Homer, the leaders pour an offering of wine to the gods before each returns to his own shelter, where they lie down and receive “the gift of sleep” (*Iliad* 9.712–713). The Latin leaves out the offering and the separate departures, bringing everyone’s rest into a single compact scene. Food, not mentioned in these two Homeric lines, joins “gentle sleep” as a means of restoring their troubled spirits.
