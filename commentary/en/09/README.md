Stunned by the crisis facing them, the leaders of the Danaans neglect food and bodily care and lament their fate. Urged on by Nestor, they send envoys to Achilles, appealing to his right hand—his pledge of friendship and his might in battle—to bring them aid. Achilles, called “the hero, son of Thetis,” takes in neither their prayers nor any of the king’s gifts. Even his flame returned, Briseis, untouched in her body, fails to move him. The king is not named in this passage but is Agamemnon; the envoys are likewise unnamed. The Greeks are called Danaans and Pelasgians. The book ends with the envoys bringing back Achilles’ fruitless reply, after which the Greeks refresh their spirits with food and gentle sleep. Achilles remains unwilling to help them.
