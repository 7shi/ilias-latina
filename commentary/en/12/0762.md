### 762–766 (*Iliad* 12.467–470, 12.154–155, 12.262–264)

> 762 Irrumpunt aditus Phryges atque in limine primo
> (The Phrygians burst through the entrances, and at the very threshold)
>
> 763 restantes sternunt Graios ualloque cateruas
> (lay low the Greeks who resist, and from the rampart the troops)
>
> 764 deturbant, alii scalas in moenia poscunt
> (they dislodge; others call for ladders against the walls)
>
> 765 et iaciunt ignes: auget uictoria uires.
> (and hurl firebrands: victory increases their strength.)
>
> 766 De muris pugnant Danai turresque per altas.
> (The Danaans fight from the walls and through the lofty towers.)

Hector’s destruction of the gates opens the way for a mass assault. The Phrygians—the Trojans, here called by the name of a people of Asia Minor—pour through the openings and overwhelm those who stand immediately in their path. The Danaans who fight back are, again, the Greeks. The attack spreads across the defenses: troops are driven from the rampart, ladders are demanded, and firebrands are thrown. “Victory increases their strength” captures the momentum of the assault: success gives the attackers fresh energy before the struggle is over.

In Homer, Hector turns to the Trojan ranks and calls on them to cross the wall. They obey, some climbing over it and others streaming through the gates, while the Greeks flee (*Iliad* 12.467–470). The Latin omits Hector’s command and expands the collective action into a broader picture of a fortified position being stormed. The fighting at the threshold, the ladders, and the firebrands have no direct counterpart in those Homeric lines.

The closing glimpse of Greek resistance draws on earlier moments in Homer, when the defenders throw stones from their towers to protect themselves and their huts, and shelter the battlements with shields before striking at the approaching enemy (*Iliad* 12.154–155, 12.262–264). Those details are reduced to a single line and placed after the breakthrough. Instead of ending with Greek flight, the passage leaves the defenses still contested: the Trojans have forced an entrance, but Greeks continue to fight from above.
