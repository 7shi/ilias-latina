### 547–550 (*Iliad* 6.240, 6.269–278, 6.286–287, 6.297–301, 6.308–309)

> 547 imperat et diuae placari numina suadet.
> (he commands, and urges that the goddess’s power be appeased.)
>
> 548 Protinus armatas innuptae Palladis arces
> (At once to the fortified citadel of unwed Pallas)
>
> 549 Iliades subeunt: festis altaria sertis
> (the Iliades ascend; with festive garlands)
>
> 550 exornant caeduntque sacras ex more bidentes.
> (they adorn the altars and slaughter sacrificial sheep according to custom.)

Hector’s command completes his order for Hecuba to be summoned. The response is immediate: the Iliades, the women of Troy, ascend to the goddess’s sanctuary in the citadel, the fortified upper part of the city. Pallas is another name for Athena, identified with the Roman Minerva—the “armed maiden” whose power Hector has just recognized behind the Greek advance. “Unwed” recalls her perpetual virginity. The Trojans are appealing to a goddess worshipped within their own city, yet now helping their enemies. Their garlands and sacrifices seek to turn her hostility into mercy.

The Latin compresses Homer’s carefully staged ceremony into a collective act of worship. In the *Iliad*, Hecuba gathers the older women and brings a precious robe to Athena’s temple. The priestess Theano opens the doors, receives the robe, and lays it on the goddess’s knees while praying for Troy’s deliverance. The women ask Athena to protect the city, its wives and children, especially by stopping Diomedes, the formidable Greek warrior. Here the robe, the priestess, and the specific prayer disappear; decorating the altars with garlands supplies a different visible expression of devotion.

The sacrifice also changes. Homer’s women promise twelve heifers untouched by the yoke if Athena will show mercy (*Iliad* 6.308–309). In the Latin, the women actually slaughter sheep as part of the customary rite. A conditional promise thus becomes an immediate offering, keeping the narrative moving swiftly from Hector’s recognition of divine hostility to Troy’s attempt to appease it.
