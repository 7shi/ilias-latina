### 675 (*Iliad* 8.327–329)

> 675 occupat excussoque incautum proterit arcu.
> (catches him off guard and knocks him down, his bow struck from his hands.)

Hector’s stone brings Teucer’s attack to an abrupt end. Continuing the sentence from the preceding passage, Hector is the attacker and Teucer the unsuspecting victim: the blow knocks the bow from the archer’s hands and brings him down.

Homer describes the injury in stages. Hector strikes with a jagged stone; Teucer’s bowstring breaks, his hand goes numb at the wrist, and he falls to one knee, dropping his bow (*Iliad* 8.327–329). The Latin gathers these movements into a single forceful action, omitting the broken string and the numbed hand but preserving the decisive result. The archer who has just killed Hector’s charioteer is now himself struck down and deprived of his weapon.
