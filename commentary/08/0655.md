### 655 (*Iliad* 8.52)

> 655 Inde acies uidet Iliacas dextraque potenti
> (From there he sees the Ilian battle lines, and with his mighty right hand)

From his seat on Ida, Jupiter looks down over the battlefield. “Ilian” is from Ilium, another name of Troy, the city besieged by the Greeks. Homer describes the god looking out over “the city of the Trojans and the ships of the Achaeans” (*Iliad* 8.52): the city and the Greek fleet mark the two sides of the conflict. The Latin turns his gaze directly toward the troops drawn up for battle. His “mighty right hand” introduces an action that continues in the next verse, preparing to show his power over the fighting below.
