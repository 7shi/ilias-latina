### 650 (*Iliad* 8.1)

> 650 Vt nitidum Titan radiis patefecerat orbem,
> (When Titan had revealed the bright world with his rays,)

A new day begins after the Greeks have burned their dead and strengthened their camp’s defences. Titan is the sun god, whose rays make the world visible again; the name recalls his departure beneath the waves at the end of Hector’s duel with Ajax. The sentence remains unfinished here, setting the scene for the action to follow.

The Latin preserves Homer’s dawn opening but changes its divine figure. In the *Iliad*, “Dawn in her saffron robe spread over all the earth” (8.1). Here the sun takes Dawn’s place, and the color of her clothing gives way to the brightness of his rays.
