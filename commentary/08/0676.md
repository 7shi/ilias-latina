### 676 (*Iliad* 8.330–334)

> 676 Ast illum fidi rapiunt de caede sodales
> (But his faithful comrades snatch him from the slaughter.)

Teucer, struck down by Hector and deprived of his bow, is rescued by his companions. Their swift intervention keeps his fall from becoming his death: “from the slaughter” means out of the killing around him, not that he has already been killed.

Homer gives the rescue more space. Ajax, Teucer’s brother, rushes to stand over him and cover him with his shield. Two comrades, Mecisteus and Alastor, then lift the wounded archer and carry him back toward the Greek ships as he groans heavily (*Iliad* 8.330–334). The Latin compresses this protective stand and evacuation into a single action by unnamed companions. Their loyalty remains central, but the shield, the journey to the ships, and Teucer’s suffering disappear in the rapid movement of the rescue.
