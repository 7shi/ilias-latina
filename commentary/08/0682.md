### 682 (*Iliad* 8.509–511)

> 682 At Phryges obsidunt inclusos aggere Graios
> (But the Phrygians besiege the Greeks, shut in by the rampart,)

The Phrygians are the Trojans, named after the neighboring people of Phrygia. The gates barred in the preceding passage have secured the Greeks’ retreat, but their protection now becomes confinement. The Trojans hold the ground outside, keeping their enemies shut within the fortified camp beside the ships.

The Latin compresses Hector’s preparations for the night into a single statement of siege. In Homer, Hector orders the Trojans to light many fires, with their glow reaching the sky, lest the Greeks attempt to escape under cover of darkness “over the broad back of the sea” (*Iliad* 8.509–511). The Latin leaves out the speech, the fires, and the prospect of flight by sea. Instead, it carries the action directly from the Greeks’ retreat to their containment: they have found shelter, but the Trojans maintain the pressure.
