Achilles, called Aeacides (“descendant of Aeacus”) and “the victor,” lays his mourned friend Patroclus to rest and leads solemn processions for the funeral rites. He then drags Hector’s pitiable remains around the burial mound and announces funeral games in honour of his dead friend.

In the race, Diomedes, called Tydides (“son of Tydeus”), defeats Meriones, described as “fierce of foot.” Aiax (Ajax) loses at wrestling to Odysseus, called Laertius (“son of Laertes”), whose cunning overcomes his opponent’s strength. Epeos defeats all competitors in boxing with leather-bound fists, Polypoetes wins the heavy discus contest, and Meriones wins with the bow.

The book ends with the games concluded and Achilles returning to his camp accompanied by crowds. Patroclus has received his funeral honours, while Hector’s body remains in Achilles’ power after its humiliation at the burial mound.
