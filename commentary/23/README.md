Achilles, called Aeacides (“descendant of Aeacus”) and “the victor,” lays his mourned friend Patroclus to rest and leads solemn processions for the funeral rites. He then drags Hector’s pitiable limbs around the burial mound and proclaims funeral games for the ashes of his dead friend.

In the race, Diomedes, called Tydides (“son of Tydeus”), defeats Meriones, described as “fierce of foot.” Ajax loses at wrestling to Ulysses, called “Laertes’ son,” whose cunning overcomes his opponent’s strength. Epeus overcomes all competitors in boxing with gauntlets, Polypoetes wins the heavy discus contest, and Meriones wins with the bow.

The book ends with the games concluded and Achilles returning to his camp accompanied by crowds. Patroclus has received his funeral honors, while Hector’s body remains in Achilles’ power after its humiliation at the burial mound.
