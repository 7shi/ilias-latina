### 1010 (*Iliad* 23.725–736)

> 1010 cuius decepit uires Laertius astu;
> (whose strength Laertius foiled by cunning;)

The strength is that of Aiax, whose defeat has just been announced; Laertius is Ulysses, named as the son of Laertes. The line explains the result as a victory of cunning over physical power, drawing on a particular moment in Homer’s wrestling match. When Aiax lifts Ulysses, Ulysses strikes him behind the knee, bringing him down and falling onto his chest. Yet this successful trick does not settle the Homeric contest. Ulysses then struggles to lift Aiax, and both tumble into the dust. Before they can grapple again, Achilles stops them and declares both winners. The Latin keeps the memorable contrast between strength and resourcefulness but turns one successful manoeuvre into the explanation for a decisive victory.
