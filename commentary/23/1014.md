### 1014 (*Iliad* 24.1–3)

> 1014 in sua castra redit turbis comitatus Achilles.
> (Achilles returns to his camp, accompanied by crowds.)

With the funeral games for Patroclus over, Achilles returns to his quarters in the Greek camp beside the ships. The sentence begun in the preceding verse carries the story across the boundary between Books 23 and 24 of the *Iliad*, moving directly from the contests to the return.

Homer opens Book 24 with the gathering breaking up: the warriors disperse to their ships and turn their thoughts to supper and the pleasures of sleep. The Latin leaves out these ordinary comforts and focuses instead on Achilles, whom it pictures returning with crowds around him. In Homer, the dispersal prepares a contrast between the other warriors’ rest and Achilles’ continuing grief for Patroclus, his dead companion. The games have honored Patroclus, but they have not brought Achilles peace.
