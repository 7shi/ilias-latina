### 1012 (*Iliad* 23.826, 23.844–849)

> 1012 et disco forti Polypoetes depulit omnes
> (And with the heavy discus Polypoetes outdid them all.)

Polypoetes is a leader of the Lapiths, a people of Thessaly in northern Greece. The Latin moves directly from boxing to the discus, again reducing a whole contest to the winner’s name and his superiority over the field. Homer’s competitors throw a solid mass of iron rather than the familiar flat athletic discus; the iron itself is also the prize, useful for making agricultural tools.

In Homer, Polypoetes throws last and sends the weight far beyond his rivals’ marks—as far beyond them as a herdsman throws his staff through a herd of cattle. The spectators shout, and his companions carry the prize back to the ships. The Latin omits these details but preserves the decisive margin of his victory.
