### 1011 (*Iliad* 23.664–665)

> 1011 caestibus aduersis cunctos superauit Epeos
> (with gauntlets face to face, Epeus overcame them all;)

Epeus is the son of Panopeus, whom Homer introduces as a tall, powerful man skilled in boxing. The fighters bound their hands with leather straps rather than wearing modern padded gloves. After the wrestling match, the Latin now gives the boxing result in a single line; Homer places boxing before wrestling and tells the contest at greater length. Epeus boasts that no one can defeat him, and only one challenger, Euryalus, comes forward. Epeus knocks him down with a blow to the cheek, then helps him up; his companions lead him away, dazed and spitting blood. “Overcame them all” thus presents Epeus as the undisputed champion rather than describing a series of victories.
