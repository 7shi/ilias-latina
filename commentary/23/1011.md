### 1011 (*Iliad* 23.664–665)

> 1011 caestibus aduersis cunctos superauit Epeos
> (In boxing with leather-bound fists, Epeos defeated them all.)

Epeos is Epeius, son of Panopeus, whom Homer introduces as a tall, powerful man skilled in boxing. The fighters bound their hands with leather straps rather than wearing modern padded gloves. After the wrestling match, the Latin now gives the boxing result in a single line; Homer places boxing before wrestling and tells the contest at greater length. Epeius boasts that no one can defeat him, and only one challenger, Euryalus, comes forward. Epeius knocks him down with a blow to the cheek, then helps him up; his companions lead him away, dazed and spitting blood. “Defeated them all” thus presents Epeos as the undisputed champion rather than describing a series of victories.
