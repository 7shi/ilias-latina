### 737–740 (*Iliad* 10.532, 10.540–545, 10.575–579)

#### Line 737

> 737 ad classes redeunt, quos Nestoris accipit aetas
>
> 738 ac recipit portis. Postquam sua castra tenebant,
>
> 739 facta duci referunt: laudat Pelopeius heros,
>
> 740 fessaque iucundae tradunt sua membra quieti.

What is Argive is the fleet, given as *ad classes*. They return to it, told with *redeunt*. The two Greeks come back, as *quos*. Nestor comes in, named *Nestoris*. Someone hears them, told with *accipit*. The one who hears them is Nestor's age, which closes the line as *aetas*.

More is added, marked by *ac*. His age welcomes them, told with *recipit*. Gates come in, named *portis*. A time is set, marked by *Postquam*. Something is their own, described as *sua*, though what it is is not yet said. What is their own is their camp, named *castra*. They held it, which closes the line as *tenebant*.

Deeds come in, named *facta*. The leader comes in, named *duci*. They report them, told with *referunt*. Someone praises them, told with *laudat*. Someone is Pelopeian, described as *Pelopeius*, though who it is is not yet said. The Pelopeian one is a hero, who closes the line as *heros*. As the commentary says, the Pelopeian hero is Agamemnon.

Something is weary as well, described as *fessaque*. Something is welcome, described as *iucundae*. They surrender something, told with *tradunt*. Something is their own, described as *sua*. What is weary is their limbs, named *membra*. What is welcome is rest, which ends the tenth book as *quieti*.
