### 730–733 (*Iliad* 10.471, 10.494–497, 10.483–488, 10.498–501)

#### Lines 730–733

> 730 intrant atque ipsum somno uinoque sepultum
>
> 731 obtruncant spoliantque uirum fusosque per herbam
>
> 732 exanimant socios. Tum tristi caede peracta
>
> 733 praeda umeros onerant, multo et candore nitentes

They enter them, told with *intrant*. More is added, marked by *atque*. Rhesus comes in, as *ipsum*. Sleep comes in, named *somno*. Wine is joined as well, named *uinoque*. He is buried in them, which closes the line as *sepultum*.

They cut him down, told with *obtruncant*. They strip him as well, told with *spoliantque*. He is the man, named *uirum*. Some are stretched out as well, described as *fusosque*, though who they are is not yet said. The grass comes in, which closes the line as *per herbam*.

They kill them, told with *exanimant*. Those who are stretched out are his companions, named *socios*. A time is set, marked by *Tum*. Something is grim, described as *tristi*. What is grim is slaughter, named *caede*. The slaughter is finished, which closes the line as *peracta*.

Plunder comes in, named *praeda*. Shoulders come in, named *umeros*. They load them, told with *onerant*. Something is great, described as *multo*. More is added, marked by *et*. Whiteness comes in, named *candore*. Some are gleaming with it, which closes the line as *nitentes*. What is gleaming comes only in the next section.
