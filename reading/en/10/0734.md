### 734–736 (*Iliad* 10.436–437, 10.513–514, 10.526–531)

#### Lines 734–735

> 734 Thracas equos rapiunt, quos nec praecederet Eurus
>
> 735 nec posset uolucri cursu superare sagitta.

Some are Thracian, described as *Thracas*, though what they are is not yet said. What is Thracian and gleaming is horses, named *equos*. They seize them, told with *rapiunt*. The horses come back, as *quos*. A denial comes, marked by *nec*. Someone could outrun them, told with *praecederet*. The one who could not is the east wind, which closes the line as *Eurus*.

Another denial follows, marked by *nec*. Something could do so, told with *posset*. Something is flying, described as *uolucri*. What is flying is its course, named *cursu*. Surpassing comes next, told with *superare*. What could not is an arrow, which closes the line as *sagitta*.

#### Line 736

> 736 Inde iterum Argolicas primae sub tempore lucis

A place is set, marked by *Inde*. It is again, marked by *iterum*. Something is Argive, described as *Argolicas*, though what it is is not yet said. As the commentary says, the Argives are the Greeks. Something is first, described as *primae*. A time comes in, given as *sub tempore*. What is first is light, which closes the line as *lucis*. Where they return comes only in the next section.
