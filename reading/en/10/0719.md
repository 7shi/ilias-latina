### 719–727 (*Iliad* 10.391–399, 10.405–414)

#### Lines 719–724

> 719 maxima Troia mihi currum promisit Achillis
>
> 720 si uestras cepisset opes. Haec dona secutus
>
> 721 in dubios casus, coram quod cernitis ipsi,
>
> 722 infelix cecidi. Nunc uos per numina diuum,
>
> 723 per mare, per Ditis fluctus obtestor opaci,
>
> 724 ne rapere hanc animam crudeli caede uelitis.

Something is mighty, described as *maxima*, though what it is is not yet said. What is mighty is Troy, named *Troia*. The promise is to Dolon, as *mihi*. A chariot comes in, named *currum*. Troy promised it, told with *promisit*. The chariot belongs to Achilles, who closes the line as *Achillis*.

A condition is set, marked by *si*. Something is theirs, described as *uestras*. Troy would capture it, told with *cepisset*. What is theirs is wealth, named *opes*. These come in, as *Haec*. They are gifts, named *dona*. Dolon is pursuing them, which closes the line as *secutus*.

Something is into what is uncertain, given as *in dubios*, though what it is is not yet said. What is uncertain is hazards, named *casus*. It is openly, marked by *coram*. This is what they see, marked by *quod*. They see it, told with *cernitis*. They see it themselves, which closes the line as *ipsi*.

He is wretched, described as *infelix*. He has fallen, told with *cecidi*. A time is set, marked by *Nunc*. He turns to them, as *uos*. He appeals by divine powers, given as *per numina*. The powers are of the gods, who close the line as *diuum*.

He appeals by the sea, given as *per mare*. Dis comes in, given as *per Ditis*. He appeals by his waves, named *fluctus*. He implores them, told with *obtestor*. Dis is dark, which closes the line as *opaci*.

A plea not to do something follows, marked by *ne*. Snatching comes next, told with *rapere*. It is this, as *hanc*. What is to be snatched is his life, named *animam*. Something is cruel, described as *crudeli*. The cruel thing is slaughter, named *caede*. They should not wish to do so, which closes the line as *uelitis*.

#### Lines 725–727

> 725 Haec pro concessa referetis dona salute:
>
> 726 consilium Priami regis remque ordine gentis
>
> 727 expediam Phrygiae." Postquam quid Troia pararet

These come in, as *Haec*. Something is in return for what is granted, given as *pro concessa*, though what it is is not yet said. They will carry something back, told with *referetis*. They are gifts, named *dona*. What is granted is his life, which closes the line as *salute*.

A plan comes in, named *consilium*. The plan is of Priam, named *Priami*. Priam is king, named *regis*. Affairs come in as well, named *remque*. It is in order, named *ordine*. The affairs belong to a people, who close the line as *gentis*.

Dolon will set them out, told with *expediam*. The people are Phrygian, named *Phrygiae*. As the commentary says, the Phrygian people are the Trojans. A time comes after, marked by *Postquam*. What something was comes next, marked by *quid*. Troy comes back, named *Troia*. Troy was preparing something, which closes the line as *pararet*. What happened after comes only in the next section.
