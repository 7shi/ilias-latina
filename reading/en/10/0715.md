### 715–718 (*Iliad* 10.376–381, 10.384–389)

#### Line 715

> 715 Ille timore pauens: "Vitam concedite" - dixit -,

Dolon comes back, as *Ille*. Fear comes in, named *timore*. He is trembling, described as *pauens*. Life comes in, named *Vitam*. He pleads for it, told with *concedite*. He says so, which closes the line as *dixit*.

#### Lines 716–717

> 716 "hoc unum satis est; quodsi perstatis in ira,
>
> 717 quanta ex morte mea capietis praemia laudis?

This comes in, as *hoc*. It is alone, described as *unum*. It is enough, described as *satis*. It is so, told with *est*. A condition follows, marked by *quodsi*. They persist, told with *perstatis*. Anger comes in, which closes the line as *in ira*.

Something is how great, described as *quanta*, though what it is is not yet said. Death comes in, given as *ex morte*. The death is his own, described as *mea*. They will gain something, told with *capietis*. What is so great is rewards, named *praemia*. The rewards are of glory, which closes the line as *laudis*.

#### Line 718

> 718 At si cur ueniam tacitis exquiritis umbris:

The plea turns, marked by *At*. A condition is set, marked by *si*. A reason is asked, marked by *cur*. He comes, told with *ueniam*. Something is silent, described as *tacitis*, though what it is is not yet said. They ask, told with *exquiritis*. What is silent is shadows, which closes the line as *umbris*. What he tells them comes only in the next section.
