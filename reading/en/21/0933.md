### 933–936 (*Iliad* 22.37–38, 22.131–135, 22.226–231)

#### Lines 933–934

> 933 non patriae tenuere preces, quin obuius iret
>
> 934 et contra magnum contendere uellet Achillem.

A denial comes, marked by *non*. Something is his father's, described as *patriae*, though what it is is not yet said. They held him back, told with *tenuere*. What is his father's is prayers, named *preces*. A prevention comes, marked by *quin*. Someone is to meet him, described as *obuius*. He would go, which closes the line as *iret*.

More is added, marked by *et*. Opposition comes in, marked by *contra*. Someone is mighty, described as *magnum*, though who it is is not yet said. To contend comes in, told with *contendere*. He would wish, told with *uellet*. Who is mighty is Achilles, who closes the line as *Achillem*.

#### Lines 935–936

> 935 Quem procul ut uidit tectum caelestibus armis,
>
> 936 ante oculos subito uisa est Tritonia Pallas

Achilles comes back, as *Quem*. A distance comes in, marked by *procul*. A time is set, marked by *ut*. He saw him, told with *uidit*. He is covered, described as *tectum*. Heavenly armor comes in, which closes the line as *caelestibus armis*.

Before his eyes comes in, given as *ante oculos*. It is suddenly, marked by *subito*. She appeared, given as *uisa est*. Someone is Tritonian, described as *Tritonia*. Pallas comes in, who closes the line as *Pallas*. As the commentary says, Tritonia Pallas is Minerva. What follows comes only in the next section.
