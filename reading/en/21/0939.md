### 939–943 (*Iliad* 22.199–201)

#### Lines 939–941

> 939 in somnis ueluti, cum pectora terruit ira,
>
> 940 hic cursu super insequitur, fugere ille uidetur,
>
> 941 festinantque ambo, gressum labor ipse moratur,

Dreams come in, given as *in somnis*. A likeness comes in, marked by *ueluti*. A time is set, marked by *cum*. The heart comes in, named *pectora*. Something has terrified it, told with *terruit*. Anger comes in, which closes the line as *ira*.

One of them comes in, as *hic*. A race comes in, named *cursu*. A place above comes in, marked by *super*. He pursues, told with *insequitur*. Fleeing comes next, told with *fugere*. The other comes in, as *ille*. He seems, which closes the line as *uidetur*.

They hurry as well, told with *festinantque*. Both come in, described as *ambo*. Steps come in, named *gressum*. Effort itself comes in, given as *labor ipse*. It slows them, which closes the line as *moratur*.

#### Lines 942–943

> 942 alternis poterant insistere coepta periclis,
>
> 943 nec requies aderat, timor undique concitat iras.

Something is by turns, described as *alternis*, though what it is is not yet said. They could, told with *poterant*. Pressing on comes next, told with *insistere*. Something begun comes in, described as *coepta*. What is by turns is dangers, which close the line as *periclis*.

A denial comes, marked by *nec*. Rest comes in, named *requies*. It was at hand, told with *aderat*. Fear comes in, named *timor*. Every side comes in, marked by *undique*. It rouses something, told with *concitat*. Anger comes in, which ends the twenty-first book as *iras*.
