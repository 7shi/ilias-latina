### 553–562 (*Iliad* 6.119–127, 6.215, 5.336, 5.855–858, 6.226, 6.232–233)

#### Lines 553–556

> 553 interea Glaucus stricto decernere ferro
>
> 554 cum Diomede parat nomenque genusque roganti
>
> 555 qui sit et unde ferat, magnis cum uiribus hastam
>
> 556 mittere temptabat; temptanti Aetolius heros:

A time meanwhile comes in, marked by *interea*. A warrior appears, named *Glaucus*. His weapon was drawn, described as *stricto*. He seeks to fight it out, told with *decernere*. The drawn weapon is steel, which closes the line as *ferro*.

It is with Diomedes, given as *cum Diomede*. He prepares for it, told with *parat*. A name comes in as well, called *nomenque*. Lineage comes in too, named *genusque*. Diomedes was asking for both, which closes the line as *roganti*.

He asked who he was, given as *qui sit*. More follows, marked by *et*. He asked whence he came, given as *unde ferat*. Something was great, described as *magnis*. It was with strength, given as *cum uiribus*. His weapon is a spear, which closes the line as *hastam*.

He sought to hurl it, as *mittere*. He was trying to do so, told with *temptabat*. The hero turns to him as he tried, described as *temptanti*. The warrior is Aetolian, described as *Aetolius*. As the commentary says, the Aetolian hero is Diomedes. He is a hero, who closes the line as *heros*.

#### Lines 557–561

> 557 "Quo ruis?" - exclamat - "quae te, scelerate, furentem
>
> 558 mens agit imparibus mecum concurrere telis?
>
> 559 Hospitis arma uides, Veneris qui uulnere dextram
>
> 560 perculit et summo pupugit certamine Martem.
>
> 561 Pone truces animos infestaque tela coerce."

He asks: *Quo ruis?* He cries out, told with *exclamat*. He asks what it is, beginning with *quae*, though what it is is not yet said. Glaucus is addressed, as *te*. He calls him a villain, named *scelerate*. He is raging, described as *furentem*.

What drives him is a mind, called *mens*. It drives him, told with *agit*. The weapons are unequal, described as *imparibus*. It is with him, given as *mecum*. He challenges him to clash, told with *concurrere*. What is unequal is weapons, which close the line as *telis*.

A guest-friend comes in, named *Hospitis*. The guest-friend has arms, called *arma*. He bids him see them, told with *uides*. The goddess is Venus, named *Veneris*. Diomedes refers to himself, as *qui*. He struck with a wound, called *uulnere*. What was struck was her right hand, which closes the line as *dextram*.

He struck it, told with *perculit*. More follows, marked by *et*. It was at the height, described as *summo*. He pierced someone, told with *pupugit*. The height was of the contest, called *certamine*. The god pierced was Mars, which closes the line as *Martem*.

He commands him to put it aside, told with *Pone*. His feelings are savage, described as *truces*. The savage feelings are anger, named *animos*. His weapons too are hostile, described as *infestaque*. The hostile things are weapons, called *tela*. He bids him hold them back, told with *coerce*.

#### Line 562

> 562 Post haec inter se posito certamine pugnae

The time after this comes in, given as *Post haec*. They turn to each other, as *inter se*. The contest was set aside, described as *posito*. What was set aside is the contest, called *certamine*. The contest was of fighting, which closes the line as *pugnae*, though what comes of it comes only in the next section.
