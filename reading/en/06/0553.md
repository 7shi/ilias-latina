### 553–562 (*Iliad* 6.119–127, 6.215, 5.336, 5.855–858, 6.226, 6.232–233)

#### Lines 553–556

> 553 interea Glaucus stricto decernere ferro
>
> 554 cum Diomede parat nomenque genusque roganti
>
> 555 qui sit et unde ferat, magnis cum uiribus hastam
>
> 556 mittere temptabat; temptanti Aetolius heros:

Time moves on, marked by *interea*. Glaucus comes in, named *Glaucus*. Something is drawn, described as *stricto*, though what it is is not yet said. Fighting it out comes next, told with *decernere*. What was drawn is steel, which closes the line as *ferro*.

It is with Diomedes, given as *cum Diomede*. Glaucus makes ready, told with *parat*. A name comes in as well, named *nomenque*. Lineage comes in too, named *genusque*. Diomedes is asking for them, which closes the line as *roganti*.

He asks who Glaucus is, given as *qui sit*. More follows, joined by *et*. He asks whence he bears his arms, given as *unde ferat*. Something is great, described as *magnis*. It is strength, given as *cum uiribus*. A spear comes in, which closes the line as *hastam*.

Hurling comes next, told with *mittere*. Glaucus was trying it, told with *temptabat*. Someone speaks to him as he tries, described as *temptanti*. Someone is Aetolian, described as *Aetolius*. He is a hero, which closes the line as *heros*. As the commentary says, the Aetolian hero is Diomedes, whose family came from Aetolia.

#### Lines 557–561

> 557 "Quo ruis?" - exclamat - "quae te, scelerate, furentem
>
> 558 mens agit imparibus mecum concurrere telis?
>
> 559 Hospitis arma uides, Veneris qui uulnere dextram
>
> 560 perculit et summo pupugit certamine Martem.
>
> 561 Pone truces animos infestaque tela coerce."

Diomedes asks where Glaucus is rushing, given as *Quo ruis*. He cries out, told with *exclamat*. Something is asked, beginning with *quae*, though what it is is not yet said. Glaucus comes in, given as *te*. Diomedes names him a villain, called *scelerate*. He is raging, which closes the line as *furentem*.

What was asked about is a mind, named *mens*. It drives him, told with *agit*. Something is unequal, described as *imparibus*. It is with Diomedes, given as *mecum*. Clashing comes next, told with *concurrere*. What is unequal is weapons, which close the line as *telis*.

A guest-friend comes in, named *Hospitis*. Arms come in, named *arma*. Glaucus sees them, told with *uides*. Venus comes in, named *Veneris*. The guest-friend comes back, as *qui*. A wound comes in, named *uulnere*. What was Venus' is her right hand, which closes the line as *dextram*.

He struck it, told with *perculit*. More follows, joined by *et*. Something is at its height, described as *summo*, though what it is is not yet said. He pierced someone, told with *pupugit*. What was at its height is the contest, named *certamine*. The one pierced is Mars, which closes the line as *Martem*. As the commentary says, Venus and Mars are the Roman counterparts of Aphrodite and Ares.

Glaucus is to put something aside, told with *Pone*. Something is savage, described as *truces*. It is his spirit, named *animos*. Something is hostile as well, described as *infestaque*. It is weapons, named *tela*. He is to hold them back, which closes the speech as *coerce*.

#### Line 562

> 562 Post haec inter se posito certamine pugnae

It is after this, given as *Post haec*. It is between them, given as *inter se*. Something is set aside, described as *posito*. It is the contest, named *certamine*. The contest is of fighting, which closes the line as *pugnae*. What they do then comes only in the next section.
