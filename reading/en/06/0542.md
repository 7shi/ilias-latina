### 542–546 (*Iliad* 6.73–76, 6.94–97, 6.86–87, 6.237)

#### Lines 542–546

> 542 Incumbunt Danai, cedit Troiana iuuentus
>
> 543 tergaque nuda tegit. Sensit Mauortius Hector
>
> 544 pro Danais pugnare deos ualidasque suorum
>
> 545 uirginis armigerae subduci numine uires
>
> 546 continuoque petit muros Hecubamque uocari

The warriors press forward, told with *Incumbunt*. They are the Danaans, named *Danai*. As the commentary says, the Danaans are the Greeks. The other side gives way, told with *cedit*. They are Trojan, described as *Troiana*. The Trojans are the young warriors, called *iuuentus*.

Their backs come in as well, called *tergaque*. They were exposed, described as *nuda*. The army covers them, as *tegit*. A leader perceived it, told with *Sensit*. He is warlike, described as *Mauortius*. The leader is Hector, called *Hector*.

It was for the Danaans, given as *pro Danais*. They were fighting, told with *pugnare*. Those who fought were gods, named *deos*. Something of his men was mighty as well, described as *ualidasque*, though what it is is not yet said. The men were his own, described as *suorum*.

A maiden comes in, named *uirginis*. She is armed, described as *armigerae*. As the commentary says, the armed maiden is Minerva. It was being drained, told with *subduci*. It was by her divine power, named *numine*. What was mighty is strength, called *uires*.

At once something follows, marked by *continuoque*. He heads for a place, told with *petit*. The place is the walls, called *muros*. He turns to Hecuba as well, named *Hecubamque*. She is to be summoned, told with *uocari*, though what is done about summoning her comes only in the next section.
