### 547–550 (*Iliad* 6.240, 6.269–278, 6.286–287, 6.297–301, 6.308–309)

#### Line 547

> 547 imperat et diuae placari numina suadet.

He commands it, told with *imperat*. More follows, marked by *et*. A goddess comes in, named *diuae*. She is to be appeased, told with *placari*. What is to be appeased is her divine will, called *numina*. He urges this, told with *suadet*.

#### Lines 548–550

> 548 Protinus armatas innuptae Palladis arces
>
> 549 Iliades subeunt: festis altaria sertis
>
> 550 exornant caeduntque sacras ex more bidentes.

The response is at once, marked by *Protinus*. Something was fortified, described as *armatas*, though what it is is not yet said. The goddess there is unwed, described as *innuptae*. The unwed one is Pallas, named *Palladis*. What was fortified is the citadel, which closes the line as *arces*.

The women come in, called *Iliades*. As the commentary says, the Iliades are the Trojan women. They go up, told with *subeunt*. Something was festive, described as *festis*. The altars come in, named *altaria*. The festive things are garlands, which close the line as *sertis*.

They adorn them, as *exornant*. They slaughter offerings as well, told with *caeduntque*. The victims were sacred, described as *sacras*. It was according to custom, given as *ex more*. The sacred victims were sheep, which close the line as *bidentes*.
