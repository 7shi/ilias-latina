### 547–550 (*Iliad* 6.240, 6.269–278, 6.286–287, 6.297–301, 6.308–309)

#### Line 547

> 547 imperat et diuae placari numina suadet.

Hector orders it, told with *imperat*. More follows, joined by *et*. A goddess comes in, named *diuae*, though what of hers is meant is not yet said. Something is to be appeased, told with *placari*. What was hers is her divine power, named *numina*. He urges it, which closes the line as *suadet*.

#### Lines 548–550

> 548 Protinus armatas innuptae Palladis arces
>
> 549 Iliades subeunt: festis altaria sertis
>
> 550 exornant caeduntque sacras ex more bidentes.

It is at once, marked by *Protinus*. Something is armed, described as *armatas*, though what it is is not yet said. Someone is unwed, described as *innuptae*. The unwed one is Pallas, named *Palladis*. As the commentary says, Pallas is Minerva. What was armed is her citadel, which closes the line as *arces*.

The Iliades come in, named *Iliades*. As the commentary says, the Iliades are the women of Troy. They go up, told with *subeunt*. Something is festive, described as *festis*, though what it is is not yet said. Altars come in, named *altaria*. What was festive is garlands, which close the line as *sertis*.

They adorn the altars, told with *exornant*. They slaughter as well, told with *caeduntque*. Something is sacred, described as *sacras*. It is according to custom, given as *ex more*. What is sacred is sheep, which close the line as *bidentes*.
