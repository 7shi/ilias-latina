### 1018–1021 (*Iliad* 22.466–470, 24.200–205)

#### Line 1018

> 1018 Andromacheque suas scindit de pectore uestes,

Andromache comes in as well, called *Andromacheque*. As the commentary says, Andromache is Hector's wife. Something of her own comes in, described as *suas*, though what it is is not yet said. She tears something, told with *scindit*. The breast comes in, given as *de pectore*. What is her own is clothes, which close the line as *uestes*.

#### Lines 1019–1021

> 1019 heu tanto spoliata uiro. Ruit omnis in uno
>
> 1020 Hectore causa Phrygum, ruit hoc defensa senectus
>
> 1021 afflicti miseranda patris, quem nec sua coniunx

A cry of grief comes, marked by *heu*. Someone is so great, described as *tanto*. She is robbed, described as *spoliata*. A husband comes in, named *uiro*. Something falls, told with *Ruit*. Something is all, described as *omnis*. One man comes in, given as *in uno*, though who it is is not yet said.

Who the one man is is Hector, called *Hectore*. The cause comes in, named *causa*. The Trojans come back, called *Phrygum*. Something falls again, told with *ruit*. Hector comes back, as *hoc*. Something is defended, described as *defensa*. Old age comes in, named *senectus*. Someone is stricken, described as *afflicti*. Something is pitiable, described as *miseranda*. A father comes in, named *patris*. As the commentary says, the father is Priam, Troy's king. The father comes back, as *quem*. A denial comes, marked by *nec*. Someone of his own comes in, described as *sua*. A wife comes in, which closes the line as *coniunx*. As the commentary says, the wife is Hecuba. What follows comes only in the next section.
