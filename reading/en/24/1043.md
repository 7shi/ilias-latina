### 1043–1045 (*Iliad* 24.515–516, 24.589–590, 24.579)

#### Lines 1043–1045

> 1043 His tandem precibus grandaeuum motus Achilles
>
> 1044 alleuat a terra corpusque exsangue parenti
>
> 1045 reddidit Hectoreum. Post haec sua dona reportat

These come in, as *His*. At last something changes, marked by *tandem*. Prayers come in, named *precibus*. An aged man comes in, described as *grandaeuum*. Someone is moved, described as *motus*. Achilles comes in, called *Achilles*.

He raises him, told with *alleuat*. The ground comes in, given as *a terra*. A body comes in as well, named *corpusque*. It is lifeless, described as *exsangue*. A parent comes in, named *parenti*. He gave it back, told with *reddidit*. It is Hector's, described as *Hectoreum*. A time is set, given as *Post haec*. Something of his own comes in, described as *sua*, though what it is is not yet said. What is his own is gifts, named *dona*. As the commentary says, the gifts are Hector's body, not the ransom. Someone carries them back, which closes the line as *reportat*. Who carries them back comes only in the next section.
