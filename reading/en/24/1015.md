### 1015–1016 (*Iliad* 22.405–411)

#### Lines 1015–1016

> 1015 Flent miseri amissum Phryges Hectora totaque maesto
>
> 1016 Troia sonat planctu; fundit miseranda querelas

Some weep, told with *Flent*. They are wretched, described as *miseri*. Someone is lost, described as *amissum*. Those who weep are the Phrygians, called *Phryges*. As the commentary says, the Phrygians are the Trojans. Who is lost is Hector, called *Hectora*. Something is whole as well, described as *totaque*, though what it is is not yet said. Something is mournful, described as *maesto*, though what it is is not yet said.

What is whole is Troy, named *Troia*. It resounds, told with *sonat*. What is mournful is lamentation, named *planctu*. Someone pours out something, told with *fundit*. She is pitiable, described as *miseranda*. Laments come in, which close the line as *querelas*. Who she is comes only in the next section.
