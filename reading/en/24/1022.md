### 1022–1024 (*Iliad* 24.189–190)

#### Lines 1022–1024

> 1022 turbaque natorum nec magni gloria regni
>
> 1023 oblitum tenuit uitae, quin iret inermis
>
> 1024 et solum inuicti castris se redderet hostis.

A throng comes in as well, named *turbaque*. Children come in, named *natorum*. Another denial comes, marked by *nec*. Something is great, described as *magni*, though what it is is not yet said. Glory comes in, named *gloria*. What is great is a kingdom, named *regni*. He is forgetful, described as *oblitum*. Something held him back, told with *tenuit*. Life comes in, named *uitae*. Something is kept from happening, marked by *quin*. He is to go, told with *iret*. He is unarmed, described as *inermis*.

More is added, marked by *et*. He is alone, described as *solum*. Someone is undefeated, described as *inuicti*, though who it is is not yet said. A camp comes in, named *castris*. He does something to himself, given as *se*. He is to give himself up, told with *redderet*. Who is undefeated is an enemy, who closes the line as *hostis*. As the commentary says, the undefeated enemy is Achilles.
