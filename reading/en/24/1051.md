### 1051–1070 (*Iliad* 24.787)

#### Line 1051

> 1051 Haec super ingenti gemitu componitur Hector:

These things come in, as *Haec*. Above comes in, marked by *super*. Great groaning comes in, given as *ingenti gemitu*. Someone is laid, told with *componitur*. The one laid is Hector, who closes the line as *Hector*.

#### Lines 1052–1053

> 1052 stant circum Iliades matres manibusque decoros
>
> 1053 abrumpunt crines laniataque pectora plangunt:

Some stand, told with *stant*. Around comes in, marked by *circum*. Some are Ilian, described as *Iliades*. Mothers come in, named *matres*. As the commentary says, the Ilian mothers are the women of Troy. Hands come in as well, named *manibusque*. Some are lovely, described as *decoros*, though what they are is not yet said.

They loosen them, told with *abrumpunt*. What they are is hair, named *crines*. Torn breasts come in as well, given as *laniataque pectora*. They beat them, told with *plangunt*.

#### Line 1054

> 1054 illo namque rogo natorum funera cernunt.

That one comes in, as *illo*. A reason is given, marked by *namque*. A pyre comes in, named *rogo*. Sons come in, named *natorum*. Funerals come in, named *funera*. They see them, which closes the line as *cernunt*.

#### Lines 1055–1056

> 1055 Tollitur et iuuenum magno cum murmure clamor
>
> 1056 flebilis: ardebat flamma namque Ilion illa.

Something is raised, told with *Tollitur*. More is added, marked by *et*. Young men come in, named *iuuenum*. Something is great, described as *magno*. Murmuring comes in, given as *cum murmure*. A cry comes in, named *clamor*.

It is mournful, described as *flebilis*. It was burning, told with *ardebat*. A flame comes in, named *flamma*. Another reason is given, marked by *namque*. Ilion comes in, called *Ilion*. That one comes back, which closes the line as *illa*.

#### Lines 1057–1062

> 1057 Inter quos gemitus laniato pectore coniunx
>
> 1058 prouolat Andromache mediosque immittere in ignes
>
> 1059 se cupit Astyanacta tenens, quam iussa suarum
>
> 1060 turba rapit. Contra tamen omnibus usque resistit,
>
> 1061 donec collapsae ceciderunt robora flammae
>
> 1062 inque leues abiit tantus dux ille fauillas.

Those groans come back, as *Inter quos*. Groans come in, named *gemitus*. Her torn breast comes in, given as *laniato pectore*. A wife comes in, named *coniunx*. She rushes forward, told with *prouolat*. The wife is Andromache, called *Andromache*. Something is in the midst as well, described as *mediosque*. Throwing comes next, told with *immittere*. Flames come in, given as *in ignes*. She does something to herself, as *se*. She longs for it, told with *cupit*. Astyanax comes in, called *Astyanacta*. As the commentary says, Astyanax is her son. She is holding him, described as *tenens*. She comes back, as *quam*. She is ordered, described as *iussa*. Some are her own, described as *suarum*, though what they are is not yet said.

What they are is a throng, named *turba*. They snatch her away, told with *rapit*. Against them comes in, marked by *Contra*. Something is set against this, marked by *tamen*. All come in, described as *omnibus*. Continuously comes in, marked by *usque*. She resists, told with *resistit*. A time is set until then, marked by *donec*. Something is collapsing, described as *collapsae*. They fell, told with *ceciderunt*. Strength comes in, named *robora*. A flame comes in, named *flammae*. Into comes in as well, marked by *inque*. Some are light, described as *leues*. He passed away, told with *abiit*. Someone is so great, described as *tantus*. A leader comes in, named *dux*. That one comes in, as *ille*. Ashes come in, which close the line as *fauillas*.

#### Lines 1063–1066

> 1063 Sed iam siste gradum finemque impone labori,
>
> 1064 Calliope, uatisque tui moderare carinam,
>
> 1065 Remis quem cernis stringentem litora paucis,
>
> 1066 Iamque tenet portum metamque potentis Homeri.

Something turns, marked by *Sed*. Now comes in, marked by *iam*. The one addressed is to halt, told with *siste*. Her step comes in, named *gradum*. An end comes in as well, named *finemque*. She is to place it, told with *impone*. Labor comes in, named *labori*. The one addressed is Calliope, called *Calliope*. As the commentary says, Calliope is the Muse of epic poetry. A poet comes in as well, named *uatisque*. Something of the one addressed comes in, described as *tui*. She is to steer, told with *moderare*. A vessel comes in, named *carinam*.

Oars come in, named *Remis*. The poet comes back, as *quem*. The one addressed sees him, told with *cernis*. He is skimming, described as *stringentem*. Shores come in, named *litora*. Some are few, which close the line as *paucis*. Now comes in as well, marked by *Iamque*. He holds it, told with *tenet*. A harbor comes in, named *portum*. A goal comes in as well, named *metamque*. Someone is mighty, described as *potentis*. He is Homer, who closes the line as *Homeri*.

#### Lines 1067–1070

> 1067 Pieridum comitata cohors, summitte rudentes
>
> 1068 Sanctaque uirgineos lauro redimita capillos
>
> 1069 Ipsa tuas depone lyras. Ades, inclita Pallas,
>
> 1070 Tuque faue cursu uatis iam, Phoebe, peracto.

The Pierides come in, called *Pieridum*. As the commentary says, the Pierides are the Muses. A band is accompanying, described as *comitata*. A band comes in, named *cohors*. They are to lower, told with *summitte*. Ropes come in, named *rudentes*. Someone is holy as well, described as *Sanctaque*. Some are maiden, described as *uirgineos*. Laurel comes in, named *lauro*. She is crowned, described as *redimita*. Hair comes in, which closes the line as *capillos*.

She herself comes in, as *Ipsa*. Some are the one addressed's own, described as *tuas*. She is to lay them down, told with *depone*. Lyres come in, named *lyras*. The one addressed is to be present, told with *Ades*. She is glorious, described as *inclita*. The goddess is Pallas, called *Pallas*. The one addressed comes in as well, as *Tuque*. He is to grant favor, told with *faue*. A course comes in, named *cursu*. A poet comes in, named *uatis*. Now comes in, marked by *iam*. He is Phoebus, called *Phoebe*. The course has been completed, which ends the twenty-fourth book as *peracto*.
