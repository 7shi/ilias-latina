### 1028–1042 (*Iliad* 24.485–486, 24.503–504, 24.502)

#### Line 1028

> 1028 haec ait: "O Graiae gentis fortissime Achilles,

These things come in, given as *haec*. The speaker speaks, told with *ait*. A call comes, marked by *O*. Someone is Greek, described as *Graiae*. A race comes in, named *gentis*. The one addressed is bravest, described as *fortissime*. He is Achilles, called *Achilles*.

#### Lines 1029–1031

> 1029 o regnis inimice meis, te Dardana solum
>
> 1030 uicta tremit pubes, te sensit nostra senectus
>
> 1031 crudelem nimium. Nunc sis mitissimus oro

Another call comes, marked by *o*. Realms come in, named *regnis*. The one addressed is an enemy, described as *inimice*. Some are the speaker's own, described as *meis*. The one addressed comes back, as *te*. Some are Dardanian, described as *Dardana*. As the commentary says, the Dardanians are the Trojans. The one addressed is alone, described as *solum*.

Some are conquered, described as *uicta*. Someone trembles, told with *tremit*. Youth comes in, named *pubes*. The one addressed comes back again, as *te*. Someone has felt him, told with *sensit*. Something is the speaker's own, described as *nostra*. Old age comes in, named *senectus*. The one addressed is cruel, described as *crudelem*. Excess comes in, marked by *nimium*. A time is set, marked by *Nunc*. The one addressed is to be something, told with *sis*. He is to be most gentle, described as *mitissimus*. The speaker prays, told with *oro*.

#### Line 1032

> 1032 et patris afflicti genibus miserere precantis

More is added, marked by *et*. A father comes in, named *patris*. He is cast down, described as *afflicti*. Knees come in, named *genibus*. The one addressed is to pity him, told with *miserere*. The father is praying, described as *precantis*.

#### Lines 1033–1035

> 1033 donaque quae porto miseri pro corpore nati
>
> 1034 accipias; si nec precibus nec flecteris auro,
>
> 1035 in senis extremis tua dextera saeuiat annis:

Gifts come in as well, named *donaque*. The gifts come back, as *quae*. The speaker carries them, told with *porto*. Someone is unhappy, described as *miseri*. A body comes in, given as *pro corpore*. A son comes in, named *nati*.

The one addressed is to accept them, told with *accipias*. A condition is set, marked by *si*. A denial comes, marked by *nec*. Prayers come in, named *precibus*. Another denial comes, marked by *nec*. The one addressed can be moved, told with *flecteris*. Gold comes in, named *auro*. An old man comes in, given as *in senis*. Some are last, described as *extremis*, though what they are is not yet said. Something of the one addressed comes in, described as *tua*. The right hand comes in, named *dextera*. It is to rage, told with *saeuiat*. What they are is years, which close the line as *annis*.

#### Line 1036

> 1036 saltem saeua pater comitabor funera nati!

At least comes in, marked by *saltem*. Something is cruel, described as *saeua*, though what it is is not yet said. A father comes in, named *pater*. The speaker will accompany something, told with *comitabor*. What is cruel is death, named *funera*. A son comes in, which closes the line as *nati*.

#### Lines 1037–1039

> 1037 Nec uitam mihi nec magnos *concedere* honores,
>
> 1038 sed funus crudele meum! Miserere parentis
>
> 1039 et pater esse meo mitis de corpore disce.

A denial comes, marked by *Nec*. Life comes in, named *uitam*. The speaker comes in, as *mihi*. Another denial comes, marked by *nec*. Some are great, described as *magnos*. Granting comes next, told with *concedere*. The base text marks this *concedere* with a sign of doubt, but here it is read as it stands. Honors come in, named *honores*.

Something is set against this, marked by *sed*. Death comes in, named *funus*. It is cruel, described as *crudele*. It is the speaker's own, described as *meum*. The one addressed is to pity, told with *Miserere*. A parent comes in, named *parentis*. More is added, marked by *et*. A father comes in, named *pater*. Being comes next, told with *esse*. Something is the speaker's own, described as *meo*. He is gentle, described as *mitis*. The flesh comes in, given as *de corpore*. The one addressed is to learn, which closes the line as *disce*.

#### Lines 1040–1042

> 1040 Hectoris interitu uicisti Dardana regna,
>
> 1041 uicisti Priamum: sortis reminiscere uictor
>
> 1042 humanae uariosque ducum tu respice casus."

Hector comes in, called *Hectoris*. Death comes in, named *interitu*. The one addressed has conquered, told with *uicisti*. Some are Dardanian, described as *Dardana*. Realms come in, which close the line as *regna*.

The one addressed has conquered again, told with *uicisti*. Priam comes in, called *Priamum*. A lot comes in, named *sortis*. The one addressed is to remember, told with *reminiscere*. He is victor, described as *uictor*. The lot is human, described as *humanae*. Some are changing as well, described as *uariosque*, though what they are is not yet said. Commanders come in, named *ducum*. The one addressed comes in, as *tu*. He is to consider them, told with *respice*. What they are is fortunes, which close the speech as *casus*.
