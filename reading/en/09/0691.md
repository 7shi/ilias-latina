### 691–693 (*Iliad* 9.307–310, 9.378–380, 9.132–134)

#### Lines 691–693

> 691 nec Danaum capit aure preces nec munera regis
>
> 692 ulla referre cupit; non illum redditus ignis
>
> 693 aut intacta suo Briseis corpore mouit.

A denial comes, marked by *nec*. The Danaans come back, as *Danaum*. He takes in something, told with *capit*. It is with his ear, named *aure*. What he would take in is prayers, named *preces*. Another denial follows, marked by *nec*. Gifts come in, named *munera*. The gifts are the king's, who closes the line as *regis*. As the commentary says, the king is Agamemnon.

The gifts are any at all, described as *ulla*. Taking them back comes next, told with *referre*. He wishes to do so, told with *cupit*. A denial comes, marked by *non*. Achilles comes back, as *illum*. Someone is returned, described as *redditus*. That one is his flame, which closes the line as *ignis*.

One alternative comes, marked by *aut*. She is untouched, described as *intacta*. Something is her own, described as *suo*. The untouched one is Briseis, named *Briseis*. What was her own is her body, named *corpore*. Nothing moved him, which closes the line as *mouit*.
