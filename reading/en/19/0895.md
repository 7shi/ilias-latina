### 895–898 (*Iliad* 20.79–85, 20.158–164)

#### Lines 895–898

> 895 dantque animos iuueni. Vidit Cythereius heros
>
> 896 occurritque uiro, sed non cum uiribus aequis
>
> 897 Aeacidae nec compar erat, tamen ira coegit
>
> 898 conferre inuictis iuuenem cum uiribus arma.

They give something as well, told with *dantque*. Courage comes in, named *animos*. The young man comes in, named *iuueni*. Someone saw him, told with *Vidit*. Someone is Cytherean, called *Cythereius*. As the commentary says, Cythereius is Aeneas, son of the goddess Venus. The hero comes in, who closes the line as *heros*.

He went to meet him as well, told with *occurritque*. The man comes in, named *uiro*. A contrast comes, marked by *sed*. A denial comes, marked by *non*. Strength comes in, given as *cum uiribus*. It is equal, which closes the line as *aequis*.

Someone is called *Aeacidae*. As the commentary says, Aeacides is Achilles, grandson of Aeacus. A denial comes again, marked by *nec*. A match comes in, described as *compar*. He was, told with *erat*. Yet something changes, marked by *tamen*. Anger comes in, named *ira*. It drove someone, which closes the line as *coegit*.

To pit something comes, told with *conferre*. Something is invincible, described as *inuictis*, though what it is is not yet said. The young man comes in, named *iuuenem*. What is invincible is strength, given as *cum uiribus*. What he pits is weapons, which close the line as *arma*.
