### 899–902 (*Iliad* 20.291–293, 20.318–325, 20.302–308)

#### Lines 899–902

> 899 Quem nisi seruasset magnarum rector aquarum,
>
> 900 ut profugus laetis Troiam repararet in aruis
>
> 901 Augustumque genus claris submitteret astris,
>
> 902 non clarae gentis nobis mansisset origo.

Aeneas comes back, as *Quem*. A condition comes, marked by *nisi*. Someone had saved him, told with *seruasset*. Something is mighty, described as *magnarum*, though what it is is not yet said. The ruler comes in, named *rector*. As the commentary says, the ruler of the mighty waters is Neptune, god of the sea. What is mighty is waters, which close the line as *aquarum*.

A purpose is set, marked by *ut*. Someone is an exile, described as *profugus*. Something is fertile, described as *laetis*, though what it is is not yet said. Troy comes in, called *Troiam*. He might restore it, told with *repararet*. What is fertile is fields, which close the line as *in aruis*.

The Augustan line comes in as well, given as *Augustumque genus*. Something is bright, described as *claris*. He might bring it forth, told with *submitteret*. Stars come in, which close the line as *astris*.

A denial comes, marked by *non*. An illustrious race comes in, given as *clarae gentis*. We come in, as *nobis*. Someone would have survived, told with *mansisset*. The founder comes in, who closes the line as *origo*.
