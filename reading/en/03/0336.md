### 336–338 (*Iliad* 3.441, 3.447–448)

#### Lines 336–338

> 336 Post haec amplexus per mutua corpora iunctis
>
> 337 incubuit membris Cygneidos; illa soluto
>
> 338 accepit flammas gremio Troiaeque suasque.

Things move on, given as *Post haec*. Someone embraces, described as *amplexus*. Something is mutual, given as *per mutua*, though what is mutual is not yet said. It is their bodies, named *corpora*. Something is joined, described as *iunctis*, though what it is is not yet said. He lay upon it, told with *incubuit*. What was joined is limbs, named *membris*. They are the limbs of the swan-daughter, named *Cygneidos*. As the commentary says, Cygneis, the swan-daughter, is Helen.

She comes in, pointed to as *illa*. Something is loosened, described as *soluto*. She received something, told with *accepit*. What she received is flames, named *flammas*. What was loosened is her lap, named *gremio*. They are Troy's flames, named *Troiaeque*. They are her own as well, which closes the line as *suasque*. As the commentary says, the flames are Paris himself.
