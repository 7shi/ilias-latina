### 336–338 (*Iliad* 3.441, 3.447–448)

#### Lines 336–338

> 336 Post haec amplexus per mutua corpora iunctis
>
> 337 incubuit membris Cygneidos; illa soluto
>
> 338 accepit flammas gremio Troiaeque suasque.

After his words of assurance, action follows, marked by *Post*. Everything just spoken is gathered up as *haec*. Paris folds her in his arms, told with *amplexus*. Their embrace is mutual, met *per mutua*. That close contact joins their bodies, named *corpora*. Their bodies press upon what is joined, described as *iunctis*, though what limbs they are is not yet said. Paris sinks down upon her, resting with *incubuit*. What was described as *iunctis* is revealed as their intertwined limbs, joined as *membris*. Those limbs belong to the swan-daughter, called *Cygneidos*. As the commentary says, the swan-daughter is Helen. Helen responds to his desire, appearing as *illa*. Her garment is loosened, marked as *soluto*.

She welcomes his passion, told with *accepit*. She receives the burning fire, named *flammas*. She takes that fire into her lap, met as *gremio*. Those flames bring ruin to Troy as well, described as *Troiaeque*. That destructive fire belongs to Helen herself, closing the section as *suasque*.
