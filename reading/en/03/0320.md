### 320–331 (*Iliad* 3.428–429, 3.432–433, 3.430–431, 3.434–436)

#### Lines 320–321

> 320 "Venisti mea flamma, Paris, superatus ab armis
>
> 321 coniugis antiqui? Vidi puduitque uidere,

Helen asks if he has come, told with *Venisti*. He is her flame, called *mea flamma*. She names him, as *Paris*. He has been overcome, described as *superatus*. It was by arms, given as *ab armis*. They are a husband's, named *coniugis*. He is her former husband, described as *antiqui*. As the commentary says, her former husband is Menelaus. She saw it, told with *Vidi*. She was ashamed, told with *puduitque*. She was ashamed to see it, which closes the line as *uidere*.

#### Lines 322–323

> 322 arreptum cum te traheret uiolentus Atrides
>
> 323 Iliacoque tuos foedaret puluere crines.

Someone was seized, described as *arreptum*. A time comes, marked by *cum*. It is Paris, given as *te*. Someone dragged him, told with *traheret*. He was violent, described as *uiolentus*. He is the son of Atreus, who closes the line as *Atrides*.

Something is Ilian, described as *Iliacoque*, though what it is is not yet said. Something is Paris's, described as *tuos*, though what it is is not yet said. Atrides fouled it, told with *foedaret*. What was Ilian is dust, named *puluere*. As the commentary says, the Ilian dust is the dust of Troy. What was his is his hair, which closes the line as *crines*.

#### Lines 324–326

> 324 Nostraque - me miseram! - timui ne Doricus ensis
>
> 325 oscula discuteret; totus mihi, mente reuincta,
>
> 326 fugerat ore color sanguisque reliquerat artus.

Something is theirs, described as *Nostraque*, though what it is is not yet said. She cries out for herself, given as *me miseram*. She feared, told with *timui*. What she feared comes next, marked by *ne*. Something is Doric, described as *Doricus*. As the commentary says, Doric is a poetic word for Greek. It is a sword, named *ensis*.

What was theirs is kisses, named *oscula*. The sword might end them, told with *discuteret*. Something is all, described as *totus*, though what it is is not yet said. It is from Helen, given as *mihi*. Her mind is frozen, given as *mente reuincta*.

It had fled, told with *fugerat*. It fled from her face, named *ore*. What was all is color, named *color*. Blood comes next, named *sanguisque*. It had left, told with *reliquerat*. What it left is her limbs, which close the line as *artus*.

#### Line 327

> 327 Quis te cum saeuo contendere suasit Atrida?

Helen asks who, beginning with *Quis*. It is about Paris, given as *te*. Someone fierce comes in, given as *cum saeuo*, though who it is is not yet said. Paris was to contend, told with *contendere*. Someone persuaded him, told with *suasit*. The fierce one is the son of Atreus, which closes the line as *Atrida*.

#### Lines 328–330

> 328 An nondum uaga fama tuas peruenit ad aures
>
> 329 de uirtute uiri? Moneo ne rursus inique
>
> 330 illius tua fata uelis committere dextrae."

Another question comes, beginning with *An*. It is not yet, marked by *nondum*. Something wanders, described as *uaga*. It is rumor, named *fama*. Something is Paris's, described as *tuas*, though what it is is not yet said. Rumor has reached them, told with *peruenit*. What was his is his ears, given as *ad aures*.

It is about valor, given as *de uirtute*. It is the man's, named *uiri*. Helen warns, told with *Moneo*. What she warns against comes next, marked by *ne*. It is again, marked by *rursus*. It is unequal, marked by *inique*.

That man comes back, as *illius*. Something is Paris's own, described as *tua*. It is his fate, named *fata*. He should not be willing, told with *uelis*. He should not entrust it, told with *committere*. It is to the man's right hand, which closes the line as *dextrae*.

#### Line 331

> 331 Dixit. Tum largis perfudit fletibus ora.

She has spoken, told with *Dixit*. Then comes more, marked by *Tum*. Something is copious, described as *largis*, though what it is is not yet said. She drenched something, told with *perfudit*. What was copious is tears, named *fletibus*. What she drenched is her face, which closes the line as *ora*.
