### 283–289 (*Iliad* 3.339–340, 3.350–352, 3.344)

#### Lines 283–286

> 283 Quem contra paribus fulgens Menelaus in armis
>
> 284 constitit et: "Tecum mihi sint certamina - dixit -
>
> 285 "nec longum nostra laetabere coniuge, quae te
>
> 286 mox raptum ire gemet, tantummodo Iuppiter adsit."

Alexander comes back, as *Quem*. His opponent stands opposite him, marked by *contra*. His equipment matches his rival's, described as *paribus*, though in what gear is not yet said. The challenger is gleaming in the field, praised as *fulgens*. He comes forward as *Menelaus*. What was described as *paribus* is revealed as his armor, met *in armis*.

He plants his feet firmly in place, told with *constitit*. His challenge follows, joined by *et*. Menelaus calls Paris to single combat, demanding: *Tecum*. He claims the fight for himself, indicated by *mihi*. He insists that the duel take place, urged with *sint*. What he calls for is the contest, named *certamina*. His speech is marked by *dixit*.

Menelaus adds a warning, opened with *nec*. That stolen pleasure will not endure, described as *longum*. What Paris enjoys belongs to Helen's husband, designated as *nostra*, though what it is is not yet said. Paris will not long rejoice in it, told with *laetabere*. What was designated as *nostra* is revealed as his wife, named *coniuge*.

The wife comes back, as *quae*. Her tears will be for Paris, pointing to *te*. That loss will happen soon, marked by *mox*. Paris will be snatched away, described as *raptum*. He is doomed to depart in death, told with *ire*. Helen will lament his fall, predicted with *gemet*. Menelaus adds a single condition, marked by *tantummodo*. The chief god is named as his protector, appearing as *Iuppiter*. He prays that the god stand by him, concluding his challenge with *adsit*.

#### Line 287

> 287 Dixit et aduersum se concitat acer in hostem.

Menelaus finishes his challenge, concluding with *Dixit*. Action follows at once, joined by *et*. He turns against what stands opposite him, described as *aduersum*, though who his target is is not yet said. He hurls his own body forward, told as *se*. He rouses himself into a charge, spurred by *concitat*. His spirit is fierce, described as *acer*. What was described as *aduersum* is revealed as his foe, attacking *in hostem*.

#### Lines 288–289

> 288 Ille uirum forti uenientem reppulit ictu
>
> 289 seque gradu celeri recipit longeque frementem

Alexander responds to the charge, appearing as *Ille*. The advancing warrior is named *uirum*. He meets him with what is powerful, described as *forti*, though with what defense is not yet said. He confronts the man as he comes on, pictured as *uenientem*. Alexander drives him back, told with *reppulit*. What was described as *forti* is revealed as a heavy blow, named *ictu*.

Alexander also moves himself away, told as *seque*. He steps back with his own stride, called *gradu*. That pace is swift, described as *celeri*. Alexander draws back from his opponent, checked with *recipit*. He also aims from a distance, joined as *longeque*. A cast spear whistles through the air, described as *frementem*, though what weapon it is comes only in the next section.
