### 283–289 (*Iliad* 3.339–340, 3.350–352, 3.344)

#### Lines 283–286

> 283 Quem contra paribus fulgens Menelaus in armis
>
> 284 constitit et: "Tecum mihi sint certamina - dixit -
>
> 285 "nec longum nostra laetabere coniuge, quae te
>
> 286 mox raptum ire gemet, tantummodo Iuppiter adsit."

Paris comes back, as *Quem*. Someone stands against him, marked by *contra*. Something matches, described as *paribus*, though what it is is not yet said. Someone gleams, described as *fulgens*. He is Menelaus, named *Menelaus*. What matched is armor, given as *in armis*.

He took his stand, told with *constitit*. More follows, joined by *et*. Menelaus turns to Paris, as *Tecum*. It is for himself, given as *mihi*. There is to be something, told with *sint*. It is a contest, named *certamina*. He says so, told with *dixit*.

A denial comes, marked by *nec*. It is about a long time, described as *longum*. Something is Menelaus' own, described as *nostra*, though what it is is not yet said. Paris will rejoice, told with *laetabere*. What was Menelaus' own is his wife, named *coniuge*.

The wife comes back, as *quae*. It is about Paris, given as *te*. It is soon, marked by *mox*. Paris will be snatched away, given as *raptum ire*. She will lament it, told with *gemet*. A proviso comes, marked by *tantummodo*. Jupiter comes in, named *Iuppiter*. He is to stand by, which closes the speech as *adsit*.

#### Line 287

> 287 Dixit et aduersum se concitat acer in hostem.

Menelaus has spoken, told with *Dixit*. More follows, joined by *et*. Someone is facing him, described as *aduersum*, though who it is is not yet said. Menelaus hurls himself, told with *se concitat*. He is fierce, described as *acer*. The one facing him is the enemy, given as *in hostem*.

#### Lines 288–289

> 288 Ille uirum forti uenientem reppulit ictu
>
> 289 seque gradu celeri recipit longeque frementem

The other one comes in, pointed to as *Ille*. A man comes next, named *uirum*. Something is powerful, described as *forti*, though what it is is not yet said. The man is advancing, described as *uenientem*. He drove him back, told with *reppulit*. What was powerful is a blow, named *ictu*.

He does something to himself, given as *seque*. It is with a step, named *gradu*. The step is swift, described as *celeri*. He draws back, told with *recipit*. Then from far off, given as *longeque*. Something whistles, described as *frementem*; what it is comes only in the next section.
