### 314–316 (*Iliad* 3.379–382)

#### Lines 314–316

> 314 et magnam ualidis contorsit uiribus hastam
>
> 315 in cladem Phrygii, sua quem Venus eripit hosti
>
> 316 et secum in thalamos defert testudine cultos.

Action continues with another throw, joined by *et*. He wields a massive weapon, pictured as *magnam*, though what weapon it is is not yet said. He puts into the cast all his power, described as *ualidis*, though with what strength is not yet said. He hurls it with a twist of his arm, told with *contorsit*. What was described as *ualidis* is revealed as his strength, met as *uiribus*. What was pictured as *magnam* is revealed as his great spear, named *hastam*.

He aims the cast to cause ruin, met *in cladem*. The one marked for destruction is the Trojan prince, named *Phrygii*. As the commentary says, this Phrygian is Paris. He is watched over by his own goddess, described as *sua*. The fugitive comes back, as *quem*. The goddess who protects him is Venus, appearing as *Venus*. She snatches him away from danger, told with *eripit*. She rescues him from his fierce opponent, designated as *hosti*.

She also carries him far from the field, joined by *et*. She takes him along with herself, keeping him as *secum*. She brings him to the bedchamber, entered *in thalamos*. She bears him into safety, carried with *defert*. The interior is inlaid with tortoiseshell, fashioned from *testudine*. The room is richly decorated, closing the section with *cultos*.
