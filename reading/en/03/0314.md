### 314–316 (*Iliad* 3.379–382)

#### Lines 314–316

> 314 et magnam ualidis contorsit uiribus hastam
>
> 315 in cladem Phrygii, sua quem Venus eripit hosti
>
> 316 et secum in thalamos defert testudine cultos.

More follows, joined by *et*. Something is great, described as *magnam*, though what it is is not yet said. Something is strong, described as *ualidis*. He hurled it, told with *contorsit*. What was strong is his force, named *uiribus*. What was great is a spear, named *hastam*.

Destruction is the aim, given as *in cladem*. Someone is Phrygian, named *Phrygii*. Something is his own, described as *sua*, though what it is is not yet said. The Phrygian comes back, as *quem*. What was his own is Venus, named *Venus*. As the commentary says, Venus is his own because he is devoted to her and enjoys her favor. She snatches him away, told with *eripit*. It is from his foe, named *hosti*.

More follows, joined by *et*. She takes him with her, given as *secum*. It is to chambers, given as *in thalamos*. She carries him down, told with *defert*. Tortoiseshell comes in, named *testudine*. The chambers are adorned with it, which closes the line as *cultos*.
