### 302–306 (*Iliad* 3.361–368)

#### Lines 302–305

> 302 Dardaniumque premit iuuenem. Mox ense rigente
>
> 303 cedentem retro dum desuper appetit hostem,
>
> 304 splendidus extremas galeae percussus ad oras
>
> 305 dissiluit mucro; gemuerunt agmina Graium.

Menelaus also turns upon the Trojan warrior, pictured as *Dardaniumque*. He bears down upon him, pressing with *premit*. His opponent is a young warrior, named *iuuenem*. As the commentary says, this Dardanian youth is Paris. Soon the duel takes a new turn, marked by *Mox*. Menelaus raises his weapon, met as *ense*. That steel is unyielding, praised as *rigente*.

His rival is retreating, shown as *cedentem*. Alexander gives ground backward, driven *retro*. While Menelaus presses his advantage, introduced with *dum*. His blow descends from above, aimed *desuper*. He lunges at his foe, told with *appetit*. The one he assails is his enemy, confronting his *hostem*.

The sword blade shines bright, described as *splendidus*, though what weapon it is is not yet said. The stroke falls upon the outer edge, designated as *extremas*, though of what armor is not yet said. The strike hits the Trojan's helmet, named *galeae*. The metal is struck with full force, driven with *percussus*. What was designated as *extremas* is revealed as the rim, met *ad oras*.

The weapon bursts apart into fragments, shattering with *dissiluit*. What was described as *splendidus* is revealed as the sword point, appearing as *mucro*. The watching host laments the blow, groaning with *gemuerunt*. The cry rises from their ranks, calling out as *agmina*. Those who grieve are the Greeks, closing the line as *Graium*.

#### Line 306

> 306 Tum uero ardescit, quamuis manus ense carebat,

Then the Greek hero's fury mounts, marked by *Tum*. His wrath truly blazes forth, affirmed with *uero*. Menelaus burns with anger, told with *ardescit*. He presses forward even though he has lost his weapon, introduced with *quamuis*. Nothing remains in his hand, named *manus*. He is left without his sword, met as *ense*. His right arm was stripped of its blade, closing the section with *carebat*.
