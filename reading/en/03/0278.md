### 278–280 (*Iliad* 3.116–117, 3.259–261, 3.271–301, 3.114–115)

#### Lines 278–280

> 278 Protinus accitur Priamus sacrisque peractis
>
> 279 foedera iunguntur. Post haec discedit uterque
>
> 280 depositis populus telis campusque patescit.

Without delay, action follows at once, marked by *Protinus*. A summons is sent, told with *accitur*. The king of Troy is called to the field, named *Priamus*. Sacred rites are also undertaken, joined as *sacrisque*. The sacrifices are fully performed, concluded with *peractis*. The solemn terms of the treaty appear as *foedera*. The agreement is sealed between them, confirmed with *iunguntur*.

The story turns to what happens next, opening with *Post*. All that has taken place is gathered up as *haec*. The troops begin to withdraw, told with *discedit*. Each of the two sides draws back, spoken of as *uterque*, though who withdraws is not yet said. Weapons have been laid down, described as *depositis*, though what was laid aside is not yet said. What was spoken of as *uterque* is revealed as the armies of both hosts, named *populus*. What was described as *depositis* is revealed as their spears, spoken of as *telis*. The open plain is also revealed, joined as *campusque*. With the armies withdrawn, the battleground lies open, closing the line as *patescit*.
