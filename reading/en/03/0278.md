### 278–280 (*Iliad* 3.116–117, 3.259–261, 3.271–301, 3.114–115)

#### Lines 278–280

> 278 Protinus accitur Priamus sacrisque peractis
>
> 279 foedera iunguntur. Post haec discedit uterque
>
> 280 depositis populus telis campusque patescit.

It happens at once, marked by *Protinus*. Someone is summoned, told with *accitur*. It is Priam, named *Priamus*. Sacrifices come next, named *sacrisque*. They are completed, described as *peractis*. A treaty comes in, named *foedera*. It is made, told with *iunguntur*.

Things move on, given as *Post haec*. Someone draws back, told with *discedit*. It is each side, named *uterque*, though each of what is not yet said. Something is laid down, described as *depositis*, though what it is is not yet said either. What draws back is the army of each, named *populus*. What was laid down is weapons, named *telis*. The field comes next, named *campusque*. It lies open, which closes the line as *patescit*.
