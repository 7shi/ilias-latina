### 307–309 (*Iliad* 3.369–370, 3.374, 3.380–381)

#### Lines 307–309

> 307 et iuuenem arrepta prosternit casside uictor
>
> 308 ad socios traheretque, et, ni caligine caeca
>
> 309 texisset Cytherea uirum subiectaque mento

Action continues without his sword, joined by *et*. Menelaus seizes upon the young warrior, named *iuuenem*. He grasps a piece of armor, described as *arrepta*, though what it is is not yet said. He hurls his enemy to the earth, told with *prosternit*. What was described as *arrepta* is revealed as Paris's helmet, seized as *casside*. He stands over him in triumph, praised as *uictor*. He begins dragging his captive toward his comrades, met *ad socios*. He would also have pulled him along, striving with *traheretque*.

Divine intervention follows, connected by *et*. A condition cuts off his triumph, introduced with *ni*. A dark mist descends upon them, met as *caligine*. That vapor is blinding, described as *caeca*. A divine protector hid the fallen warrior, told with *texisset*. The goddess who rescues him is Cytherea, named *Cytherea*. As the commentary says, Cytherea is Venus. The one she shields from harm is the Trojan warrior, appearing as *uirum*. What is fastened beneath also comes into play, pictured as *subiectaque*, though what it is comes only in the next section. It sits beneath the youth's chin, closing the section with *mento*.
