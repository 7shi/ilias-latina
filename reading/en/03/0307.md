### 307–309 (*Iliad* 3.369–370, 3.374, 3.380–381)

#### Lines 307–309

> 307 et iuuenem arrepta prosternit casside uictor
>
> 308 ad socios traheretque, et, ni caligine caeca
>
> 309 texisset Cytherea uirum subiectaque mento

More follows, joined by *et*. A youth comes in, named *iuuenem*. Something is seized, described as *arrepta*, though what it is is not yet said. Menelaus throws him down, told with *prosternit*. What was seized is the helmet, named *casside*. Menelaus is the victor, named *uictor*. His comrades come in, given as *ad socios*. He would have dragged him, told with *traheretque*.

More follows, joined by *et*. A condition comes, marked by *ni*. Mist comes in, named *caligine*. It is blinding, described as *caeca*. Someone covered something, told with *texisset*. The one who covered is Cytherea, named *Cytherea*. As the commentary says, Cytherea is Venus. What she covered is the man, named *uirum*. Something is fastened beneath, described as *subiectaque*, though what it is comes only in the next section. It is beneath the chin, named *mento*.
