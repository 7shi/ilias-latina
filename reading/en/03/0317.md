### 317–319 (*Iliad* 3.383–384, 3.424–427)

#### Lines 317–319

> 317 Ipsa dehinc Helenam muris accersit ab altis
>
> 318 Dardanioque suos Paridi deducit amores.
>
> 319 Quem tali postquam conspexit uoce locutast:

The goddess herself carries out what follows, appearing as *Ipsa*. Next she turns from the chamber to the city, marked by *dehinc*. She seeks out Helen, named *Helenam*. She finds her above upon the battlements, resting on *muris*. Venus calls her away, told with *accersit*. She summons her down from the heights, met *ab altis*.

She also guides her to the Trojan prince, pictured as *Dardanioque*. She brings to him his own, described as *suos*, though what it is is not yet said. The prince who waits for her is Paris, designated as *Paridi*. The goddess conducts her into the chamber, guided with *deducit*. What was described as *suos* is revealed as his beloved, named *amores*.

Helen looks upon the man before her, met as *Quem*. She addresses him in such a manner, pictured as *tali*, though with what voice is not yet said. Her words come after her gaze falls on him, introduced with *postquam*. She beholds her defeated husband, seen with *conspexit*. What was pictured as *tali* is revealed as her sharp tone, sounding as *uoce*. She spoke her mind without affection, closing the section with *locutast*.
