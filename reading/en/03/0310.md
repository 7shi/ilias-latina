### 310–312 (*Iliad* 3.371–376)

#### Lines 310–312

> 310 fortia rupisset laxatis uincula nodis,
>
> 311 ultimus ille dies Paridi foret. Abstrahit auro
>
> 312 fulgentem galeam secum Menelaus et ardens

What was fastened beneath is strong, described as *fortia*. Venus would have broken something, told with *rupisset*. Something is loosened, described as *laxatis*. What was strong is the bonds, named *uincula*. What was loosened is knots, named *nodis*.

That would have been last, described as *ultimus*. It is that one, pointed to as *ille*. It is a day, named *dies*. It would have been for Paris, named *Paridi*. So it would have turned out, told with *foret*. Someone drags something away, told with *Abstrahit*. Gold comes in, named *auro*. Something gleams with it, described as *fulgentem*. What gleams is the helmet, named *galeam*. He takes it with him, given as *secum*. He is Menelaus, named *Menelaus*. More follows, joined by *et*. He is burning, which closes the line as *ardens*.
