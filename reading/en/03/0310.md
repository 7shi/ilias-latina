### 310–312 (*Iliad* 3.371–376)

#### Lines 310–312

> 310 fortia rupisset laxatis uincula nodis,
>
> 311 ultimus ille dies Paridi foret. Abstrahit auro
>
> 312 fulgentem galeam secum Menelaus et ardens

What was pictured as *subiectaque* had strong resistance, described as *fortia*, though what they are is not yet said. Venus would have torn them apart, told with *rupisset*. The fastenings are loosened, pictured as *laxatis*, though what is loosened is not yet said. What was described as *fortia* is revealed as the bonds under his chin, named *uincula*. What was pictured as *laxatis* is revealed as the knots of his gear, met as *nodis*.

That would have marked his final hour, marked as *ultimus*. That day is pointed to with *ille*. It would have been the day of his death, arriving as *dies*. That destruction awaited Paris, designated as *Paridi*. That fate would have been his lot, confirmed with *foret*. Menelaus hauls the prize away, told with *Abstrahit*. The armor is adorned with gold, met as *auro*. It gleams in the light, described as *fulgentem*. The spoil he carries off is the helmet, named *galeam*. He takes it along with himself, keeping it as *secum*. The Greek champion stands holding it, appearing as *Menelaus*. His rage does not subside, joined by *et*. He blazes with fury, closing the section as *ardens*.
