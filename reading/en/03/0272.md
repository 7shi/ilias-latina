### 272–276 (*Iliad* 3.59, 3.64–75)

#### Line 272

> 272 "Quid nimis indignis" - inquit - "me uocibus urges,

Paris asks why his brother speaks so harshly, opening with *Quid*. He protests that the reproach is excessive, marked by *nimis*. He calls the rebuke undeserved, described as *indignis*, though in what way is not yet said. His response is introduced with *inquit*. The one who is assailed is Paris himself, pointing to *me*. What was described as *indignis* is revealed as Hector's words, named *uocibus*. Paris asks why he assails him with them, closing the line as *urges*.

#### Lines 273–274

> 273 o patriae, germane, decus? Nam nec mihi coniunx
>
> 274 prauaque luxuria est potior uirtutis honore

Paris calls upon his brother, beginning with *o*. He names their native country as *patriae*, though what he is to it is not yet said. He speaks to him as his brother, addressing him as *germane*. He honors him as the glory of their homeland, praised as *decus*.

Paris explains his thinking, opening with *Nam*. He sets down a denial, marked by *nec*. He speaks for himself, indicating *mihi*. The first thing he names is his wife, appearing as *coniunx*. He pairs her with corrupt pleasure, spoken of as *prauaque luxuria*. He states how they stand, affirmed with *est*. Neither is dearer to him, told with *potior*. He sets them against valor, named *uirtutis*. What matters more is the honor of that valor, closing the line as *honore*.

#### Lines 275–276

> 275 nec uires temptare uiri dextramque recuso,
>
> 276 dummodo uictorem coniunx cum pace sequatur."

Paris adds another denial, marked by *nec*. What he must face is his enemy's strength, named *uires*. He is ready to test it in combat, told with *temptare*. The strength belongs to his challenger, spoken of as *uiri*. He will test that warrior's right hand as well, joined as *dextramque*. Paris does not refuse the duel, declared with *recuso*.

He sets a single condition, introduced with *dummodo*. The prize should belong to the conqueror, named *uictorem*. The prize to be followed is his wife, returning as *coniunx*. As the commentary says, this wife is Helen. The handover should be peaceful, accompanied *cum pace*. Paris asks that she follow the winner, closing his reply as *sequatur*.
