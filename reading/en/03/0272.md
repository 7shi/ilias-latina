### 272–276 (*Iliad* 3.59, 3.64–75)

#### Line 272

> 272 "Quid nimis indignis" - inquit - "me uocibus urges,

Paris asks why, beginning with *Quid*. It is too much, marked by *nimis*. Something is undeserved, described as *indignis*, though what it is is not yet said. Paris says so, told with *inquit*. It concerns himself, given as *me*. What was undeserved is words, named *uocibus*. Hector presses him with them, which closes the line as *urges*.

#### Lines 273–274

> 273 o patriae, germane, decus? Nam nec mihi coniunx
>
> 274 prauaque luxuria est potior uirtutis honore

Paris cries out, beginning with *o*. Their homeland comes in, named *patriae*, though what of it is meant is not yet said. Hector is his brother, addressed as *germane*. What was of the homeland is its glory, named *decus*.

A reason follows, marked by *Nam*. It begins with a denial, marked by *nec*. It is for Paris himself, given as *mihi*. His wife comes in, named *coniunx*. Corrupt pleasure is with her, named *prauaque luxuria*. Neither is dearer, given as *est potior*. Valor comes in, named *uirtutis*. It is the honor of valor, which closes the line as *honore*.

#### Lines 275–276

> 275 nec uires temptare uiri dextramque recuso,
>
> 276 dummodo uictorem coniunx cum pace sequatur."

Another denial follows, marked by *nec*. Strength comes next, named *uires*. Paris would test it, told with *temptare*. It is the man's strength, named *uiri*. His right hand too, as *dextramque*. Paris does not refuse, which closes the line as *recuso*.

A proviso comes, marked by *dummodo*. There is a victor, named *uictorem*. The wife returns, named *coniunx*. It is to be in peace, given as *cum pace*. She is to follow the victor, which closes the reply as *sequatur*.
