### 332–335 (*Iliad* 3.437–440)

#### Lines 332–333

> 332 Tristis Alexander: "Non me superauit Atrides,
>
> 333 o meus ardor" - ait - "sed castae Palladis ira.

Paris is sorrowful after her reproach, described as *Tristis*. The Trojan prince replies as *Alexander*. He opens with a denial, *Non*. The defeat did not fall upon himself, pointing to *me*. He denies that his enemy conquered him, told with *superauit*. The one he refuses to credit is the son of Atreus, appearing as *Atrides*.

He calls upon her with longing, beginning with *o*. She is his own burning love, addressed as *meus ardor*. He speaks to his wife, marked by *ait*. He offers another cause for his fall, introduced with *sed*. The divine power who intervened is chaste, described as *castae*. The anger belongs to Pallas, named *Palladis*. As the commentary says, Pallas is Minerva. What overcame him was her wrath, closing the thought as *ira*.

#### Lines 334–335

> 334 Mox illum nostris succumbere turpiter armis
>
> 335 aspicies aderitque meo Cytherea labori."

Paris promises a swift reversal, marked by *Mox*. The one who will fall is Menelaus, pointing to *illum*. He will yield to what is his own, described as *nostris*, though to what gear is not yet said. His enemy will fall in defeat, told with *succumbere*. Menelaus will give way with dishonor, marked as *turpiter*. What was described as *nostris* is revealed as his weapons, met as *armis*.

Helen will witness his revenge, promised with *aspicies*. His divine protector will also stand by him, assured as *aderitque*. She will assist what belongs to him, designated as *meo*. The goddess who aids him is Cytherea, named *Cytherea*. She will support him in his struggle, closing the speech with *labori*.
