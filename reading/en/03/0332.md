### 332–335 (*Iliad* 3.437–440)

#### Lines 332–333

> 332 Tristis Alexander: "Non me superauit Atrides,
>
> 333 o meus ardor" - ait - "sed castae Palladis ira.

Someone is downcast, described as *Tristis*. He is Alexander, named *Alexander*. A denial comes, marked by *Non*. It is about himself, given as *me*. Someone overcame him, told with *superauit*. The denial is that it was the son of Atreus, which closes the line as *Atrides*.

He calls to her, beginning with *o*. She is his flame, called *meus ardor*. He says so, told with *ait*. Something else is set against it, marked by *sed*. The one who is chaste is Pallas, given as *castae Palladis*. What overcame him is her anger, which closes the line as *ira*.

#### Lines 334–335

> 334 Mox illum nostris succumbere turpiter armis
>
> 335 aspicies aderitque meo Cytherea labori."

Something will happen soon, marked by *Mox*. That man comes back, as *illum*. Something is Paris's own, described as *nostris*, though what it is is not yet said. The man will yield, told with *succumbere*. It will be shamefully, marked by *turpiter*. What was Paris's own is arms, named *armis*.

Helen will see it, told with *aspicies*. Someone will be there, told with *aderitque*. Something is Paris's, described as *meo*, though what it is is not yet said. The one who will be there is Cytherea, named *Cytherea*. What was Paris's is his struggle, which closes the line as *labori*.
