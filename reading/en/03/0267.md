### 267–271 (*Iliad* 3.52, 3.69, 3.68, 3.73, 3.70, 3.58)

#### Lines 267–270

> 267 Aequius aduersis tecum concurrat in armis
>
> 268 impiger Atrides, spectet Danaumque Phrygumque
>
> 269 depositis populus telis. Vos, foedere iuncto,
>
> 270 aduersas conferte manus, decernite ferro."

Something would be fairer, given as *Aequius*. Something is opposed, described as *aduersis*, though what it is is not yet said. It would be against Paris, given as *tecum*. Someone would fight, told with *concurrat*. What was described as *aduersis* is arms, given as *in armis*.

The fighter would be tireless, described as *impiger*. He is the son of Atreus, named *Atrides*. As the commentary says, Atrides here names Menelaus, Helen's husband. Someone should watch, told with *spectet*. The Danaans come in, named *Danaumque*. The Phrygians too, as *Phrygumque*. As the commentary says, the Danaans are the Greeks, and the Phrygians are the Trojans.

Something is laid down, described as *depositis*, though what it is is not yet said. The watchers are the army, named *populus*. What was laid down is weapons, named *telis*. Hector turns to the two, as *Vos*. A pact comes in, named *foedere*. It is agreed, described as *iuncto*. Something is opposed, described as *aduersas*. They are to bring it together, told with *conferte*. What was opposed is hands, named *manus*. They are to settle it, told with *decernite*. It is by the sword, which closes the speech as *ferro*.

#### Line 271

> 271 Dixit. Quem contra paucis Priameius heros:

Hector has spoken, told with *Dixit*. Hector comes back, as *Quem*. Someone answers against him, marked by *contra*. It is in few words, given as *paucis*. The one who answers is Priam's son, described as *Priameius*. He is a hero, which closes the line as *heros*.
