### 267–271 (*Iliad* 3.52, 3.69, 3.68, 3.73, 3.70, 3.58)

#### Lines 267–270

> 267 Aequius aduersis tecum concurrat in armis
>
> 268 impiger Atrides, spectet Danaumque Phrygumque
>
> 269 depositis populus telis. Vos, foedere iuncto,
>
> 270 aduersas conferte manus, decernite ferro."

Hector proposes a fairer course, opening with *Aequius*. The weapons should be turned against each other, described as *aduersis*, though in what conflict is not yet said. The clash should be fought against Paris himself, addressed as *tecum*. Hector urges him to fight directly, told with *concurrat*. What was described as *aduersis* is revealed as their opposing arms, met *in armis*.

The challenger is tireless, described as *impiger*. He is the son of Atreus, appearing as *Atrides*. As the commentary says, this son of Atreus is Menelaus. The two hosts should watch the duel, urged with *spectet*. The Greeks are to look on, joined as *Danaumque*. As the commentary says, the Danaans are the Greeks. The Trojans also join them as spectators, added as *Phrygumque*. As the commentary says, the Phrygians are the Trojans.

Their weapons are laid aside, described as *depositis*, though what they lay down is not yet said. Those who watch are the troops of both armies, named *populus*. What was described as *depositis* is revealed as their spears, spoken of as *telis*. Hector addresses the two rivals directly, turning to them as *Vos*. They are to make a covenant, named *foedere*. That agreement is to be struck, told with *iuncto*. They are to set their hands against one another, described as *aduersas*. They are commanded to join in combat, ordered with *conferte*. The hands they bring together are recognized as *manus*. Hector commands them to settle the quarrel, urging: *decernite*. They are to decide it with the sword, closing the speech with *ferro*.

#### Line 271

> 271 Dixit. Quem contra paucis Priameius heros:

Hector finishes his speech, concluding with *Dixit*. Hector comes back, as *Quem*. A reply is directed against him, marked by *contra*. The answer is brief, spoken in *paucis*. The one who responds is the son of Priam, appearing as *Priameius*. He stands as a prince, closing the line as *heros*. As the commentary says, this son of Priam is Paris.
