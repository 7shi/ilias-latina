### 290–301 (*Iliad* 3.346–349, 3.355–359)

#### Line 290

> 290 hastam deinde iacit, quam deuitauit Atrides

What whistled is a spear, named *hastam*. It comes next, marked by *deinde*. He hurls it, told with *iacit*. The spear comes back, as *quam*. Someone avoided it, told with *deuitauit*. He is the son of Atreus, who closes the line as *Atrides*.

#### Lines 291–293

> 291 inque uicem misso fixisset corpora telo
>
> 292 praedonis Phrygii, ni uastum ferrea pectus
>
> 293 texisset lorica uiri septemplice tergo.

It goes the other way, given as *inque uicem*. Something is thrown, described as *misso*. He would have pierced, told with *fixisset*. What he would have pierced is the body, named *corpora*. What was thrown is a spear, named *telo*.

The body is a robber's, named *praedonis*. He is Phrygian, described as *Phrygii*. As the commentary says, the Phrygian means Paris. But a condition comes, marked by *ni*. Something is broad, described as *uastum*. Something is of iron, described as *ferrea*, though what it is is not yet said. What was broad is a chest, named *pectus*. Something covered it, told with *texisset*. What was of iron is a corslet, named *lorica*. It is the man's, named *uiri*. It is sevenfold, described as *septemplice*. It is of hide, which closes the line as *tergo*.

#### Lines 294–296

> 294 Insequitur iuxta clamor; tum aduersus uterque
>
> 295 constitit et galeam galea terit et pede plantam
>
> 296 coniungit stridetque mucro mucrone corusco;

A shout follows, told with *Insequitur*. It is close by, marked by *iuxta*. The shout comes in, named *clamor*. Then it changes, marked by *tum*. Each faces the other, described as *aduersus*. It is each of them, named *uterque*.

Each takes his stand, told with *constitit*. More follows, joined by *et*. A helmet comes in, named *galeam*. It is against another helmet, named *galea*. The one rubs the other, told with *terit*. More follows, joined by *et*. A foot comes in, named *pede*. A sole comes next, named *plantam*.

He joins them, told with *coniungit*. Something shrieks, told with *stridetque*. It is a blade, named *mucro*. It is against a blade, named *mucrone*. That blade flashes, which closes the line as *corusco*.

#### Line 297

> 297 corpus collectum tegitur fulgentibus armis.

A body comes in, named *corpus*. It is drawn together, described as *collectum*. It is covered, told with *tegitur*. Something gleams, described as *fulgentibus*. It is arms, which close the line as *armis*.

#### Lines 298–299

> 298 Non aliter fortes nitida de coniuge tauri
>
> 299 bella gerunt uastisque replent mugitibus auras.

It is no different, given as *Non aliter*. Something is mighty, described as *fortes*, though what it is is not yet said. Something is sleek, described as *nitida*. It is a mate, given as *de coniuge*. The mighty ones are bulls, named *tauri*.

War comes next, named *bella*. They wage it, told with *gerunt*. Something is huge, described as *uastisque*, though what it is is not yet said. They fill something, told with *replent*. What was huge is bellows, named *mugitibus*. What they fill is the air, which closes the line as *auras*.

#### Lines 300–301

> 300 Atque diu rigido captabant corpora ferro,
>
> 301 cum memor Atrides raptae sibi coniugis instat

More follows, joined by *Atque*. It goes on long, marked by *diu*. Something is unyielding, described as *rigido*, though what it is is not yet said. They sought something, told with *captabant*. It is bodies, named *corpora*. What was unyielding is steel, named *ferro*.

Then a moment comes, marked by *cum*. Someone remembers, described as *memor*. He is the son of Atreus, named *Atrides*. Someone was stolen, described as *raptae*, though who it is is not yet said. She was stolen from him, given as *sibi*. The stolen one is his wife, named *coniugis*. He presses on, which closes the line as *instat*.
