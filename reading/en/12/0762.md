### 762–766 (*Iliad* 12.467–470, 12.154–155, 12.262–264)

#### Lines 762–765

> 762 Irrumpunt aditus Phryges atque in limine primo
>
> 763 restantes sternunt Graios ualloque cateruas
>
> 764 deturbant, alii scalas in moenia poscunt
>
> 765 et iaciunt ignes: auget uictoria uires.

Someone bursts through, told with *Irrumpunt*. Entrances come in, spoken of as *aditus*. Those who burst through are the Phrygians, named *Phryges*. As the commentary says, the Phrygians are the Trojans. More is added, marked by *atque*. A threshold comes in, given as *in limine*. It is the very first, which closes the line as *primo*.

Some are resisting, described as *restantes*, though who they are is not yet said. The Phrygians lay them low, told with *sternunt*. Those who resist are the Greeks, named *Graios*. A rampart comes in as well, given as *ualloque*. Troops come in, which close the line as *cateruas*.

They dislodge them, told with *deturbant*. Others come in, as *alii*. Ladders come in, named *scalas*. The walls come in, given as *in moenia*. They call for them, which closes the line as *poscunt*.

More is added, marked by *et*. They hurl something, told with *iaciunt*. What they hurl is firebrands, named *ignes*. Something increases, told with *auget*. What increases it is victory, named *uictoria*. What victory increases is strength, which closes the line as *uires*.

#### Line 766

> 766 De muris pugnant Danai turresque per altas.

The walls come in, given as *De muris*. Someone fights, told with *pugnant*. Those who fight are the Danaans, named *Danai*. As the commentary says, the Danaans are the Greeks. Towers come in as well, spoken of as *turresque*. They are lofty, which closes the line as *per altas*.
