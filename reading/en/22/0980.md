### 980–987 (*Iliad* 22.338–343, 24.486–487)

#### Lines 980–981

> 980 "En concede meos miseris genitoribus artus,
>
> 981 quos pater infelix multo mercabitur auro:

An appeal begins, marked by *En*. Yielding comes next, told with *concede*. Something is his own, described as *meos*, though what it is is not yet said. Wretched parents come in, given as *miseris genitoribus*. What is his own is limbs, which close the line as *artus*.

The limbs come back, as *quos*. An unhappy father comes in, given as *pater infelix*. Something is much, described as *multo*, though what it is is not yet said. He will ransom them, told with *mercabitur*. What is much is gold, which closes the line as *auro*.

#### Lines 982–984

> 982 dona feres uictor. Priami nunc filius orat,
>
> 983 te Priamus, dux ille ducum, quem Graecia solum
>
> 984 pertimuit: si, nec precibus nec munere uictus,

Gifts come in, named *dona*. The one addressed will carry them off, told with *feres*. Someone is victor, described as *uictor*. Someone is Priam's, described as *Priami*, though who it is is not yet said. A time is set, marked by *nunc*. Who is Priam's is a son, named *filius*. He begs, which closes the line as *orat*.

Achilles is addressed as *te*. Priam comes in, called *Priamus*. A leader comes in, named *dux*. That one comes in, as *ille*. Leaders come in, named *ducum*. The leader comes back, as *quem*. Greece comes in, called *Graecia*. As the commentary says, Greece here stands for the Greek forces. He is alone, which closes the line as *solum*.

It feared him greatly, told with *pertimuit*. A condition comes, marked by *si*. A denial comes, marked by *nec*. Prayers come in, named *precibus*. Another denial comes, marked by *nec*. A gift comes in, named *munere*. Someone is won over, which closes the line as *uictus*.

#### Line 985

> 985 nec lacrimis miseri nec clara gente moueris,

A denial comes, marked by *nec*. Tears come in, named *lacrimis*. Someone is wretched, described as *miseri*. Another denial comes, marked by *nec*. Noble lineage comes in, given as *clara gente*. The one addressed is moved, which closes the line as *moueris*.

#### Lines 986–987

> 986 afflicti miserere patris: moueat tua Peleus
>
> 987 pectora pro Priamo, pro nostro corpore Pyrrhus."

Someone is stricken, described as *afflicti*. Pity is asked for, told with *miserere*. A father comes in, named *patris*. Someone should move something, told with *moueat*. Something is yours, described as *tua*, though what it is is not yet said. Peleus comes in, who closes the line as *Peleus*. As the commentary says, Peleus is Achilles' father.

What is yours is the heart, named *pectora*. Priam comes in, given as *pro Priamo*. Something is our own, described as *pro nostro*, though what it is is not yet said. What is our own is a body, named *corpore*. Pyrrhus comes in, who closes the line as *Pyrrhus*. As the commentary says, Pyrrhus is Achilles' son.
