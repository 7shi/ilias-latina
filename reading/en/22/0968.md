### 968–976 (*Iliad* 22.294–299, 22.317–320)

#### Lines 968–970

> 968 Dumque retro cedit fraternaque rebus in artis
>
> 969 respicit auxilia et nullam uidet esse salutem,
>
> 970 sensit adesse dolos. Quid agat? quae numina supplex

A time is set as well, marked by *Dumque*. A backward direction comes in, marked by *retro*. He gives ground, told with *cedit*. Something is a brother's as well, described as *fraternaque*, though what it is is not yet said. Matters come in, named *rebus*. Narrow straits come in, given as *in artis*.

He looks back, told with *respicit*. What is a brother's is help, named *auxilia*. More is added, marked by *et*. None comes in, described as *nullam*, though what it is is not yet said. He sees, told with *uidet*. Being comes next, told with *esse*. What is none is safety, which closes the line as *salutem*.

He perceives, told with *sensit*. Being at hand comes next, told with *adesse*. Deceit comes in, named *dolos*. What comes in, as *Quid*. He is to do comes next, told with *agat*. What gods come in, given as *quae numina*. He is a suppliant, which closes the line as *supplex*.

#### Lines 971–972

> 971 inuocet? et toto languescunt corpore uires
>
> 972 auxiliumque negant; retinet uix dextera ferrum,

He is to invoke comes next, told with *inuocet*. More is added, marked by *et*. Something is whole, described as *toto*, though what it is is not yet said. Something grows faint, told with *languescunt*. What is whole is a body, named *corpore*. Strength comes in, which closes the line as *uires*.

Help comes in as well, named *auxiliumque*. They deny it, told with *negant*. Someone holds something, told with *retinet*. Scarcely comes in, marked by *uix*. A right hand comes in, named *dextera*. A blade comes in, which closes the line as *ferrum*.

#### Lines 973–976

> 973 nox oculos inimica tegit nec subuenit ullum
>
> 974 defesso auxilium; pugnat moriturus et alto
>
> 975 corde premit gemitus. Instat Nereius heros
>
> 976 turbatumque premit procul undique; tunc iacit hastam

Night comes in, named *nox*. Eyes come in, named *oculos*. It is hostile, described as *inimica*. It covers them, told with *tegit*. A denial comes, marked by *nec*. Something comes to aid, told with *subuenit*. Any comes in, described as *ullum*, though what it is is not yet said.

He is exhausted, described as *defesso*. What is any is help, named *auxilium*. He fights, told with *pugnat*. He is doomed to die, described as *moriturus*. More is added, marked by *et*. Something is deep, described as *alto*, though what it is is not yet said.

What is deep is a heart, named *corde*. He stifles something, told with *premit*. Groans come in, named *gemitus*. Someone bears down, told with *Instat*. The Nereian hero comes in, which closes the line as *Nereius heros*. As the commentary says, the Nereian hero is Achilles, whose mother Thetis is one of the Nereids.

He is driven in confusion as well, described as *turbatumque*. He presses him, told with *premit*. A distance comes in, marked by *procul*. Every side comes in, marked by *undique*. A time is set, marked by *tunc*. He hurls something, told with *iacit*. A spear comes in, which closes the line as *hastam*. What follows comes only in the next section.
