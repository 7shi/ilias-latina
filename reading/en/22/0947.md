### 947–950 (*Iliad* 22.226–231, 22.247, 22.296–303)

#### Lines 947–948

> 947 Huic subito ante oculos similis Tritonia fratri
>
> 948 occurrens iuuenem simulato decipit ore;

Hector comes back, as *Huic*. A time is set, marked by *subito*. Eyes come in, given as *ante oculos*. Someone is like, described as *similis*, though to whom is not yet said. Tritonia comes in, called *Tritonia*. As the commentary says, Tritonia is Minerva. Whom she is like is a brother, which closes the line as *fratri*.

She is meeting him, described as *occurrens*. The young man comes in, named *iuuenem*. Something is disguised, described as *simulato*, though what it is is not yet said. She deceives him, told with *decipit*. What is disguised is a face, which closes the line as *ore*.

#### Lines 949–950

> 949 nam cum Deiphobi tutum se credidit armis,
>
> 950 transtulit ad Danaos iterum sua numina Pallas.

A reason is given, marked by *nam*. A time is set, marked by *cum*. Deiphobus comes in, called *Deiphobi*. As the commentary says, Deiphobus is Hector's brother. Someone is safe, described as *tutum*. He does something to himself, given as *se*. He believed, told with *credidit*. Arms come in, which close the line as *armis*.

Someone turned something, told with *transtulit*. The Danaans come in, given as *ad Danaos*. As the commentary says, the Danaans are the Greeks. A time comes again, marked by *iterum*. Her divine favor comes in, given as *sua numina*. The one who turned it is Pallas, who closes the line as *Pallas*. As the commentary says, Tritonia and Pallas are names for the same goddess, Minerva.
