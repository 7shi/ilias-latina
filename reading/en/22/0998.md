### 998–1001 (*Iliad* 22.396–398, 24.16, 22.399–404)

#### Lines 998–1001

> 998 deligat ad currum pedibusque exsanguia membra
>
> 999 ter circum muros uictor trahit; altius ipsos
>
> 1000 fert domini successus equos. Tum maximus heros
>
> 1001 detulit ad Danaos foedatum puluere corpus.

Achilles binds him, told with *deligat*. A chariot comes in, given as *ad currum*. Feet come in as well, named *pedibusque*. Lifeless limbs come in, which close the line as *exsanguia membra*.

Three times comes in, marked by *ter*. The walls come in, given as *circum muros*. He is victor, described as *uictor*. He drags them, told with *trahit*. Higher comes in, marked by *altius*. Something is the very, described as *ipsos*, though what it is is not yet said.

Something carries them, told with *fert*. A master comes in, named *domini*. Success comes in, named *successus*. What are the very are horses, named *equos*. A time is set, marked by *Tum*. The greatest hero comes in, who closes the line as *maximus heros*.

He brought something back, told with *detulit*. The Danaans come in, given as *ad Danaos*. Something is befouled, described as *foedatum*, though what it is is not yet said. Dust comes in, named *puluere*. What is befouled is a body, which closes the line as *corpus*.
