### 988–995 (*Iliad* 22.344–347, 22.354)

#### Line 988

> 988 Talia Priamides. Quem contra durus Achilles:

Such things come in, described as *Talia*. Priamides comes in, called *Priamides*. As the commentary says, Priamides is Hector, son of Priam. Hector comes back, as *Quem*. Opposition comes in, marked by *contra*. Unyielding Achilles comes in, who closes the line as *durus Achilles*.

#### Lines 989–991

> 989 "Quid mea supplicibus temptas inflectere dictis
>
> 990 pectora, quem possem direptum more ferarum,
>
> 991 si sineret natura, meis absumere malis?

A question comes, marked by *Quid*. Something is his own, described as *mea*, though what it is is not yet said. Suppliant words come in, given as *supplicibus dictis*. The one addressed tries something, told with *temptas*. Softening comes next, told with *inflectere*.

What is his own is the heart, named *pectora*. Hector comes back, as *quem*. The speaker could do something, told with *possem*. He has been torn apart, described as *direptum*. The manner of wild beasts comes in, given as *more ferarum*.

A condition comes, marked by *si*. Something would allow it, told with *sineret*. Nature comes in, named *natura*. Something is his own, described as *meis*, though what it is is not yet said. Devouring comes next, told with *absumere*. What is his own is jaws, which close the line as *malis*.

#### Lines 992–993

> 992 Te uero tristesque ferae cunctaeque uolucres
>
> 993 diripient, auidosque canes tua uiscera pascent.

Hector is addressed as *Te*. Something is set against this, marked by *uero*. Grim beasts come in as well, given as *tristesque ferae*. All the birds come in as well, given as *cunctaeque uolucres*.

They will tear him apart, told with *diripient*. Ravenous dogs come in as well, given as *auidosque canes*. Your entrails come in, given as *tua uiscera*. They will feed, which closes the line as *pascent*.

#### Lines 994–995

> 994 Haec ex te capient Patrocli gaudia manes,
>
> 995 si sapiunt umbrae." Dum talia magnus Achilles

This comes in, as *Haec*, though what it is is not yet said. Hector comes back, given as *ex te*. Some will gain something, told with *capient*. Patroclus comes in, called *Patrocli*. As the commentary says, Patroclus is Achilles' companion. What this is is joy, named *gaudia*. Spirits come in, which close the line as *manes*.

A condition comes, marked by *si*. Some can feel, told with *sapiunt*. Shades come in, named *umbrae*. As the commentary says, the shades are the spirits of the dead. A time is set, marked by *Dum*. Such things come in, described as *talia*. Great Achilles comes in, who closes the line as *magnus Achilles*. What he does comes only in the next section.
