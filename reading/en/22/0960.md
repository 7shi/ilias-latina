### 960–967 (*Iliad* 22.289–291)

#### Lines 960–961

> 960 Exclamant Danai. Contra Priameius heros
>
> 961 uibratum iaculum Vulcania torquet in arma.

Some shout, told with *Exclamant*. The Danaans come in, named *Danai*. Opposition comes in, marked by *Contra*. Someone is Priam's son, described as *Priameius*. The hero comes in, who closes the line as *heros*. As the commentary says, the hero, Priam's son, is Hector.

His brandished javelin comes in, given as *uibratum iaculum*. Something is of Vulcan, described as *Vulcania*, though what it is is not yet said. He hurls it, told with *torquet*. What is of Vulcan is armor, which closes the line as *in arma*.

#### Lines 962–963

> 962 Nec successus adest: nam duro inflectitur auro
>
> 963 dissiluitque mucro. Gemuerunt agmina Troum.

A denial comes, marked by *Nec*. Success comes in, named *successus*. It is at hand, told with *adest*. A reason is given, marked by *nam*. Something is hard, described as *duro*, though what it is is not yet said. Something is bent, told with *inflectitur*. What is hard is gold, which closes the line as *auro*.

It shatters as well, told with *dissiluitque*. The point comes in, named *mucro*. Some groaned, told with *Gemuerunt*. Ranks come in, named *agmina*. They are of the Trojans, which close the line as *Troum*.

#### Lines 964–965

> 964 Concurrunt iterum collatis fortiter armis
>
> 965 inque uicem duros euitant comminus enses.

They charge together, told with *Concurrunt*. It is again, marked by *iterum*. Something is clashed, described as *collatis*, though what it is is not yet said. It is bravely, marked by *fortiter*. What is clashed is weapons, which close the line as *armis*.

Something is in turn, given as *inque uicem*. Some are hard, described as *duros*, though what they are is not yet said. They dodge them, told with *euitant*. Something is at close quarters, marked by *comminus*. What are hard is swords, which close the line as *enses*.

#### Lines 966–967

> 966 Nec sufferre ualet ultra sortemque supremam
>
> 967 stantemque Aeaciden defectis uiribus Hector.

A denial comes, marked by *Nec*. Bearing comes next, told with *sufferre*. He has the strength, told with *ualet*. Any longer comes in, marked by *ultra*. His final lot comes in as well, given as *sortemque supremam*.

Someone is standing firm as well, described as *stantemque*, though who it is is not yet said. Who is standing firm is Aeacides, called *Aeaciden*. As the commentary says, Aeacides is Achilles, grandson of Aeacus. His strength is failing, given as *defectis uiribus*. The one who cannot bear it is Hector, who closes the line as *Hector*.
