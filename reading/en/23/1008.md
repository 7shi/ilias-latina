### 1008–1009 (*Iliad* 23.536–538, 23.700–701)

#### Lines 1008–1009

> 1008 Tydides *tyrsin* cursu pedibusque ferocem
>
> 1009 Merionem superat; luctando uincitur Aiax

Tydides comes in, called *Tydides*. As the commentary says, Tydides is Diomedes, son of Tydeus. A word comes in that makes no sense, given as *tyrsin*. As the commentary says, one word of the first verse is damaged beyond sure reading. A race comes in, named *cursu*. Feet come in as well, named *pedibusque*. Someone is fierce, described as *ferocem*, though who it is is not yet said.

Who is fierce is Meriones, called *Merionem*. As the commentary says, Meriones is a Cretan commander. He defeats him, told with *superat*. Wrestling comes in, given as *luctando*. Someone is beaten, told with *uincitur*. Who is beaten is Ajax, who closes the line as *Aiax*. As the commentary says, Ajax is the great Greek warrior, son of Telamon. What follows comes only in the next section.
