### 1004–1005 (*Iliad* 23.108–110, 23.161–165)

#### Lines 1004–1005

> 1004 Interea uictor defleti corpus amici
>
> 1005 funerat Aeacides pompasque ad funera ducit.

A time is set, marked by *Interea*. Someone is victor, described as *uictor*. Someone is mourned, described as *defleti*, though who it is is not yet said. A body comes in, named *corpus*. Who is mourned is a friend, which closes the line as *amici*. As the commentary says, the friend is Patroclus.

Someone buries it, told with *funerat*. Who buries it is Aeacides, called *Aeacides*. As the commentary says, Aeacides is Achilles, grandson of Aeacus. Processions come in as well, named *pompasque*. Funeral rites come in, given as *ad funera*. He leads them, which closes the line as *ducit*.
