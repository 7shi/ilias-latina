### 807–810 (*Iliad* 16.278–283, 16.294–296)

#### Line 807

> 807 prouolat et falsa conterret imagine Troas.

He rushes forth, told with *prouolat*. More is added, marked by *et*. Something is false, described as *falsa*, though what it is is not yet said. He terrifies someone, told with *conterret*. What is false is a semblance, named *imagine*. The Trojans come in, which closes the line as *Troas*.

#### Lines 808–810

> 808 Qui modo turbabant Danaos animoque fremebant,
>
> 809 nunc trepidi fugiunt, fugientibus imminet ille
>
> 810 perturbatque ferox acies uastumque per agmen

The Trojans come back, as *Qui*. A time is set, marked by *modo*. They were throwing someone into confusion, told with *turbabant*. The Danaans come in, named *Danaos*. Their courage comes in, named *animoque*. They were roaring, which closes the line as *fremebant*.

Another time is set, marked by *nunc*. They are fearful, described as *trepidi*. They flee, told with *fugiunt*. The fugitives come in, described as *fugientibus*. Someone presses close upon them, told with *imminet*. Patroclus comes back, which closes the line as *ille*.

He throws them into confusion as well, told with *perturbatque*. He is fierce, described as *ferox*. Their lines come in, named *acies*. Something is vast as well, described as *uastumque*, though what it is is not yet said. What is vast is the host, which closes the line as *per agmen*. What more he does comes only in the next section.
