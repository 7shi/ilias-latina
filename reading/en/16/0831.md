### 831–833 (*Iliad* 16.793–804, 16.818–819)

#### Line 831

> 831 mentitos uultus simulati pandit Achillis

Lying features come in, given as *mentitos uultus*. Someone is counterfeit, described as *simulati*, though who it is is not yet said. Apollo lays them open, told with *pandit*. The counterfeit one is Achilles, who closes the line as *Achillis*. As the commentary says, the counterfeit Achilles is Patroclus wearing his companion's armor.

#### Lines 832–833

> 832 denudatque uirum, quem bello maximus Hector
>
> 833 pugnantem falsis postquam deprendit in armis,

Apollo strips someone bare as well, told with *denudatque*. The man comes in, named *uirum*. Patroclus comes back, as *quem*. Someone is in war, given as *bello*. Someone is greatest, described as *maximus*. The greatest one is Hector, who closes the line as *Hector*.

He is fighting, described as *pugnantem*. Something is false, given as *falsis*, though what it is is not yet said. A time is set, marked by *postquam*. Hector caught him, told with *deprendit*. What is false is armor, which closes the line as *in armis*. What follows comes only in the next section.
