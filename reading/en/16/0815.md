### 815–830 (*Iliad* 16.727–732, 16.787–789)

#### Lines 815–817

> 815 turbantemque acies respexit feruidus Hector,
>
> 816 tollit atrox animos uastisque immanis in armis
>
> 817 occurrit contra magnoque hunc increpat ore:

He is throwing something into disorder as well, described as *turbantemque*. The lines come in, named *acies*. Someone had looked upon him, told with *respexit*. He is fiery, described as *feruidus*. Hector closes the line as *Hector*.

He rouses something, told with *tollit*. He is fierce, described as *atrox*. His courage comes in, named *animos*. Something is vast as well, described as *uastisque*, though what it is is not yet said. He is huge, described as *immanis*. What is vast is his armor, which closes the line as *in armis*.

He comes out to meet him, told with *occurrit*. It is in opposition, marked by *contra*. Something is loud as well, described as *magnoque*, though what it is is not yet said. Patroclus comes back, as *hunc*. Hector challenges him, told with *increpat*. What is loud is his voice, which closes the line as *ore*.

#### Line 818

> 818 "Huc, age, huc conuerte gradum, fortissime Achilles:

A place is set, marked by *Huc*. He urges him on, told with *age*. The place is repeated, as *huc*. He bids him turn, told with *conuerte*. His step comes in, named *gradum*. Someone is bravest, described as *fortissime*. The one called bravest is Achilles, who closes the line as *Achilles*.

#### Lines 819–820

> 819 iam nosces ultrix quid Troica dextera possit
>
> 820 et quantum bello ualeat fortissimus Hector.

A time is set, marked by *iam*. Patroclus will learn, told with *nosces*. Something is avenging, described as *ultrix*, though what it is is not yet said. What can be done comes in, as *quid*. Something is Trojan, described as *Troica*. What is avenging is the right hand, named *dextera*. It can do so, which closes the line as *possit*.

More is added, marked by *et*. How much comes in, as *quantum*. Someone is in war, given as *bello*. He is strong, told with *ualeat*. Someone is bravest, described as *fortissimus*. The bravest one is Hector, who closes the line as *Hector*.

#### Lines 821–822

> 821 Nam licet ipse suis Mauors te protegat armis,
>
> 822 inuito tamen haec perimet te dextera Marte."

A reason is given, marked by *Nam*. It is permitted, told with *licet*. He comes himself, as *ipse*. Something is his own, described as *suis*, though what it is is not yet said. Mavors comes in, named *Mauors*. As the commentary says, Mavors is Mars, the god of war. Patroclus is addressed as *te*. Mavors should protect him, told with *protegat*. What is his own is his armor, which closes the line as *armis*.

Someone is unwilling, described as *inuito*, though who it is is not yet said. Yet something stands, marked by *tamen*. This comes in, as *haec*. It will destroy him, told with *perimet*. Patroclus is addressed again as *te*. A right hand comes in, named *dextera*. The unwilling one is Mars, who closes the line as *Marte*.

#### Lines 823–824

> 823 Ille silet spernitque minas animosaque dicta,
>
> 824 ut quem mentitur uerus credatur Achilles.

Patroclus comes back, pointed to as *Ille*. He remains silent, told with *silet*. He scorns something as well, told with *spernitque*. Threats come in, named *minas*. Something is spirited as well, described as *animosaque*, though what it is is not yet said. What is spirited is words, which close the line as *dicta*.

A purpose is set, marked by *ut*. Achilles comes back, as *quem*. He impersonates him, told with *mentitur*. Someone is true, described as *uerus*, though who it is is not yet said. He should be believed to be so, told with *credatur*. The true one is Achilles, who closes the line as *Achilles*.

#### Lines 825–828

> 825 Tunc prior intorquet collectis uiribus hastam
>
> 826 Dardanides, quam prolapsam celeri excipit ictu
>
> 827 Patroclus redditque uices et, mutua dona,
>
> 828 quod clipeo excussum uiridi tellure resedit.

A time is set, marked by *Tunc*. Someone is first, described as *prior*. He hurls something, told with *intorquet*. Something is gathered, described as *collectis*, though what it is is not yet said. What is gathered is strength, named *uiribus*. A spear comes in, which closes the line as *hastam*.

The first one is called *Dardanides*. As the commentary says, Dardanides is Hector, after Dardanus, an ancestor of Troy's royal family. The spear comes back, as *quam*. It glides forward, described as *prolapsam*. Something is swift, described as *celeri*, though what it is is not yet said. Someone catches it, told with *excipit*. What is swift is a stroke, which closes the line as *ictu*.

Patroclus comes back, named *Patroclus*. He pays back as well, told with *redditque*. A turn comes in, named *uices*. More is added, marked by *et*. Something is in exchange, described as *mutua*, though what it is is not yet said. What is in exchange is gifts, which close the line as *dona*.

What he sends comes in, as *quod*. A shield comes in, named *clipeo*. It was struck off, described as *excussum*. Something is green, described as *uiridi*, though what it is is not yet said. What is green is the earth, named *tellure*. It came to rest, which closes the line as *resedit*.

#### Lines 829–830

> 829 Tunc rigidos stringunt enses et comminus armis
>
> 830 inter se miscent, donec Troianus Apollo

A time is set, marked by *Tunc*. Something is stiff, described as *rigidos*, though what it is is not yet said. They draw something, told with *stringunt*. What is stiff is swords, named *enses*. More is added, marked by *et*. Close quarters come in, given as *comminus*. Arms come in, which close the line as *armis*.

They engage one another, given as *inter se*. They join battle, told with *miscent*. A limit is set, marked by *donec*. Someone is Trojan, described as *Troianus*, though who it is is not yet said. The Trojan one is Apollo, who closes the line as *Apollo*. What follows comes only in the next section.
