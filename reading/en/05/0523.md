### 523–525 (*Iliad* 5.628, 5.638–642, 5.657–659)

#### Lines 523–525

> 523 Tlepolemus magno satus Hercule, sed neque uires
>
> 524 hunc seruare patris nec tot potuere labores,
>
> 525 quin caderet tenuemque daret de corpore uitam.

The warrior fighting against him is Tlepolemus, named *Tlepolemus*. Someone is great, described as *magno*. He is born of someone, described as *satus*. The great one is Hercules, called *Hercule*. A turn comes, as *sed*. A denial follows, marked by *neque*. Strength comes in, which closes the line as *uires*, though whose strength it is is not yet said.

Tlepolemus comes back, as *hunc*. To save him is told with *seruare*. The father whose strength it was is named *patris*. Another denial follows, marked by *nec*. They were so many, described as *tot*. What they could do is told with *potuere*. What was so many is labors, called *labores*.

The outcome follows, marked by *quin*. That he fell is told with *caderet*. Something also is faint, described as *tenuemque*. Giving it is told with *daret*. It came from his body, given as *de corpore*. What was faint is life, named *uitam*.
