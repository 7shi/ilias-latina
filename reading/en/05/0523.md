### 523–525 (*Iliad* 5.628, 5.638–642, 5.657–659)

#### Lines 523–525

> 523 Tlepolemus magno satus Hercule, sed neque uires
>
> 524 hunc seruare patris nec tot potuere labores,
>
> 525 quin caderet tenuemque daret de corpore uitam.

The one who fights against Sarpedon is Tlepolemus, named *Tlepolemus*. Someone is great, described as *magno*. Tlepolemus is born of him, described as *satus*. The great one is Hercules, named *Hercule*. Something stands against this, marked by *sed*. A denial begins, marked by *neque*. Strength comes in, named *uires*, though whose strength it is is not yet said.

Tlepolemus comes back, as *hunc*. Saving comes next, told with *seruare*. The strength is his father's, named *patris*. Another denial follows, marked by *nec*. Something is so many, described as *tot*, though what it is is not yet said. None of it could save him, told with *potuere*. What was so many is labors, which close the line as *labores*.

What they could not keep from him comes next, marked by *quin*. He would fall, told with *caderet*. Something is faint as well, described as *tenuemque*. He would give it up, told with *daret*. It is from his body, given as *de corpore*. What was faint is life, which closes the line as *uitam*.
