### 486–495 (*Iliad* 5.494–497)

#### Lines 486–487

> 486 Nec cessat spes una Phrygum fortissimus Hector
>
> 487 sternere caede uiros atque agmina uertere Graium.

A denial begins, marked by *Nec*. Someone does not cease, told with *cessat*. He is the sole hope, described as *spes una*. He is the hope of the Phrygians, named *Phrygum*. He is the bravest, called *fortissimus*. The hero is Hector, who closes the line as *Hector*, though what he does not cease to do comes only in the next line.

He does not cease to strike down, told with *sternere*. It is in slaughter, given as *caede*. Those he strikes are men, called *uiros*. More follows, joined by *atque*. Ranks come in, named *agmina*. He turns them in flight, described as *uertere*. The ranks belong to the Greeks, who close the line as *Graium*.

#### Lines 488–492

> 488 Vt lupus in campis pecudes cum uidit apertis
>
> 489 (non actor gregis ipse, comes non horrida terret
>
> 490 turba canum), fremit esuriens et neglegit omnes
>
> 491 in mediosque greges auidus ruit, haut secus Hector
>
> 492 inuadit Danaos et territat ense cruento.

A comparison begins, marked by *Vt*. A wolf comes in, named *lupus*. It is on the plains, given as *in campis*. Sheep appear, called *pecudes*. A time is set, marked by *cum*. The wolf sees them, told with *uidit*. The plains were open, which closes the line as *apertis*.

A denial begins, marked by *non*. The driver comes in, named *actor*. He is the driver of the flock, called *gregis*. It is the man himself, given as *ipse*. An attendant comes next, described as *comes*. Another denial follows, marked by *non*. Something is fierce, described as *horrida*, though what it is is not yet said. Neither frightens the wolf, which closes the line as *terret*.

What was fierce is a pack, named *turba*. It is a pack of dogs, called *canum*. The wolf growls, told with *fremit*. It is hungry, described as *esuriens*. More follows, joined by *et*. It disregards them, given as *neglegit*. It scorns all of them, which closes the line as *omnes*.

It goes into the midst as well, given as *in mediosque*. The midst is that of the flocks, named *greges*. The wolf is greedy, described as *auidus*. In it rushes, told with *ruit*. Just so the story turns back, marked by *haut secus*. The one like the wolf is Hector, who closes the line as *Hector*.

Hector assails them, told with *inuadit*. Those he attacks are the Danaans, named *Danaos*. More follows, joined by *et*. He terrifies them, given as *territat*. A weapon strikes fear, called *ense*. The sword is bloody, which closes the line as *cruento*.

#### Lines 493–494

> 493 Deficiunt Graiorum acies, Phryges acrius instant
>
> 494 attolluntque animos: geminat uictoria uires.

They falter, told with *Deficiunt*. The Greeks come in, called *Graiorum*. What falters is their battle lines, named *acies*. The Phrygians come in, given as *Phryges*. They act more fiercely, marked by *acrius*. They press on, which closes the line as *instant*.

The Trojans lift something as well, told with *attolluntque*. What they raise is their spirits, named *animos*. Something doubles, given as *geminat*. What doubles it is victory, called *uictoria*. What is doubled is their strength, which closes the line as *uires*.

#### Line 495

> 495 Vt uidit socios infesto cedere Marte,

A time comes, marked by *Vt*. Someone saw it, told with *uidit*. The ones he saw are his comrades, named *socios*. Something is hostile, described as *infesto*, though what it is is not yet said. His comrades were giving way, given as *cedere*. What was hostile is Mars, which closes the line as *Marte*, though what he does when he sees it comes only in the next section.
