### 520–522 (*Iliad* 5.580, 5.629–630)

#### Lines 520–521

> 520 Antilochique Mydon. Post hos Iouis inclita proles
>
> 521 Sarpedon bellum funestaque proelia miscet.

Antilochus strikes as well, named *Antilochique*. The fallen driver is Mydon, called *Mydon*. A time after these comes in, given as *Post hos*. Jupiter comes in, named *Iouis*. Someone is renowned, described as *inclita*. The renowned one is his son, who closes the line as *proles*, though who he is comes only in the next line.

The son is Sarpedon, called *Sarpedon*. War comes in, named *bellum*. Something is deadly as well, described as *funestaque*. What is deadly is battles, called *proelia*. Sarpedon stirs them up, which closes the line as *miscet*.

#### Line 522

> 522 Quem contra infelix non aequis dimicat armis

It is against him, given as *Quem contra*. A warrior is ill-fated, described as *infelix*. A denial follows, marked by *non*. Something would be equal, described as *aequis*, though what it is is not yet said. He fights, told with *dimicat*. What was unequal is his arms, which close the line as *armis*, though who fights against him comes only in the next section.
