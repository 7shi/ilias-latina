### 520–522 (*Iliad* 5.580, 5.629–630)

#### Lines 520–521

> 520 Antilochique Mydon. Post hos Iouis inclita proles
>
> 521 Sarpedon bellum funestaque proelia miscet.

Antilochus comes in as well, named *Antilochique*. The weapons are now his, as they were Menelaus' before. The one who falls by them is Mydon, named *Mydon*. It is after these, given as *Post hos*. Jupiter comes in, named *Iouis*. Someone is renowned, described as *inclita*. The renowned one is his son, who closes the line as *proles*.

The son is Sarpedon, named *Sarpedon*. War comes in, named *bellum*. Something is deadly as well, described as *funestaque*. What is deadly is battles, named *proelia*. Sarpedon stirs them up, which closes the line as *miscet*.

#### Line 522

> 522 Quem contra infelix non aequis dimicat armis

Someone stands against Sarpedon, given as *Quem contra*. He is ill-fated, described as *infelix*. A denial comes, marked by *non*. Something is equal, described as *aequis*, though what it is is not yet said. He fights, told with *dimicat*. What is not equal is his arms, which close the line as *armis*. Who he is comes only in the next section.
