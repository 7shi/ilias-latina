### 447–448 (*Iliad* 5.159–160, 5.163–164)

#### Lines 447–448

> 447 Post hos infestus Chromiumque et Echemmona telo
>
> 448 proturbat celeri pariterque ad Tartara mittit.

It comes after these, given as *Post hos*. Diomedes is hostile, described as *infestus*. Chromius comes in as well, named *Chromiumque*. Another is joined to him by *et*. He is Echemmon, named *Echemmona*. A weapon comes in, named *telo*, though what kind it is is not yet said.

Diomedes drives them back, told with *proturbat*. The weapon is swift, described as *celeri*. It is done to both alike, marked by *pariterque*. Tartarus comes in, given as *ad Tartara*. As the commentary says, Tartarus here means the underworld, the realm of the dead. Diomedes sends them there, which closes the line as *mittit*.
