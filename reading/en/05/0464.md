### 464–466 (*Iliad* 5.311–318, 5.330–333)

#### Lines 464–465

> 464 quem Venus aetherias genetrix delapsa per auras
>
> 465 accipit et nigra corpus caligine condit.

Aeneas comes back, as *quem*. Venus enters, named *Venus*. Something is heavenly, described as *aetherias*, though what it is is not yet said. She is his mother, called *genetrix*. She glides down, told with *delapsa*. What was heavenly is the air, which closes the line as *per auras*.

Venus gathers him up, told with *accipit*. More follows, joined by *et*. Something is dark, described as *nigra*. What she shields is his body, named *corpus*. What was dark is a mist, called *caligine*. She hides him, which closes the line as *condit*.

#### Line 466

> 466 Non tulit Oenides animo nebulasque per ipsas

A denial begins, marked by *Non*. Someone could not endure it, told with *tulit*. The one who could not bear it is the grandson of Oeneus, named *Oenides*. As the commentary says, the grandson of Oeneus is Diomedes. He felt it in his spirit, called *animo*. Clouds come in as well, described as *nebulasque*. He charges through those very clouds, which closes the line as *per ipsas*, though what he does among them comes only in the next section.
