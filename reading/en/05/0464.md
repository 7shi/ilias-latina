### 464–466 (*Iliad* 5.311–318, 5.330–333)

#### Lines 464–465

> 464 quem Venus aetherias genetrix delapsa per auras
>
> 465 accipit et nigra corpus caligine condit.

Aeneas comes back, as *quem*. Venus enters, named *Venus*. Something is heavenly, described as *aetherias*, though what it is is not yet said. She is his mother, called *genetrix*. She glides down, told with *delapsa*. What was heavenly is the air, which closes the line as *per auras*.

Venus gathers him up, told with *accipit*. More follows, joined by *et*. Something is dark, described as *nigra*. His body comes in, named *corpus*. What was dark is a mist, named *caligine*. She hides him, which closes the line as *condit*.

#### Line 466

> 466 Non tulit Oenides animo nebulasque per ipsas

A denial begins, marked by *Non*. Someone bears it, told with *tulit*. The one who does not bear it is the grandson of Oeneus, named *Oenides*. As the commentary says, Oenides is Diomedes, named as the grandson of Oeneus. It is in his spirit, named *animo*. Clouds come in as well, named *nebulasque*. It is through the clouds themselves, which closes the line as *per ipsas*. What he does there comes only in the next section.
