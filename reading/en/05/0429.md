### 429–430 (*Iliad* 5.43–44)

#### Lines 429–430

> 429 Hinc petit Idomeneus aduersa parte ruentem
>
> 430 Maeoniden Phaestum, cuius post funera laetus

The fighting moves next, marked by *Hinc*. Someone attacks, told with *petit*. The one attacking is Idomeneus, named *Idomeneus*. A side is opposite, described as *aduersa*. It is from that side, called *parte*. Someone is rushing, which closes the line as *ruentem*, though who it is is not yet said.

The rushing one is the son of Maeon, called *Maeoniden*. His name is Phaestus, named *Phaestum*. Phaestus comes back, as *cuius*. It comes after his death, given as *post funera*. Idomeneus is rejoicing, which closes the line as *laetus*, though what he does in his joy comes only in the next section.
