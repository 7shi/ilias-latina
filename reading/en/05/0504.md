### 504–509 (*Iliad* 5.533–534, 5.561)

#### Lines 504–505

> 504 sic ferus Atrides aduersos fertur in hostes
>
> 505 infestasque Phrygum proturbat cuspide turmas.

It is like this, marked by *sic*. Someone is fierce, described as *ferus*. He is the son of Atreus, named *Atrides*. As the commentary says, this son of Atreus is Agamemnon. Something is opposite, described as *aduersos*, though what it is is not yet said. He is borne forward, told with *fertur*. What was opposite is the enemy, which closes the line as *in hostes*.

Something is hostile as well, described as *infestasque*, though what it is is not yet said. The Phrygians come in, named *Phrygum*. He drives them back, told with *proturbat*. A spearpoint comes in, named *cuspide*. What was hostile is their squadrons, which close the line as *turmas*.

#### Lines 506–507

> 506 Virtus clara ducis uires accendit Achiuum
>
> 507 et spes exacuit languentia militis arma:

Courage comes in, named *Virtus*. It is renowned, described as *clara*. It belongs to the leader, named *ducis*. Strength comes in, named *uires*. His courage kindles it, told with *accendit*. The strength belongs to the Achaeans, who close the line as *Achiuum*. As the commentary says, Achaeans and Danaans are names for the Greeks.

More follows, joined by *et*. Hope comes in, named *spes*. It sharpens something, told with *exacuit*. Something is flagging, described as *languentia*, though what it is is not yet said. It belongs to the soldiers, named *militis*. What was flagging is their weapons, which close the line as *arma*.

#### Line 508

> 508 funduntur Teucri, Danai laetantur ouantes.

Someone is routed, told with *funduntur*. Those routed are the Teucrians, named *Teucri*. As the commentary says, Phrygians and Teucrians are the Trojans here. The Danaans come in, named *Danai*. They rejoice, told with *laetantur*. They exult in triumph, which closes the line as *ouantes*.

#### Line 509

> 509 Tandem hic Aenean immisso tendere curru

At last something changes, marked by *Tandem*. It is here, marked by *hic*. Aeneas comes in, named *Aenean*. Something has been given free rein, described as *immisso*, though what it is is not yet said. He presses forward, told with *tendere*. What was given free rein is his chariot, which closes the line as *curru*, and what happens to him comes only in the next section.
