### 473–482 (*Iliad* 5.512–513)

#### Line 473

> 473 accenditque animos iterumque ad bella reducit.

Apollo kindles something in him as well, told with *accenditque*. It is his spirit, named *animos*. It happens once more as well, marked by *iterumque*. It is back to war, given as *ad bella*. Apollo leads him back, which closes the line as *reducit*.

#### Lines 474–475

> 474 Vndique consurgunt acies et puluere caelum
>
> 475 conditur horrendisque sonat clamoribus aether.

It is on all sides, marked by *Vndique*. They rise up, told with *consurgunt*. Those who rise are the battle lines, named *acies*. More follows, joined by *et*. Dust comes in, named *puluere*. The sky comes in, which closes the line as *caelum*, though what becomes of it is not yet said.

The sky is hidden by that dust, told with *conditur*. Something is dreadful as well, described as *horrendisque*, though what it is is not yet said. Something resounds, told with *sonat*. What was dreadful is shouts, named *clamoribus*. What resounds is the upper air, which closes the line as *aether*.

#### Lines 476–477

> 476 Hic alius rapido deiectus in aequora curru
>
> 477 proteritur pedibusque simul calcatur equorum

Here the view turns, marked by *Hic*. One man comes in, as *alius*. Something is swift, described as *rapido*, though what it is is not yet said. The man is thrown down, described as *deiectus*. The plain comes in, given as *in aequora*. What was swift is his chariot, which closes the line as *curru*.

He is crushed, told with *proteritur*. Feet come in as well, named *pedibusque*. It happens at the same time, marked by *simul*. He is trampled, told with *calcatur*. The feet belong to horses, which closes the line as *equorum*.

#### Lines 478–480

> 478 atque alius uolucri traiectus corpora telo
>
> 479 quadrupedis tergo pronus ruit; illius ense
>
> 480 deiectum longe caput a ceruice cucurrit;

More follows, joined by *atque*. Another comes in, as *alius*. Something is flying, described as *uolucri*, though what it is is not yet said. He is pierced through, described as *traiectus*. It is through the body, named *corpora*. What was flying is a missile, which closes the line as *telo*.

A four-footed steed comes in, named *quadrupedis*. Its back comes in, named *tergo*. He falls forward, described as *pronus*. Down he plunges, told with *ruit*. Someone else comes in, as *illius*, though what of his is meant is not yet said. A sword comes in, which closes the line as *ense*.

Something is struck off, described as *deiectum*. It is far, marked by *longe*. What was his is his head, named *caput*. It is from the neck, given as *a ceruice*. The head rolled, which closes the line as *cucurrit*.

#### Lines 481–482

> 481 hic iacet exanimis fuso super arma cerebro:
>
> 482 sanguine manat humus, campi sudore madescunt.

Here is another, marked by *hic*. He lies on the field, told with *iacet*. He is lifeless, described as *exanimis*. Something has been spilled, described as *fuso*, though what it is is not yet said. It is over armor, given as *super arma*. What was spilled is his brains, which closes the line as *cerebro*.

Blood comes in, named *sanguine*. Something streams with it, told with *manat*. What streams is the ground, named *humus*. The plains come in, named *campi*. Sweat comes in, named *sudore*. The fields are drenched, which closes the line as *madescunt*.
