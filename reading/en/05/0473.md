### 473–482 (*Iliad* 5.512–513)

#### Line 473

> 473 accenditque animos iterumque ad bella reducit.

Apollo kindles something in him as well, told with *accenditque*. It is his spirit, named *animos*. It happens once more as well, marked by *iterumque*. It is back to war, given as *ad bella*. Apollo leads him back, which closes the line as *reducit*.

#### Lines 474–475

> 474 Vndique consurgunt acies et puluere caelum
>
> 475 conditur horrendisque sonat clamoribus aether.

It is on all sides, marked by *Vndique*. They rise up, told with *consurgunt*. Those who rise are the battle lines, called *acies*. More follows, joined by *et*. Dust comes in, given as *puluere*. The sky comes in, which closes the line as *caelum*, though what becomes of it is not yet said.

The sky is hidden by that dust, told with *conditur*. Something is dreadful as well, described as *horrendisque*, though what it is is not yet said. A din resounds, given as *sonat*. What was dreadful is shouts, named *clamoribus*. What resounds is the upper air, which closes the line as *aether*.

#### Lines 476–477

> 476 Hic alius rapido deiectus in aequora curru
>
> 477 proteritur pedibusque simul calcatur equorum

Here the view turns, marked by *Hic*. One man comes in, as *alius*. Something is swift, described as *rapido*, though what it is is not yet said. The man is thrown down, told with *deiectus*. He falls onto the plain, given as *in aequora*. What was swift is his chariot, which closes the line as *curru*.

He is crushed, told with *proteritur*. Feet come upon him as well, called *pedibusque*. It happens at the same time, marked by *simul*. He is trampled underfoot, given as *calcatur*. The feet belong to horses, which closes the line as *equorum*.

#### Lines 478–480

> 478 atque alius uolucri traiectus corpora telo
>
> 479 quadrupedis tergo pronus ruit; illius ense
>
> 480 deiectum longe caput a ceruice cucurrit;

More follows, joined by *atque*. Another warrior appears, named *alius*. Something is flying, described as *uolucri*, though what it is is not yet said. He is pierced through, told with *traiectus*. The wound is in his body, given as *corpora*. What was flying is a missile, which closes the line as *telo*.

A four-footed steed comes in, called *quadrupedis*. The man was on its back, named *tergo*. He falls forward, described as *pronus*. Down he plunges, told with *ruit*. Another man is pointed to, as *illius*. A sword comes in, which closes the line as *ense*, though what it strikes comes only in the next line.

Something was struck off by it, described as *deiectum*. It flew far away, marked by *longe*. What was struck off is his head, named *caput*. It parted from his neck, given as *a ceruice*. The severed head rolled, which closes the line as *cucurrit*.

#### Lines 481–482

> 481 hic iacet exanimis fuso super arma cerebro:
>
> 482 sanguine manat humus, campi sudore madescunt.

Here another fallen man is seen, marked by *hic*. He lies on the field, told with *iacet*. He is lifeless, described as *exanimis*. Something has been spilled, given as *fuso*, though what it is is not yet said. It poured over armor, as *super arma*. What was spilled is his brains, which closes the line as *cerebro*.

Blood comes in, named *sanguine*. Things stream with it, told with *manat*. What streams is the ground, called *humus*. The plains come in, given as *campi*. Sweat is upon them, as *sudore*. The fields are drenched, which closes the line as *madescunt*.
