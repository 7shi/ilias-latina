### 389–393 (*Iliad* 5.1, 5.8)

#### Lines 389–393

> 389 Hic postquam Danaum longe cedentia uidit
>
> 390 agmina Tydides tumidumque increscere Martem,
>
> 391 in medias acies, qua plurimus imminet hostis,
>
> 392 irruit et uersas prosternit caede phalangas;
>
> 393 huc illuc ensemque ferox hastamque coruscat.

Something happens here, marked by *Hic*. It comes after something, marked by *postquam*. The Danaans come in, named *Danaum*. As the commentary says, the Danaans are the Greek army fighting against Troy. It is far back, marked by *longe*. Something is giving way, described as *cedentia*, though what it is is not yet said. Someone saw it, told with *uidit*.

What was giving way is the ranks, named *agmina*. The one who saw is the son of Tydeus, named *Tydides*. As the commentary says, Tydides is Diomedes. Something is swollen as well, described as *tumidumque*, though what it is is not yet said. It grows greater, told with *increscere*. What was swollen is Mars, named *Martem*. As the commentary says, Mars here also gives his name to war itself.

The midst comes in, given as *in medias*. It is the battle lines, named *acies*. It is where something is, marked by *qua*. Someone is there in the greatest numbers, described as *plurimus*. He presses, told with *imminet*. The one pressing is the enemy, which closes the line as *hostis*.

Diomedes rushes in, told with *irruit*. More follows, joined by *et*. Some have been routed, described as *uersas*. He lays them low, told with *prosternit*. It is with slaughter, named *caede*. The routed ones are phalanxes, which close the line as *phalangas*.

It is this way, marked by *huc*. It is that way too, marked by *illuc*. A sword comes in, named *ensemque*. He is fierce, described as *ferox*. A spear comes in as well, named *hastamque*. He flashes them, which closes the line as *coruscat*.
