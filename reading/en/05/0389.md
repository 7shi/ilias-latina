### 389–393 (*Iliad* 5.1, 5.8)

#### Lines 389–393

> 389 Hic postquam Danaum longe cedentia uidit
>
> 390 agmina Tydides tumidumque increscere Martem,
>
> 391 in medias acies, qua plurimus imminet hostis,
>
> 392 irruit et uersas prosternit caede phalangas;
>
> 393 huc illuc ensemque ferox hastamque coruscat.

A moment arrives, given as *Hic*. It happens after something, marked by *postquam*. The Danaans come in, named *Danaum*. As the commentary says, the Danaans are the Greeks. Something is far back, as *longe*. Something is giving way, described as *cedentia*, though what it is is not yet said. Someone saw it, which closes the line as *uidit*.

What was giving way is the ranks, named *agmina*. The one who saw them is the son of Tydeus, called *Tydides*. As the commentary says, this son of Tydeus is Diomedes. Something is swollen as well, described as *tumidumque*, though what it is is not yet said. It grows greater, told with *increscere*. What was swollen is Mars, who closes the line as *Martem*.

He goes into the midst, given as *in medias*. It is the battle lines, named *acies*. The place is marked by *qua*. Someone is there in greatest numbers, described as *plurimus*. They press hard, told with *imminet*. The ones pressing are the enemy, who close the line as *hostis*.

Diomedes rushes in, told with *irruit*. More follows, joined by *et*. Some have been routed, described as *uersas*, though who they are is not yet said. He strikes them down, given as *prosternit*. It is with slaughter, named *caede*. Those laid low are the phalanxes, which close the line as *phalangas*.

It is this way, marked by *huc*. It is that way, as *illuc*. A sword comes in as well, named *ensemque*. Diomedes is fierce, described as *ferox*. A spear comes in as well, called *hastamque*. He brandishes them, which closes the line as *coruscat*.
