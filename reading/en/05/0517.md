### 517–518 (*Iliad* 5.541–542, 5.561)

#### Lines 517–518

> 517 desilit et ualido Crethona<que> comminus ictu
>
> 518 Orsilochumque ferit, quorum post funera uictus

Aeneas leaps down, told with *desilit*. More follows, joined by *et*. Something is mighty, described as *ualido*, though what it is is not yet said. Crethon comes in, named *Crethona\<que>*. It is at close quarters, marked by *comminus*. What was mighty is a blow, which closes the line as *ictu*.

Orsilochus comes in as well, named *Orsilochumque*. Aeneas strikes them both, told with *ferit*. The two come back, as *quorum*. It is after their deaths, given as *post funera*. Someone is overcome, described as *uictus*; who it is comes only in the next section.
