### 517–518 (*Iliad* 5.541–542, 5.561)

#### Lines 517–518

> 517 desilit et ualido Crethona<que> comminus ictu
>
> 518 Orsilochumque ferit, quorum post funera uictus

Aeneas leaps down, told with *desilit*. More follows, joined by *et*. Something is mighty, described as *ualido*, though what it is is not yet said. Crethon comes in as well, named *Crethona<que>*. The combat is at close quarters, marked by *comminus*. What was mighty is a blow, which closes the line as *ictu*, though who is struck comes only in the next line.

Orsilochus is reached as well, called *Orsilochumque*. Aeneas strikes them both, told with *ferit*. The fallen pair comes back, as *quorum*. A time is set after their deaths, given as *post funera*. Someone is overcome, which closes the line as *uictus*, though who is overcome comes only in the next section.
