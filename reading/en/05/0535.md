### 535–536 (*Iliad* 5.859–887)

#### Line 535

> 535 attonitumque simul caelum petere ipsa coegit.

He was stunned as well, described as *attonitumque*. It happened at once, marked by *simul*. Heaven comes in, named *caelum*. To make for it is told with *petere*. Pallas herself comes in, as *ipsa*. Forcing him is told with *coegit*.

#### Line 536

> 536 Hic ille aetherio queritur sua uulnera regi

The scene shifts here, marked by *Hic*. Mavors comes back, as *ille*. The one he turns to is heavenly, described as *aetherio*, though who it is is not yet said. His complaint is told with *queritur*. What he complains of is his own, described as *sua*. The complaint is of wounds, named *uulnera*. The heavenly one is a king, who closes the line as *regi*. As the commentary says, the king of heaven is Jupiter.
