### 535–536 (*Iliad* 5.859–887)

#### Line 535

> 535 attonitumque simul caelum petere ipsa coegit.

He is stunned as well, described as *attonitumque*. It is at once, marked by *simul*. Heaven comes in, named *caelum*. He is to make for it, told with *petere*. Pallas herself comes in, as *ipsa*. She forced him to, which closes the line as *coegit*.

#### Line 536

> 536 Hic ille aetherio queritur sua uulnera regi

It is here, marked by *Hic*. Mavors comes back, pointed to as *ille*. Someone is heavenly, described as *aetherio*, though who it is is not yet said. Mavors complains, told with *queritur*. Something is his own, described as *sua*. It is his wounds, named *uulnera*. The heavenly one is a king, which closes the line as *regi*. As the commentary says, the king of heaven is Jupiter.
