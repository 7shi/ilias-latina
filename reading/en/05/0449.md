### 449–453 (*Iliad* 5.290, 5.294–296, 5.291–293)

#### Lines 449–451

> 449 Tu quoque Tydidae prostratus, Pandare, dextra
>
> 450 occidis, infelix, accepto uulnere tristi,
>
> 451 dextera qua naris fronti coniungitur imae;

The poem turns to someone, addressing him as *Tu*. It is so with him too, marked by *quoque*. The son of Tydeus comes in, named *Tydidae*, though what of his is meant is not yet said. The one addressed has been laid low, described as *prostratus*. He is Pandarus, called *Pandare*. What was Tydides' is his right hand, which closes the line as *dextra*.

Pandarus falls dead, told with *occidis*. He is unfortunate, described as *infelix*. Something has been received, described as *accepto*. It is a wound, named *uulnere*. The wound is grievous, which closes the line as *tristi*.

Something is on the right, described as *dextera*, though what it is is not yet said. It is the place, marked by *qua*. What was on the right is the nostril, named *naris*. The forehead comes in, named *fronti*. The nostril is joined to it, told with *coniungitur*. It is the lowest part of the forehead, which closes the line as *imae*.

#### Lines 452–453

> 452 dissipat et cerebrum galeae cum parte reuulsum
>
> 453 ossaque confossa spargit Tydideus ensis.

Something scatters, told with *dissipat*. More follows, joined by *et*. What is scattered is the brain, named *cerebrum*. A helmet comes in, named *galeae*. It is part of it, given as *cum parte*. The brain is torn out with it, which closes the line as *reuulsum*.

Bones come in as well, named *ossaque*. They have been pierced, described as *confossa*. Something strews them, told with *spargit*. Something is Tydides', described as *Tydideus*. What scatters and strews is his sword, which closes the line as *ensis*.
