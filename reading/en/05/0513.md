### 513–516 (*Iliad* 8.119–122)

#### Line 513

> 513 pectus in aurigae stomachoque infigitur alto;

A chest comes in, given as *pectus in*. It belongs to the charioteer, named *aurigae*. The stomach comes in as well, named *stomachoque*. The weapon is driven in, told with *infigitur*. It is deep, which closes the line as *alto*.

#### Lines 514–515

> 514 ille ruens ictu media inter lora rotasque
>
> 515 uoluitur et uitam calido cum sanguine fundit.

The charioteer is pointed to, as *ille*. He is falling, described as *ruens*. It is from the blow, named *ictu*. Something is in the midst, described as *media*, though the midst of what is not yet said. It is amid the reins, given as *inter lora*. Wheels come in as well, which close the line as *rotasque*.

He rolls among them, told with *uoluitur*. More follows, joined by *et*. His life comes in, named *uitam*. Something is warm, described as *calido*, though what it is is not yet said. What was warm is his blood, given as *cum sanguine*. He pours it out, which closes the line as *fundit*.

#### Line 516

> 516 Ingemit Aeneas curruque animosus ab alto

Someone groans, told with *Ingemit*. The one who groans is Aeneas, named *Aeneas*. A chariot comes in as well, named *curruque*. He is undaunted, described as *animosus*. It is from the high chariot, which closes the line as *ab alto*. What he does from it comes only in the next section.
