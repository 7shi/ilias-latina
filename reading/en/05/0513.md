### 513–516 (*Iliad* 8.119–122)

#### Line 513

> 513 pectus in aurigae stomachoque infigitur alto;

It strikes into a chest, given as *pectus in*. The chest belongs to the charioteer, called *aurigae*. His stomach is reached as well, named *stomachoque*. The weapon is driven in, told with *infigitur*. The wound is deep, which closes the line as *alto*.

#### Lines 514–515

> 514 ille ruens ictu media inter lora rotasque
>
> 515 uoluitur et uitam calido cum sanguine fundit.

The charioteer is pointed to, as *ille*. He plunges down, told with *ruens*. It is from the blow, named *ictu*. Something is in the midst, described as *media*, though the midst of what is not yet said. It is amid the reins, given as *inter lora*. Wheels are there as well, which closes the line as *rotasque*, though what becomes of him comes only in the next line.

He rolls among them, told with *uoluitur*. More follows, joined by *et*. His life comes in, named *uitam*. Something is warm, described as *calido*, though what it is is not yet said. What was warm is his blood, given as *cum sanguine*. He pours it out, which closes the line as *fundit*.

#### Line 516

> 516 Ingemit Aeneas curruque animosus ab alto

Someone groans, told with *Ingemit*. The one who groans is Aeneas, named *Aeneas*. His chariot comes in as well, called *curruque*. He is undaunted, described as *animosus*. It is from on high, which closes the line as *ab alto*, though what he does from it comes only in the next section.
