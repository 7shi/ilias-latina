### 396–404 (*Iliad* 5.136–143, 5.93–94, 5.9–12)

#### Lines 396–400

> 396 Ille, boum ueluti uiso grege saeua leaena,
>
> 397 quam stimulat ieiuna fames, ruit agmina contra
>
> 398 et prostrata necat uesano corpora dente,
>
> 399 sic ruit in medios hostes Calydonius heros,
>
> 400 uirginis armigerae monitis et numine tutus.

Diomedes is pointed to as *Ille*. Cattle come in, named *boum*. A comparison begins, marked by *ueluti*. Something has been seen, described as *uiso*, though what it is is not yet said. What was seen is a herd of them, called *grege*. The attacker is fierce, given as *saeua*. She is a lioness, who closes the line as *leaena*.

The lioness is referred to again as *quam*. Something drives her, told with *stimulat*. Something is ravenous, described as *ieiuna*. What drives her is hunger, named *fames*. She charges forward, given as *ruit*. Ranks face her, called *agmina*. She goes against them, which closes the line as *contra*.

More follows, joined by *et*. Some are laid low, described as *prostrata*, though who they are is not yet said. She slaughters them, told with *necat*. Something is frenzied, described as *uesano*, though what it is is not yet said. Those laid low are their bodies, named *corpora*. What was frenzied is her teeth, which close the line as *dente*.

The comparison is answered, marked by *sic*. Diomedes rushes in, told with *ruit*. He goes into the midst, given as *in medios*. It is the enemy, named *hostes*. He is the Calydonian hero, who closes the line as *Calydonius heros*. As the commentary says, the Calydonian hero is Diomedes.

A maiden comes in, named *uirginis*. She is armed, described as *armigerae*. Diomedes has her counsel, called *monitis*. More follows, joined by *et*. She brings divine power, as *numine*. He is protected by it, which closes the line as *tutus*.

#### Lines 401–402

> 401 Conuersi dant terga Phryges, fugientibus ille
>
> 402 instat et exstructos morientum calcat aceruos.

Some have turned, described as *Conuersi*, though who they are is not yet said. They give something, told with *dant*. What they give is their backs, named *terga*. The ones who turned are the Phrygians, called *Phryges*. As the commentary says, the Phrygians are the Trojans. They are in flight, given as *fugientibus*. Diomedes comes back, which closes the line as *ille*.

He presses upon them, told with *instat*. More follows, joined by *et*. Something is heaped up, described as *exstructos*, though what it is is not yet said. It is of the dying, called *morientum*. He tramples them underfoot, given as *calcat*. What was heaped up is heaps of bodies, which close the line as *aceruos*.

#### Lines 403–404

> 403 Dumque ferit sternitque uiros, uidet ecce Daretis
>
> 404 aduerso stantes furibundus in agmine natos,

A time is marked as well, given as *Dumque*. He strikes, told with *ferit*. He also lays them low, described as *sternitque*. Those he fells are men, named *uiros*. Diomedes catches sight of someone, as *uidet*. Something appears, marked by *ecce*. Dares comes in, which closes the line as *Daretis*.

Something stands opposite, described as *aduerso*, though what it is is not yet said. They stand in place, called *stantes*. Diomedes is raging, given as *furibundus*. What was opposite is the battle line, as *in agmine*. The ones he sees are Dares' sons, which closes the line as *natos*. What he does to them comes only in the next section.
