### 396–404 (*Iliad* 5.136–143, 5.93–94, 5.9–12)

#### Lines 396–400

> 396 Ille, boum ueluti uiso grege saeua leaena,
>
> 397 quam stimulat ieiuna fames, ruit agmina contra
>
> 398 et prostrata necat uesano corpora dente,
>
> 399 sic ruit in medios hostes Calydonius heros,
>
> 400 uirginis armigerae monitis et numine tutus.

Diomedes comes back, pointed to as *Ille*, though what he does is not yet said. Cattle come in, named *boum*. It is like something, marked by *ueluti*. Something has been seen, described as *uiso*. It is a herd, named *grege*. Something is fierce, described as *saeua*, though what it is is not yet said. The fierce one is a lioness, who closes the line as *leaena*.

The lioness comes back, as *quam*. Something drives her, told with *stimulat*. Something is ravenous, described as *ieiuna*. What drives her is hunger, named *fames*. She charges, told with *ruit*. Ranks come in, named *agmina*. She charges against them, which closes the line as *contra*.

More follows, joined by *et*. Some have fallen, described as *prostrata*. She kills them, told with *necat*. Something is frenzied, described as *uesano*. The fallen ones are bodies, named *corpora*. What was frenzied is her teeth, which close the line as *dente*.

So it is with him, marked by *sic*. He rushes, told with *ruit*. It is into the midst, given as *in medios*. The midst is the enemy, named *hostes*. The one who rushes is the Calydonian hero, which closes the line as *Calydonius heros*. As the commentary says, he is the Calydonian hero through his father Tydeus, who came from Calydon.

A maiden comes in, named *uirginis*. She is armed, described as *armigerae*. As the commentary says, the armed maiden is Pallas. Counsel comes in, named *monitis*. More follows, joined by *et*. Divine power comes in, named *numine*. He is protected by them, which closes the line as *tutus*.

#### Lines 401–402

> 401 Conuersi dant terga Phryges, fugientibus ille
>
> 402 instat et exstructos morientum calcat aceruos.

Some have turned, described as *Conuersi*, though who they are is not yet said. They give something, told with *dant*. It is their backs, named *terga*. The ones who turned are the Phrygians, named *Phryges*. As the commentary says, the Phrygians here are the Trojans. Some are fleeing, described as *fugientibus*. Diomedes comes back, pointed to as *ille*.

He presses on them, told with *instat*. More follows, joined by *et*. Something is heaped up, described as *exstructos*, though what it is is not yet said. It is of the dying, named *morientum*. He tramples it, told with *calcat*. What was heaped up is piles, which close the line as *aceruos*.

#### Lines 403–404

> 403 Dumque ferit sternitque uiros, uidet ecce Daretis
>
> 404 aduerso stantes furibundus in agmine natos,

A time is set, marked by *Dumque*. He strikes, told with *ferit*. He fells as well, told with *sternitque*. Those he fells are men, named *uiros*. He sees something, told with *uidet*. It is sudden, marked by *ecce*. Dares comes in, named *Daretis*, though what of his is meant is not yet said.

Something is opposite, described as *aduerso*, though what it is is not yet said. Some are standing, described as *stantes*. Diomedes is raging, described as *furibundus*. What was opposite is the ranks, given as *in agmine*. What was Dares' is his sons, which closes the line as *natos*. As the commentary says, Dares is their father, a Trojan priest.
