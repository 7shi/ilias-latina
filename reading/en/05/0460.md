### 460–463 (*Iliad* 5.302–305, 5.309–310)

#### Lines 460–462

> 460 saxum ingens medio quod forte iacebat in agro,
>
> 461 bis seni quod uix iuuenes tellure mouerent,
>
> 462 sustulit et magno conamine misit in hostem.

Diomedes turns instead to a rock, named *saxum*. That rock is huge, described as *ingens*. It is in the midst, given as *medio*, though of what is not yet said. The rock comes back, as *quod*. It happened to be there, marked by *forte*. It was lying, told with *iacebat*. What it lay in was the field, which closes the line as *in agro*.

Twice six men are needed, given as *bis seni*. The rock is referred to again, as *quod*. They could scarcely do so, marked by *uix*. Those men are youths, called *iuuenes*. The ground comes in, named *tellure*. They could barely move it, which closes the line as *mouerent*.

Diomedes lifts the stone, told with *sustulit*. More follows, joined by *et*. Something is great, described as *magno*. What is great is his effort, named *conamine*. He hurled it, told with *misit*. It is aimed against the enemy, which closes the line as *in hostem*.

#### Line 463

> 463 Ille ruit prostratus humi cum fortibus armis,

Aeneas comes back, pointed to as *Ille*. He crashes down, told with *ruit*. He is laid low, described as *prostratus*. It is on the ground, called *humi*. It is with stout protection, given as *cum fortibus*. What was stout is his armor, which closes the line as *armis*.
