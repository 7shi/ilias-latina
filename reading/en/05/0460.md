### 460–463 (*Iliad* 5.302–305, 5.309–310)

#### Lines 460–462

> 460 saxum ingens medio quod forte iacebat in agro,
>
> 461 bis seni quod uix iuuenes tellure mouerent,
>
> 462 sustulit et magno conamine misit in hostem.

A rock comes in, named *saxum*. It is huge, described as *ingens*. Something is in the middle, described as *medio*, though the middle of what is not yet said. The rock comes back, as *quod*. It is by chance, marked by *forte*. It was lying, told with *iacebat*. The middle is that of the field, which closes the line as *in agro*.

The count is twice six, given as *bis seni*. The rock comes back again, as *quod*. It is scarcely, marked by *uix*. The twelve are young men, named *iuuenes*. The ground comes in, named *tellure*. They would move it, which closes the line as *mouerent*.

Diomedes lifts it, told with *sustulit*. More follows, joined by *et*. Something is great, described as *magno*. It is his effort, named *conamine*. He hurled it, told with *misit*. It went at the enemy, which closes the line as *in hostem*.

#### Line 463

> 463 Ille ruit prostratus humi cum fortibus armis,

Aeneas comes back, pointed to as *Ille*. He falls, told with *ruit*. He is laid low, described as *prostratus*. It is on the ground, marked by *humi*. Something stout is with him, given as *cum fortibus*. What was stout is his armor, which closes the line as *armis*.
