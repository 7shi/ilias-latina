### 413–423 (*Iliad* 5.20–24)

#### Lines 413–416

> 413 conspexit frater, stricto celer aduolat ense
>
> 414 germanique cupit fatorum exsistere uindex.
>
> 415 Sed neque uim saeui nec fortia sustinet arma
>
> 416 Tydidae contraque tamen defendere temptat.

The witness caught sight of him, told with *conspexit*. He is his brother, named *frater*. Something is drawn, described as *stricto*, though what it is is not yet said. He is swift, called *celer*. He rushes forward, given as *aduolat*. What was drawn is a sword, which closes the line as *ense*.

His brother comes in as well, named *germanique*. He desires something, told with *cupit*. It concerns his fate, called *fatorum*. He would stand forth, given as *exsistere*. He wants to be an avenger, which closes the line as *uindex*.

A turn comes, marked by *Sed*. A denial begins, given as *neque*. Might comes in, named *uim*. The foe is fierce, described as *saeui*. Another denial follows, as *nec*. Something is strong, called *fortia*. Idaeus cannot withstand them, told with *sustinet*. What is strong is weapons, which close the line as *arma*.

The weapons belong to the son of Tydeus, named *Tydidae*. Idaeus faces him as well, described as *contraque*. Yet he still acts, marked by *tamen*. He tries to ward him off, told with *defendere*. He attempts it, which closes the line as *temptat*.

#### Lines 417–423

> 417 Vt uolucris, discerpta sui cum corpora nati
>
> 418 accipitrem laniare uidet nec tendere contra,
>
> 419 auxilium neque ferre suo ualet anxia nato
>
> 420 quodque potest, leuibus plaudit sua pectora pennis,
>
> 421 sic hostem Idaeus germani caede superbum
>
> 422 spectat atrox miseroque nequit succurrere fratri
>
> 423 et, nisi cessisset, dextra cecidisset eadem.

A comparison begins, marked by *Vt*. A bird comes in, named *uolucris*. Something is torn apart, described as *discerpta*, though what it is is not yet said. It is her own, called *sui*. A time is marked, given as *cum*. What was torn is the body, as *corpora*. It belongs to her offspring, who closes the line as *nati*.

A hawk comes in, named *accipitrem*. It tears the flesh, told with *laniare*. The mother bird watches, as *uidet*. A denial comes, marked by *nec*. She cannot strive, given as *tendere*. It would be against the hawk, which closes the line as *contra*.

Help is needed, named *auxilium*. Another denial follows, marked by *neque*. She cannot bring it, told with *ferre*. The young one is her own, called *suo*. She has no power, given as *ualet*. The mother is anxious, described as *anxia*. It is for her offspring, who closes the line as *nato*.

She does what she can, marked by *quodque*. It is within her power, told with *potest*. Something is light, described as *leuibus*, though what it is is not yet said. She beats her body, given as *plaudit*. It is her own, called *sua*. She beats her breast, named *pectora*. What was light is her wings, which close the line as *pennis*.

The comparison turns, marked by *sic*. The enemy comes in, named *hostem*. Idaeus is the watcher, called *Idaeus*. It concerns his brother, as *germani*. It is his slaughter, given as *caede*. The enemy is proud, which closes the line as *superbum*.

He watches, told with *spectat*. Idaeus is fierce, described as *atrox*. The brother is wretched as well, called *miseroque*. He cannot act, given as *nequit*. He cannot bring aid, as *succurrere*. It is to his brother, which closes the line as *fratri*.

More follows, joined by *et*. A condition comes, marked by *nisi*. He had withdrawn, told with *cessisset*. A right hand comes in, named *dextra*. He would have fallen, given as *cecidisset*. The hand was the same, which closes the line as *eadem*.
