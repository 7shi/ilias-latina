### 413–423 (*Iliad* 5.20–24)

#### Lines 413–416

> 413 conspexit frater, stricto celer aduolat ense
>
> 414 germanique cupit fatorum exsistere uindex.
>
> 415 Sed neque uim saeui nec fortia sustinet arma
>
> 416 Tydidae contraque tamen defendere temptat.

Someone saw him, told with *conspexit*. It is his brother, named *frater*. Something is drawn, described as *stricto*. The brother is swift, described as *celer*. He flies up, told with *aduolat*. What was drawn is a sword, which closes the line as *ense*.

A brother comes in, named *germanique*, though what of the brother's is meant is not yet said. Idaeus longs for something, told with *cupit*. What was the brother's is his fate, named *fatorum*. Idaeus would stand forth, told with *exsistere*. He would be its avenger, which closes the line as *uindex*.

Something stands against this, marked by *Sed*. A denial begins, marked by *neque*. Might comes in, named *uim*. It is the savage one's, named *saeui*. Another denial follows, marked by *nec*. Something is strong, described as *fortia*. He withstands, told with *sustinet*. What was strong is arms, named *arma*.

They are the arms of the son of Tydeus, named *Tydidae*. Something is against him as well, given as *contraque*. It is so all the same, marked by *tamen*. Idaeus would hold him off, told with *defendere*. He tries, which closes the line as *temptat*.

#### Lines 417–423

> 417 Vt uolucris, discerpta sui cum corpora nati
>
> 418 accipitrem laniare uidet nec tendere contra,
>
> 419 auxilium neque ferre suo ualet anxia nato
>
> 420 quodque potest, leuibus plaudit sua pectora pennis,
>
> 421 sic hostem Idaeus germani caede superbum
>
> 422 spectat atrox miseroque nequit succurrere fratri
>
> 423 et, nisi cessisset, dextra cecidisset eadem.

It is like something, marked by *Vt*. A bird comes in, named *uolucris*. Something is torn, described as *discerpta*, though what it is is not yet said. It is her own, described as *sui*. A time is set, marked by *cum*. What was torn is the body, named *corpora*. It is of her young, which closes the line as *nati*.

A hawk comes in, named *accipitrem*. It is mangling, told with *laniare*. She sees it, told with *uidet*. A denial follows, marked by *nec*. She would strive, told with *tendere*. It would be against the hawk, which closes the line as *contra*.

Help comes in, named *auxilium*. Another denial follows, marked by *neque*. She would bring it, told with *ferre*. It is for her own, described as *suo*. She has the power, told with *ualet*. She is anxious, described as *anxia*. Her own is her young, which closes the line as *nato*.

What she can do comes next, given as *quodque*. She can, told with *potest*. Something is light, described as *leuibus*. She beats something, told with *plaudit*. It is her own, described as *sua*. It is her breast, named *pectora*. What was light is her wings, which close the line as *pennis*.

So it is with someone, marked by *sic*. The enemy comes in, named *hostem*. Idaeus comes in, named *Idaeus*. A brother comes in, named *germani*. It is his slaughter, named *caede*. The enemy is proud of it, which closes the line as *superbum*.

Idaeus watches, told with *spectat*. He is fierce, described as *atrox*. Someone is wretched as well, described as *miseroque*, though who it is is not yet said. Idaeus cannot, told with *nequit*. He would come to aid, told with *succurrere*. The wretched one is his brother, which closes the line as *fratri*.

More follows, joined by *et*. A condition comes, marked by *nisi*. He had withdrawn, told with *cessisset*. A right hand comes in, named *dextra*. He would have fallen, told with *cecidisset*. The hand is the same one, which closes the line as *eadem*.
