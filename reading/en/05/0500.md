### 500–503 (*Iliad* 5.161–162)

#### Lines 500–501

> 500 Vt Libycus cum forte leo procul agmina uidit
>
> 501 laeta boum passim uirides errare per herbas,

A comparison begins, marked by *Vt*. Someone is Libyan, described as *Libycus*, though who it is is not yet said. A time is set, marked by *cum*. It happens by chance, given as *forte*. The Libyan creature is a lion, named *leo*. It watches from afar, as *procul*. Droves come into view, called *agmina*. The lion sees them, which closes the line as *uidit*, though what they are comes only in the next line.

The droves are thriving, described as *laeta*. They are herds of cattle, named *boum*. They are scattered here and there, marked by *passim*. Something is green, given as *uirides*, though what it is is not yet said. The cattle wander, told with *errare*. What was green is the grass, which closes the line as *per herbas*.

#### Lines 502–503

> 502 attollit ceruice iubas sitiensque cruoris
>
> 503 in mediam erecto contendit pectore turbam,

The lion raises something, told with *attollit*. It is on its neck, named *ceruice*. What it raises is its mane, called *iubas*. It is thirsting as well, described as *sitiensque*. What it thirsts for is blood, which closes the line as *cruoris*.

It heads into the midst, given as *in mediam*, though the midst of what is not yet said. Something is held high, described as *erecto*, though what it is is not yet said. The lion charges forward, told with *contendit*. What is held high is its chest, named *pectore*. The midst is that of the herd, which closes the line as *turbam*, though what he does among them comes only in the next section.
