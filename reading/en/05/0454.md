### 454–459 (*Iliad* 5.297–302)

#### Lines 454–455

> 454 Iamque manum Aeneas simul et Calydonius heros
>
> 455 contulerant, iactis inter se comminus hastis;

A new encounter begins now, marked by *Iamque*. Hand to hand combat comes in, named *manum*, though who joins it is not yet said. Aeneas comes in, called *Aeneas*. It is at the same time, given as *simul*. Another warrior is joined to him by *et*. He is the Calydonian hero, who closes the line as *Calydonius heros*.

They had joined battle, told with *contulerant*. Something had been hurled, described as *iactis*, though what it is is not yet said. It was at each other, given as *inter se*. It was at close quarters, marked by *comminus*. What had been hurled is spears, which close the line as *hastis*.

#### Lines 456–457

> 456 undique rimabant inimico corpora ferro,
>
> 457 et modo cedebant retro, modo deinde coibant.

They search on every side, marked by *undique*. They probe for an opening, told with *rimabant*. Something is hostile, described as *inimico*. What they probe is bodies, named *corpora*. What was hostile is steel, which closes the line as *ferro*.

Their movement continues, joined by *et*. At one moment they move, marked by *modo*. They gave way, told with *cedebant*. It was backward, given as *retro*. At another moment they turn, called *modo*. Then they advance again, told with *deinde*. They closed in once more, which closes the line as *coibant*.

#### Lines 458–459

> 458 Postquam utrique diu steterant nec uulnera magnus
>
> 459 qua daret infesto Tydides ense uidebat,

A time comes, marked by *Postquam*. Both warriors hold firm, described as *utrique*. They had remained for a long time, given as *diu*. They had stood their ground, told with *steterant*. A denial follows, marked by *nec*. Wounds come in, named *uulnera*. Someone is mighty, which closes the line as *magnus*, though who it is is not yet said.

He looks where an opening might be, marked by *qua*. He would inflict them, told with *daret*. Something is hostile, described as *infesto*. The mighty warrior is the son of Tydeus, named *Tydides*. What was hostile is his sword, called *ense*. He could see no way, which closes the line as *uidebat*, though what he does next comes only in the next section.
