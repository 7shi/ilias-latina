### 424–428 (*Iliad* 5.38–41)

#### Lines 424–425

> 424 Nec minus in Teucros armis furit alter Atrides
>
> 425 insequiturque acies et ferro funera miscet.

A denial comes, marked by *Nec*. It is no less, marked by *minus*. The Teucrians come in, given as *in Teucros*. As the commentary says, the Teucrians are the Trojans. Arms come in, named *armis*. Someone rages, told with *furit*. The raging one is the other son of Atreus, who closes the line as *alter Atrides*. As the commentary says, the other son of Atreus is Agamemnon.

Agamemnon pursues as well, told with *insequiturque*. The battle lines come in, named *acies*. More follows, joined by *et*. It is with the sword, given as *ferro*. Carnage comes in, named *funera*. He wreaks it, which closes the line as *miscet*.

#### Lines 426–428

> 426 Obuius huic fatis occurrit ductus iniquis
>
> 427 infelix Odius, quem uastae cuspidis ictu
>
> 428 sternit et ingenti scapulas transuerberat hasta.

Someone comes into his path, described as *Obuius*, though who it is is not yet said. It is in Agamemnon's way, given as *huic*. Fate comes in, named *fatis*. He meets him, told with *occurrit*. He was driven, described as *ductus*. The fate was cruel, which closes the line as *iniquis*.

The one who meets him is unhappy, described as *infelix*. The one in his path is Odius, named *Odius*. Odius comes back, as *quem*. Something is massive, described as *uastae*. What is massive is a spearhead, named *cuspidis*. A blow comes from it, which closes the line as *ictu*.

Agamemnon lays him low, told with *sternit*. More follows, joined by *et*. Something is huge, described as *ingenti*, though what it is is not yet said. His shoulders come in, named *scapulas*. Agamemnon drives through them, told with *transuerberat*. What was huge is his spear, which closes the line as *hasta*.
