### 436–441 (*Iliad* 5.95–99, 5.134–135)

#### Lines 436–437

> 436 Parte alia uolitat sinuoso Pandarus arcu
>
> 437 Tydidenque oculis immensa per agmina quaerit;

A part of the field comes in, named *Parte*. It is another, described as *alia*. Someone flits about, told with *uolitat*. Something is curved, described as *sinuoso*, though what it is is not yet said. The one flitting about is Pandarus, named *Pandarus*. What was curved is his bow, which closes the line as *arcu*.

The son of Tydeus comes back as well, named *Tydidenque*. Eyes come in, called *oculis*. Something is vast, described as *immensa*, though what it is is not yet said. It is through the ranks, given as *per agmina*. Pandarus searches for him, which closes the line as *quaerit*.

#### Lines 438–440

> 438 quem postquam Troum sternentem corpora uidit,
>
> 439 horrida contento derexit spicula cornu
>
> 440 et summas umeri destringit acumine partes.

Diomedes comes back, as *quem*. A time is set, marked by *postquam*. The Trojans come in, named *Troum*. Diomedes is laying them low, described as *sternentem*. Those he fells are bodies, called *corpora*. Pandarus sees him, which closes the line as *uidit*.

Something is fearsome, described as *horrida*, though what it is is not yet said. Something is drawn taut, given as *contento*. Pandarus aims, told with *derexit*. What was fearsome is arrows, named *spicula*. What was drawn taut is his horn bow, which closes the line as *cornu*.

More follows, joined by *et*. The hit is on the surface, described as *summas*. A shoulder comes in, named *umeri*. The arrow grazes him, told with *destringit*. It is with its point, called *acumine*. What was on the surface is the parts of the shoulder, which close the line as *partes*.

#### Line 441

> 441 Tum uero ardescit iuuenis Calydonius ira

Then something happens, marked by *Tum*. It is indeed so, given as *uero*. He blazes up, told with *ardescit*. The wounded warrior is a youth, described as *iuuenis*. He comes from Calydon, called *Calydonius*. What blazes is anger, which closes the line as *ira*.
