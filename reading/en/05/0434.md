### 434–435 (*Iliad* 5.76, 5.79–80)

#### Lines 434–435

> 434 Eurypylus gladio uenientem Hypsenora fundit
>
> 435 et pariter uita iuuenem spoliauit et armis.

The one wearing the armor is Eurypylus, named *Eurypylus*. A sword comes in, called *gladio*. Someone approaches, described as *uenientem*. The approaching one is Hypsenor, given as *Hypsenora*. Eurypylus lays him low, which closes the line as *fundit*.

Eurypylus goes further, joined by *et*. It is done alike, marked by *pariter*. Life comes in, named *uita*. The victim is a young man, described as *iuuenem*. Eurypylus strips him, told with *spoliauit*. Another loss is joined by *et*. What he loses is armor, which closes the line as *armis*.
