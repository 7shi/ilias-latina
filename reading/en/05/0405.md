### 405–412 (*Iliad* 5.11, 5.15–19)

#### Lines 405–410

> 405 Phegeaque Idaeumque simul; quem cuspide Phegeus
>
> 406 occupat ante graui, sed uulnera depulit umbo
>
> 407 uitatumque solo ferrum stetit. Haud mora: totis
>
> 408 ingentem torquet Tydides uiribus hastam
>
> 409 transadigitque uiri pectus: pars cuspidis ante
>
> 410 eminet et prodit scapulis pars altera fossis.

The first is Phegeus, named *Phegeaque*. The second is Idaeus, named *Idaeumque*. They are together, marked by *simul*. Diomedes comes back, as *quem*. A spear-point comes in, named *cuspide*, though what kind it is is not yet said. Phegeus comes in, named *Phegeus*.

He attacks Diomedes, told with *occupat*. It is first, marked by *ante*. The spear-point is heavy, described as *graui*. Something stands against this, marked by *sed*. A wound comes in, named *uulnera*. Something warded it off, told with *depulit*. What warded it off is the shield-boss, which closes the line as *umbo*.

Something was avoided as well, described as *uitatumque*. The ground comes in, named *solo*. What was avoided is the iron, named *ferrum*. It stood fixed, told with *stetit*. There is no delay, given as *Haud mora*. Something is all, described as *totis*, though what it is is not yet said.

Something is huge, described as *ingentem*. Someone hurls it, told with *torquet*. He is the son of Tydeus, named *Tydides*. What was described as *totis* is his strength, named *uiribus*. What was huge is a spear, which closes the line as *hastam*.

He drives it through as well, told with *transadigitque*. It is the man, named *uiri*. What it goes through is his chest, named *pectus*. Part comes in, named *pars*. It is of the point, named *cuspidis*. It is in front, marked by *ante*.

It stands out, told with *eminet*. More follows, joined by *et*. Something comes forth, told with *prodit*. Shoulder blades come in, named *scapulis*. What comes forth is the other part, named *pars altera*. The shoulder blades are pierced, which closes the line as *fossis*.

#### Lines 411–412

> 411 Hunc ubi fundentem calidum de pectore flumen
>
> 412 uersantemque oculos animamque per ora uomentem

Phegeus comes back, pointed to as *Hunc*. A time is set, marked by *ubi*. He is pouring something, described as *fundentem*. Something is hot, described as *calidum*, though what it is is not yet said. It is from his chest, given as *de pectore*. What was hot is a stream, which closes the line as *flumen*.

He is rolling something as well, described as *uersantemque*. It is his eyes, named *oculos*. His life comes in as well, named *animamque*. It is through his mouth, given as *per ora*. He is vomiting it, which closes the line as *uomentem*. Who sees him comes only in the next section.
