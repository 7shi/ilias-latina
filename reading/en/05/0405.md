### 405–412 (*Iliad* 5.11, 5.15–19)

#### Lines 405–410

> 405 Phegeaque Idaeumque simul; quem cuspide Phegeus
>
> 406 occupat ante graui, sed uulnera depulit umbo
>
> 407 uitatumque solo ferrum stetit. Haud mora: totis
>
> 408 ingentem torquet Tydides uiribus hastam
>
> 409 transadigitque uiri pectus: pars cuspidis ante
>
> 410 eminet et prodit scapulis pars altera fossis.

The first son is Phegeus, named *Phegeaque*. Idaeus is with him, called *Idaeumque*. They stand together, marked by *simul*. Diomedes is faced by them, as *quem*. A spear comes in, given as *cuspide*. The attacker is Phegeus, who closes the line as *Phegeus*.

Phegeus attacks him, told with *occupat*. He strikes first, marked by *ante*. His spear was heavy, described as *graui*. A turn comes, as *sed*. A wound is threatened, named *uulnera*. The blow is warded off, given as *depulit*. What warded it off is the shield-boss, which closes the line as *umbo*.

The blow was avoided as well, described as *uitatumque*. The ground comes next, named *solo*. What was avoided is the iron point, called *ferrum*. It stood fixed in the earth, given as *stetit*. There is no delay, told as *Haud mora*. Something is done with all, described as *totis*, though of what is not yet said.

Something is huge, described as *ingentem*, though what it is is not yet said. Someone hurls it, told with *torquet*. The hurler is the son of Tydeus, named *Tydides*. What was done with all is his strength, called *uiribus*. What was huge is his spear, which closes the line as *hastam*.

He drives it through as well, told with *transadigitque*. It is the man's, called *uiri*. What he pierces is the chest, named *pectus*. A part of something comes next, as *pars*. It is of the spear-point, given as *cuspidis*. It is in front, which closes the line as *ante*.

The front part stands out, told with *eminet*. More follows, joined by *et*. It comes forth, given as *prodit*. It is from the shoulder blades, named *scapulis*. It is the other part, called *pars altera*. The shoulder blades were pierced, which closes the line as *fossis*.

#### Lines 411–412

> 411 Hunc ubi fundentem calidum de pectore flumen
>
> 412 uersantemque oculos animamque per ora uomentem

The fallen warrior is pointed to as *Hunc*. A moment is set, marked by *ubi*. He pours something out, told with *fundentem*. Something is hot, described as *calidum*. It pours from his chest, given as *de pectore*. What was hot is a stream of blood, which closes the line as *flumen*.

He also rolls something, told with *uersantemque*. What he rolls is his eyes, named *oculos*. His life comes in as well, called *animamque*. It pours through his mouth, given as *per ora*. He spews it out, which closes the line as *uomentem*. What the witness does comes only in the next section.
