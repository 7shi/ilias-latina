### 496–499 (*Iliad* 5.528–532)

#### Lines 496–497

> 496 rex Danaum sublimis equo uolat agmina circum
>
> 497 hortaturque duces animosque in proelia firmat.

The one who acts is a king, named *rex*. He is the king of the Danaans, called *Danaum*. As the commentary says, the king of the Danaans is Agamemnon. He sits aloft, described as *sublimis*. It is upon his horse, given as *equo*. He speeds along, told with *uolat*. The troops come in, as *agmina*. He rides around them, which closes the line as *circum*.

He urges someone on as well, told with *hortaturque*. Those he urges are the leaders, named *duces*. Their spirits come in as well, called *animosque*. It is for battle, given as *in proelia*. He strengthens their resolve, which closes the line as *firmat*.

#### Lines 498–499

> 498 Mox ipse in medios audax se proripit hostes
>
> 499 oppositasque acies stricto diuerberat ense.

Soon the action changes, marked by *Mox*. It is the king himself, called *ipse*. He heads into the midst, given as *in medios*, though the midst of what is not yet said. He is bold, described as *audax*. He turns himself, as *se*. He rushes onward, told with *proripit*. The midst is that of the enemy, who close the line as *hostes*.

Something stands against him as well, given as *oppositasque*. What opposes him is ranks, named *acies*. Something has been drawn, described as *stricto*, though what it is is not yet said. He cuts through the ranks, told with *diuerberat*. What was drawn is his sword, which closes the line as *ense*.
