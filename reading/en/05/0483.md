### 483–485 (*Iliad* 5.514)

#### Line 483

> 483 Emicat interea Veneris pulcherrima proles

Someone springs forward, told with *Emicat*. It happens in the meantime, marked by *interea*. Venus comes in, named *Veneris*. Someone is most beautiful, described as *pulcherrima*. The most beautiful one is her son, who closes the line as *proles*. As the commentary says, he is Aeneas, whom Apollo has just restored to battle.

#### Lines 484–485

> 484 densaque Graiorum premit agmina nudaque late
>
> 485 terga metit gladio funestaque proelia miscet.

Something is close-packed as well, described as *densaque*, though what it is is not yet said. The Greeks come in, called *Graiorum*. Aeneas presses hard upon them, told with *premit*. What was close-packed is their ranks, named *agmina*. Something is unprotected as well, described as *nudaque*, though what it is is not yet said. It is far and wide, which closes the line as *late*.

What was unprotected is their backs, named *terga*. He reaps them, told with *metit*. A sword comes in, named *gladio*. Something is deadly as well, described as *funestaque*. What is deadly is fighting, named *proelia*. Aeneas stirs it up, which closes the line as *miscet*.
