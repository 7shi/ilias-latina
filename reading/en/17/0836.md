### 836–838 (*Iliad* 17.132–137)

#### Lines 836–838

> 836 Vindicat exstincti corpus Telamonius Aiax
>
> 837 oppositoque tegit clipeo. Priameia pubes
>
> 838 laetitia exsultat, Danai sua uulnera maerent.

Someone defends something, told with *Vindicat*. Someone is dead, described as *exstincti*. His body comes in, named *corpus*. Someone is Telamonian, described as *Telamonius*, though who it is is not yet said. Who is Telamonian is Ajax, who closes the line as *Aiax*. As the commentary says, Telamonian Ajax is Ajax, the son of Telamon.

Something is held before it as well, described as *oppositoque*, though what it is is not yet said. Ajax covers him, told with *tegit*. What is held before it is a shield, named *clipeo*. The youth of Priam comes in, which closes the line as *Priameia pubes*. As the commentary says, the youth of Priam are the Trojan warriors, named after Priam, their king.

Joy comes in, named *laetitia*. They exult, told with *exsultat*. The Danaans come in, as *Danai*. As the commentary says, the Danaans are the Greeks. Their own wounds come in, given as *sua uulnera*. They mourn, which ends the seventeenth book as *maerent*.
