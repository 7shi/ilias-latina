### 780–781 (*Iliad* 14.409–413, 14.418)

#### Lines 780–781

> 780 quem saxo ingenti percussum maximus Aiax
>
> 781 depulit et toto prostratum corpore fudit.

Hector comes back, as *quem*. A huge stone comes in, given as *saxo ingenti*. He is struck, described as *percussum*. Someone is mighty, described as *maximus*, though who it is is not yet said. The mighty one is Ajax, who closes the line as *Aiax*. As the commentary says, this Ajax is the son of Telamon.

Ajax drove him back, told with *depulit*. More is added, marked by *et*. Something is whole, described as *toto*, though what it is is not yet said. Hector is prostrate, described as *prostratum*. What is whole is his body, named *corpore*. Ajax laid him low, told with *fudit*.
