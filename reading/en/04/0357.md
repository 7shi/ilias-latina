### 357–359 (*Iliad* 4.450–451, 4.456)

#### Lines 357–359

> 357 inque uicem Troumque cadunt Danaumque cateruae
>
> 358 nec requies datur ulla uiris: sonat undique Mauors
>
> 359 telorumque uolant cunctis e partibus imbres.

Things happen in turn, marked by *inque uicem*. The Trojans come in, named *Troumque*. They fall, told with *cadunt*. The Danaans come in as well, called *Danaumque*. As the commentary says, the Danaans are the Greeks. What fall are companies, which close the line as *cateruae*.

A denial comes, marked by *nec*. Rest comes in, named *requies*. It would be given, told with *datur*. None at all is given, described as *ulla*. Those who get none are the men, spoken of as *uiris*. Something resounds, told with *sonat*. It is on all sides, marked by *undique*. What resounds is the god of war, who closes the line as *Mauors*. As the commentary says, Mavors is the Roman war god, also called Mars.

Missiles come in as well, named *telorumque*. Something flies, told with *uolant*. Something is all, described as *cunctis*, though what it is is not yet said. What was described as *cunctis* is the sides they come from, given as *e partibus*. What fly are showers of them, which close the line as *imbres*.
