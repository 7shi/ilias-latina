### 366–372 (*Iliad* 4.482, 4.489–494)

#### Lines 366–369

> 366 ora rigat moriens. Tum magnis Antiphus hastam
>
> 367 uiribus aduersum conatus corpore toto
>
> 368 torquet in Aeaciden; telumque errauit ab hoste
>
> 369 inque hostem cecidit, transfixit et inguina Leucon:

His face comes in, named *ora*. The blood drenches it, told with *rigat*. He does so as he dies, described as *moriens*. Then things move on, marked by *Tum*. Something is great, described as *magnis*, though what it is is not yet said. Antiphus comes in, called *Antiphus*. A spear comes next, which closes the line as *hastam*.

What was described as *magnis* is his strength, named *uiribus*. Someone stands opposite, described as *aduersum*. Antiphus strains forward, given as *conatus*. A body comes in, called *corpore*. It is his whole body, which closes the line as *toto*.

He hurls the spear, given as *torquet*. It goes at the descendant of Aeacus, as *in Aeaciden*. As the commentary says, this descendant of Aeacus is Ajax. The weapon comes in as well, named *telumque*. It went astray, told with *errauit*. It strayed from the enemy, which closes the line as *ab hoste*.

It goes toward a foe instead, given as *inque hostem*. It struck there, as *cecidit*. It pierced through, told with *transfixit*. More follows, joined by *et*. What it pierced is the groin, named *inguina*. The struck foe is Leucus, who closes the line as *Leucon*.

#### Lines 370–371

> 370 concidit infelix prostratus uulnere forti
>
> 371 et carpit uirides moribundus dentibus herbas.

He falls, told with *concidit*. The fallen warrior is wretched, described as *infelix*. He is laid low, given as *prostratus*. A wound comes in, named *uulnere*. It is mighty, which closes the line as *forti*.

More follows, joined by *et*. He plucks at something, told with *carpit*. Something is green, described as *uirides*, though what it is is not yet said. He is dying, called *moribundus*. It is with his teeth, given as *dentibus*. What was green is the grass, which closes the line as *herbas*.

#### Line 372

> 372 Impiger Atrides casu commotus amici

Someone is tireless, described as *Impiger*. He is the son of Atreus, named *Atrides*. A fall comes next, called *casu*. He is stirred by it, given as *commotus*. The fall was his friend's, which closes the line as *amici*. What he does comes only in the next section.
