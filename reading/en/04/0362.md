### 362–365 (*Iliad* 4.473–474, 4.480–481)

#### Lines 362–365

> 362 Inde manu forti Graiorum terga prementem
>
> 363 occupat Anthemione satum Telamonius Aiax
>
> 364 et praedurato transfixit pectora telo:
>
> 365 purpuream uomit ille animam cum sanguine mixtam,

Things move on from there, marked by *Inde*. A hand comes in, named *manu*. It is strong, described as *forti*. The Greeks come in, called *Graiorum*. Their backs come next, as *terga*. Someone is pressing upon them, which closes the line as *prementem*.

Someone overtakes him, told with *occupat*. Anthemion comes in, named *Anthemione*. The one overtaken is his son, called *satum*. As the commentary says, this son of Anthemion is Simoisius. The one who overtakes him is of Telamon's line, described as *Telamonius*. He is Ajax, who closes the line as *Aiax*.

More follows, joined by *et*. Something is hardened, described as *praedurato*, though what it is is not yet said. He pierces him through, told with *transfixit*. What he pierces is the chest, named *pectora*. What was hardened is a weapon, which closes the line as *telo*.

Something is purple, described as *purpuream*. He spews it out, told with *uomit*. The dying man is pointed to as *ille*. What was purple is his life, named *animam*. It comes with blood, given as *cum sanguine*. Life and blood are mingled, which closes the line as *mixtam*.
