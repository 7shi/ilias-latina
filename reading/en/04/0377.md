### 377–379 (*Iliad* 4.517–520, 4.524–526)

#### Lines 377–378

> 377 Iamque Amarynciden saxi deiecerat ictu
>
> 378 Pirous Imbrasides dederatque silentibus umbris;

Time moves on as well, marked by *Iamque*. The son of Amarynceus comes in, named *Amarynciden*. As the commentary says, this son of Amarynceus is Diores. A stone comes next, called *saxi*. Someone had struck him down, told with *deiecerat*. It was with a blow, which closes the line as *ictu*.

The one who struck him down is Pirous, named *Pirous*. He is the son of Imbrasus, called *Imbrasides*. He had also given him over, told with *dederatque*. Those he gave him to are silent, described as *silentibus*. They are the shades, which close the line as *umbris*.

#### Line 379

> 379 dumque auidus praedae iuuenem spoliare parabat,

A time is set as well, marked by *dumque*. Pirous is eager, described as *auidus*. His eagerness is for plunder, named *praedae*. The fallen man is a youth, called *iuuenem*. He made to strip him, told with *spoliare*. He made ready for it, which closes the line as *parabat*. What interrupts him comes only in the next section.
