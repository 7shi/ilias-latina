### 380–388 (*Iliad* 4.527–528, 4.531, 4.536, 4.451, 4.543–544)

#### Lines 380–383

> 380 desuper hasta uenit dextra librata Thoantis
>
> 381 perque uiri scapulas animosaque pectora transit;
>
> 382 in uultus ruit ille suos calidumque cruorem
>
> 383 ore uomit stratusque super sua palpitat arma.

Something comes from above, marked by *desuper*. It is a spear, named *hasta*. It approaches, told with *uenit*. A right hand comes next, called *dextra*. The spear was poised in it, described as *librata*. The hand belongs to Thoas, which closes the line as *Thoantis*.

It strikes through as well, given as *perque*, though through what is not yet said. The victim is a man, called *uiri*. What it strikes through is his shoulders, named *scapulas*. Something is spirited as well, described as *animosaque*. It is his breast, as *pectora*. The spear passes all the way through, which closes the line as *transit*.

His face comes in, given as *in uultus*. He falls onto it, told with *ruit*. The man is pointed to as *ille*. The face is his own, described as *suos*. Something is hot as well, described as *calidumque*. What is hot is blood, which closes the line as *cruorem*.

His mouth comes in, named *ore*. He spews the blood out, told with *uomit*. He is stretched out as well, described as *stratusque*. It is upon something, marked by *super*. It is his own, called *sua*. He twitches, told with *palpitat*. What he lies upon is armor, which closes the line as *arma*.

#### Lines 384–388

> 384 Sanguine Dardanii manabant undique campi,
>
> 385 manabant amnes passim. Pugnabat ubique
>
> 386 immixtis ardens amborum exercitus armis
>
> 387 et modo Troianis uirtus, modo crescit Achiuis
>
> 388 laetaque per uarios petitur uictoria casus.

Blood comes in, named *Sanguine*. Something is Dardanian, described as *Dardanii*, though what it is is not yet said. Things ran with blood, told with *manabant*. It is on all sides, marked by *undique*. What was Dardanian is the plains, which close the line as *campi*.

They ran again, told with *manabant*. Rivers come next, named *amnes*. It is everywhere, marked by *passim*. Fighting goes on, told with *Pugnabat*. It is on every side, which closes the line as *ubique*.

Something is mingled, described as *immixtis*, though what it is is not yet said. Something is blazing, described as *ardens*. It is of both sides, marked by *amborum*. The one fighting is the army, named *exercitus*. What was mingled is weapons, which close the line as *armis*.

More follows, joined by *et*. It is now one way, marked by *modo*. It is for the Trojans, named *Troianis*. Courage comes in, named *uirtus*. Now it is the other way, marked by *modo*. Courage grows, told with *crescit*. It turns to the Achaeans, who close the line as *Achiuis*. As the commentary says, the Achaeans are the Greeks.

Something is joyful as well, described as *laetaque*. It is through various ways, given as *per uarios*. It is sought, told with *petitur*. What is joyful is victory, named *uictoria*. The various ways are fortunes, which ends the fourth book as *casus*.
