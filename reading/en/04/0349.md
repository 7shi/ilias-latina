### 349–350 (*Iliad* 4.138, 4.150, 4.210–212)

#### Lines 349–350

> 349 dissecat. Excedit pugna gemebundus Atrides
>
> 350 castraque tuta petit, quem doctus ab arte paterna

The missile cuts through the tunic, told with *dissecat*. Someone withdraws, told with *Excedit*. It is from the battle, named *pugna*. The one withdrawing is groaning, described as *gemebundus*. He is the son of Atreus, which closes the line as *Atrides*. As the commentary says, this son of Atreus is Menelaus.

The camp comes in as well, named *castraque*. The camp is safe, described as *tuta*. Menelaus heads for it, told with *petit*. Menelaus comes back, as *quem*. Someone is taught, described as *doctus*. He is taught by art, given as *ab arte*. The art is his father's, which closes the line as *paterna*. Who is taught comes only in the next section.
