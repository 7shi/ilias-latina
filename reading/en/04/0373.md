### 373–376 (*Iliad* 4.495–499, 4.501–504)

#### Lines 373–376

> 373 Democoonta petit teloque aduersa trabali
>
> 374 tempora transadigit uaginaque horridus ensem
>
> 375 eripit; ille suis moriens resupinus in armis
>
> 376 concidit et terram moribundo uertice pulsat.

A warrior comes in, named *Democoonta*. Atrides attacks him, told with *petit*. A weapon comes in as well, called *teloque*. Something faces him, described as *aduersa*, though what it is is not yet said. The weapon is like a beam, which closes the line as *trabali*.

What was facing him is the temples, named *tempora*. The weapon drives through them, told with *transadigit*. A sheath comes in as well, called *uaginaque*. Atrides is fearsome, described as *horridus*. A sword comes next, which closes the line as *ensem*.

He snatches the sword out, told with *eripit*. The struck man is pointed to as *ille*. Something is his own, described as *suis*, though what it is is not yet said. He is dying, described as *moriens*. He is on his back, described as *resupinus*. What was his own is his armor, which closes the line as *in armis*.

He falls, told with *concidit*. More follows, joined by *et*. The earth comes in, named *terram*. Something is dying, described as *moribundo*. It is his head, called *uertice*. He strikes the ground with it, which closes the line as *pulsat*.
