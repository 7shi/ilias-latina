### 750–753 (*Iliad* 11.145–147, 11.221, 11.240, 11.248–261)

#### Line 750

> 750 Hippolochum; post hos gladio petit Iphidamanta.

The brother is Hippolochus, named *Hippolochum*. It is after them, given as *post hos*. A sword comes in, named *gladio*. Agamemnon attacks someone, told with *petit*. The one he attacks is Iphidamas, who closes the line as *Iphidamanta*.

#### Lines 751–753

> 751 Hic frater dextram iaculo ferit; ille dolore
>
> 752 acrior accepto fugientem Antenore natum
>
> 753 persequitur traxitque ferox cum uulnere poenas.

A place is set, marked by *Hic*. His brother comes in, named *frater*. A right arm comes in, named *dextram*. A javelin comes in, named *iaculo*. He strikes it, told with *ferit*. Agamemnon comes back, as *ille*. Pain comes in, which closes the line as *dolore*.

He is fiercer, described as *acrior*. The pain is received, described as *accepto*. Someone is fleeing, described as *fugientem*, though who it is is not yet said. Antenor comes in, named *Antenore*. The one who is fleeing is his son, who closes the line as *natum*. As the commentary says, the son of Antenor is Coon.

Agamemnon pursues him, told with *persequitur*. He exacts something as well, told with *traxitque*. He is fierce, described as *ferox*. His wound comes in, given as *cum uulnere*. What he exacts is vengeance, which closes the line as *poenas*.
