### 796–798 (*Iliad* 15.405–409, 15.415–416, 15.716–718)

#### Line 796

> 796 atque inde aduersis propellunt uiribus hostem.

More is added, marked by *atque*. A place is set, marked by *inde*. Something is opposing, described as *aduersis*, though what it is is not yet said. They drive something back, told with *propellunt*. What is opposing is their strength, named *uiribus*. The enemy comes in, which closes the line as *hostem*.

#### Lines 797–798

> 797 Fit pugna ante rates, saeuit Mauortius Hector
>
> 798 et poscit flammas totamque incendere classem

Something takes place, told with *Fit*. A battle comes in, named *pugna*. The ships come in, given as *ante rates*. Someone rages, told with *saeuit*. Someone is Mavortian, described as *Mauortius*, though who it is is not yet said. As the commentary says, Mavortian is from Mavors, a name of Mars, the Roman god of war. The Mavortian one is Hector, who closes the line as *Hector*.

More is added, marked by *et*. Hector calls for something, told with *poscit*. Flames come in, named *flammas*. Something is whole as well, described as *totamque*. Burning comes next, told with *incendere*. What is whole is the fleet, which closes the line as *classem*. What more Hector does comes only in the next section.
