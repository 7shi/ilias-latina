### 132–135 (*Iliad* 2.53–56, 2.76–83)

#### Line 132

> 132 in coetum proceres remque omnibus ordine pandit:

Those he calls come into council, given as *in coetum*. They are the chiefs, named *proceres*. He has a matter as well, named *remque*. It is for them all, given as *omnibus*. It goes in order, given as *ordine*. He sets it out, told with *pandit*.

#### Line 133

> 133 cuncti promittunt socias in proelia uires

Now it is all of them, named *cuncti*. They promise, told with *promittunt*. Something is shared, described as *socias*, though what is shared is not yet said. It is for battle, given as *in proelia*. What was described as *socias* is their strength, which closes the line as *uires*.

#### Lines 134–135

> 134 hortanturque ducem. Quorum rex fortia dictis
>
> 135 pectora collaudans grates agit omnibus aequas.

They also encourage someone, told with *hortanturque*. It is their leader, named *ducem*. Next the chiefs come back, as *Quorum*, though what of theirs is meant is not yet said. The king comes in, named *rex*. Something is brave, described as *fortia*, though what is brave is not yet said either. Words come next, named *dictis*.

What was described as *fortia*, and belongs to the chiefs, is their hearts, named *pectora*. He praises them, told with *collaudans*. Thanks come next, named *grates*. He gives them, told with *agit*. They go to all, given as *omnibus*. The thanks are equal, which closes the verse as *aequas*.
