### 218–222 (*Iliad* 2.731–733)

#### Lines 218–219

> 218 quem sequitur iuxta Podalirius atque Machaon,
>
> 219 altaque ter denis sulcarunt aequora proris.

Philoctetes comes back, as *quem*. Others follow him, told with *sequitur*. They come close behind, marked by *iuxta*. The first is Podalirius, named *Podalirius*. Another joins him, marked by *atque*. He is Machaon, who closes the line as *Machaon*.

Something is deep, described as *altaque*, though what is deep is not yet said. The count is three tens, given as *ter denis*. They plowed the deep thing, told with *sulcarunt*. What was described as *altaque* is the seas, named *aequora*. They plowed them with ships, named by their prows as *proris*.

#### Lines 220–221

> 220 His ducibus Graiae Troiana ad litora puppes
>
> 221 bis septem uenere minus quam mille ducentae.

The leaders already named come back, as *His ducibus*. Something is Greek, described as *Graiae*, though what it is is not yet said. Something belongs to Troy, described as *Troiana*. It is the shores, given as *ad litora*. What was described as *Graiae* is the ships, named by their sterns as *puppes*.

The count is twice seven, given as *bis septem*. The ships came, told with *uenere*. They are fewer, marked by *minus*. Another number is set against them, marked by *quam*. That number is twelve hundred, which closes the line as *mille ducentae*.

#### Line 222

> 222 Iamque citi appulerant classes camposque tenebant,

Things have moved on, marked by *Iamque*. The men were swift, described as *citi*. They had brought something ashore, told with *appulerant*. It is their fleets, named *classes*. The plains come next, named *camposque*. They held them, which closes the line as *tenebant*.
