### 120–128 (*Iliad* 2.16–23, 2.26–29)

#### Lines 120–122

> 120 Nec mora: Somnus abit leuibusque per aera pennis
>
> 121 deuolat in thalamos Agamemnonis: ille sopore
>
> 122 corpus inundatum leni prostratus habebat.

There is no delay, told as *Nec mora*. The god of sleep comes now, as *Somnus*. He goes off, told with *abit*. Something is light, described as *leuibusque*, though what is light is not yet said. The way goes through the air, given as *per aera*. What was described as *leuibusque* is wings, which close the line as *pennis*.

He flies down, told with *deuolat*. He goes into a bedchamber, given as *in thalamos*. It is Agamemnon's, named *Agamemnonis*. Then the king himself, pointed to as *ille*. He lies in slumber, spoken of as *sopore*, though what slumber it is is not yet said. His body comes next, named *corpus*. It is steeped, described as *inundatum*. What was spoken of as *sopore* is a gentle slumber, given as *leni*. He lies stretched out, pictured as *prostratus*. So he held his body, which closes the scene as *habebat*.

#### Line 123

> 123 Ad quem sic loquitur curarum operumque leuator:

Somnus goes to him, given as *Ad quem*. It is in this way, marked by *sic*. He speaks, told with *loquitur*. Cares come first, named *curarum*. Labors join them, as *operumque*. The speaker is the one who lightens them both, which closes the line as *leuator*.

#### Lines 124–128

> 124 "Rex Danaum, Atrida, uigila et mandata Tonantis
>
> 125 quae tibi iussa simul delatus ab aethere porto,
>
> 126 accipe: cum primum Titan se emerserit undis,
>
> 127 fortibus arma iube socios aptare lacertis
>
> 128 et petere Iliacos instructo milite campos."

He hails the king of the Danaans, called *Rex Danaum*. As the commentary says, the Danaans are the Greeks. He calls him son of Atreus, as *Atrida*. He bids him wake, told with *uigila*. More follows, joined by *et*. There are orders, named *mandata*. They are the Thunderer's, named *Tonantis*. As the commentary says, the Thunderer is Jupiter.

These orders return as *quae*. They are for the king, spoken of as *tibi*. They were commanded, given as *iussa*. It is all at once, marked by *simul*. Somnus has come down, described as *delatus*. He came from heaven, given as *ab aethere*. He brings them, told with *porto*.

The king is to receive them, told with *accipe*. Then a time is set, marked by *cum*. It is the first moment, marked by *primum*. Titan comes in, named *Titan*. He does something to himself, given as *se*. He will have risen out of something, told with *emerserit*. It is the waves, which close the line as *undis*.

Something is strong, described as *fortibus*, though what is strong is not yet said. Armor comes next, named *arma*. The king is to give an order, told with *iube*. It is for his comrades, called *socios*. They are to fit the armor, told with *aptare*. What was described as *fortibus* is their arms, which close the line as *lacertis*. More follows, joined by *et*. They are also to make for something, told with *petere*. It belongs to Ilion, described as *Iliacos*, though what it is is not yet said. The troops are drawn up, given as *instructo milite*. What was described as *Iliacos* is the plains, which end the speech as *campos*.
