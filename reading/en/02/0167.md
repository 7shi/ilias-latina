### 167–170 (*Iliad* 2.494–495, 2.509–510)

#### Lines 167–170

> 167 Peneleos princeps et bello Leitus acer,
>
> 168 Arcesilaus atrox Prothoenorque Cloniusque
>
> 169 Boeoti decies quinas egere carinas
>
> 170 et tumidos ualido pulsarunt remige fluctus.

The catalogue begins with Peneleus, named *Peneleos*. He is the leader, called *princeps*. More follows, joined by *et*. War comes in, named *bello*. Leitus comes next, named *Leitus*. He is fierce in war, described as *acer*. Then Arcesilaus, named *Arcesilaus*. He is grim, described as *atrox*. Prothoenor follows, as *Prothoenorque*. Clonius comes last, as *Cloniusque*. These are Boeotians, named *Boeoti*. The count is fifty, given as *decies quinas*, though fifty of what is not yet said. They brought them, told with *egere*. The fifty are ships, named by their keels as *carinas*.

More follows, joined by *et*. Something swells, described as *tumidos*, though what swells is not yet said. Something is strong, described as *ualido*. They beat at the swelling thing, told with *pulsarunt*. The strong ones are the rowers, named *remige*. What was described as *tumidos* is the waves, which close the line as *fluctus*.
