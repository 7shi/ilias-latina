### 167–170 (*Iliad* 2.494–495, 2.509–510)

#### Lines 167–170

> 167 Peneleos princeps et bello Leitus acer,
>
> 168 Arcesilaus atrox Prothoenorque Cloniusque
>
> 169 Boeoti decies quinas egere carinas
>
> 170 et tumidos ualido pulsarunt remige fluctus.

The first commander to step forward is Peneleus, appearing as *Peneleos*. He stands at the head, designated as *princeps*. A second figure is joined by *et*, with war as his proving ground, spoken of as *bello*. The warrior himself is Leitus, entering as *Leitus*. He shows himself fierce in combat, described as *acer*. Beside them stands Arcesilaus, identified as *Arcesilaus*. He is known as relentless, characterized as *atrox*. A fourth leader is added, named *Prothoenorque*. The fifth commander follows, recorded as *Cloniusque*. Together these men lead the Boeotians, called *Boeoti*. Their fleet numbered fifty ships, counted as *decies quinas*. They brought them across the water, told with *egere*. The ships are shown by their keels, given as *carinas*.

The voyage meets something swelling, joined by *et* as *tumidos*, though what is swelling is not yet said. Power is at work, appearing as *ualido*. They beat against the sea, told with *pulsarunt*. What was described as *ualido* is the strength of the rowers, named *remige*. What was called *tumidos* is the surging waves, closing the line as *fluctus*.
