### 111–112 (*Iliad* 2.1–2)

#### Lines 111–112

> 111 Nox erat et toto fulgebant sidera mundo
>
> 112 humanumque genus requies diuumque tenebat,

The section opens with night, which comes first as *Nox*. It was then, told with *erat*. More follows, joined by *et*. Something is whole, described as *toto*, though what is whole is not yet said. Things were shining, told with *fulgebant*. What shone were the stars, appearing as *sidera*. What was described as *toto* is the sky, which closes the line as *mundo*.

The next line begins with humankind, named *humanumque genus*. With it comes rest, named *requies*. The gods are there too, as *diuumque*. Rest held them both, which closes the line as *tenebat*.
