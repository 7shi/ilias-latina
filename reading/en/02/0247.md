### 247–248 (*Iliad* 2.862, 2.876)

#### Lines 247–248

> 247 florentes aetate uiri, quos Phorcus et ingens
>
> 248 Ascanius sequitur, simul et Iouis inclita proles

The two are flourishing, described as *florentes*. It is in their age, named *aetate*. They are men, named *uiri*. The two come back, as *quos*. Phorcus comes in, named *Phorcus*. More follows, joined by *et*. Someone is huge, described as *ingens*, though who it is is not yet said.

The huge one is Ascanius, named *Ascanius*. As the commentary says, this Ascanius is not the young son of Aeneas, but an adult warrior leading troops to Troy's defense. Phorcus and Ascanius follow them, told with *sequitur*. Someone comes with them, marked by *simul*. More follows, joined by *et*. Jupiter comes in, named *Iouis*. Someone is his famous son, which closes the line as *inclita proles*; who he is comes only in the next section.
