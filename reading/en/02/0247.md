### 247–248 (*Iliad* 2.862, 2.876)

#### Lines 247–248

> 247 florentes aetate uiri, quos Phorcus et ingens
>
> 248 Ascanius sequitur, simul et Iouis inclita proles

The two brothers from the previous verse are depicted in their prime, described as *florentes*. What they flourish in is their years, spoken of as *aetate*. They stand as valiant men, appearing as *uiri*. These warriors are followed by other allies, referred to as *quos*. The first of these new leaders enters as *Phorcus*. Another commander is joined by *et*, a warrior of mighty stature, described as *ingens*, though who is mighty is not yet said.

What was described as *ingens* is revealed at the start of the next line, named *Ascanius*. As the commentary says, this Ascanius is not the young son of Aeneas, but an adult warrior leading troops to Troy's defense. He follows behind the others, told with *sequitur*. Another ally marches along with them, marked by *simul*. He is joined by *et* as a son of the chief god, whose father is called *Iouis*. He is celebrated as his renowned child, closing the line as *inclita proles*, though who this son is is not yet said.
