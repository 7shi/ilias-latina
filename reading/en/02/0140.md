### 140–143 (*Iliad* 2.245–246, 2.265–266)

#### Line 140

> 140 correptum dictis sceptro percussit eburno.

Thersites is rebuked, described as *correptum*. It is done with words, named *dictis*. A scepter comes next, named *sceptro*, though what kind it is is not yet said. Ulysses struck him with it, told with *percussit*. The scepter is of ivory, which closes the line as *eburno*.

#### Line 141

> 141 Tum uero ardescit conceptis litibus ira:

Then something happens, marked by *Tum*. It happens indeed, marked by *uero*. Something blazes up, told with *ardescit*. Quarrels have arisen, given as *conceptis litibus*. What blazes up is anger, which closes the line as *ira*.

#### Lines 142–143

> 142 uix telis caruere manus, ad sidera clamor
>
> 143 tollitur et cunctos pugnandi corripit ardor.

Something barely happened, marked by *uix*. It concerns weapons, named *telis*. Something was kept from them, told with *caruere*. What was kept from the weapons were hands, named *manus*. Now the stars come in, given as *ad sidera*, though what reaches them is not yet said. It is a shout, which closes the line as *clamor*.

The shout rises, told with *tollitur*. More follows, joined by *et*. It is all of them, named *cunctos*. Fighting comes next, given as *pugnandi*. Something seizes them, told with *corripit*. What seizes them is eagerness, which ends the scene as *ardor*.
