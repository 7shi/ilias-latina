### 140–143 (*Iliad* 2.245–246, 2.265–266)

#### Line 140

> 140 correptum dictis sceptro percussit eburno.

Thersites is seized and sharply checked, described as *correptum*. The rebuke begins with spoken words, delivered *dictis*. A physical blow follows with a royal staff, named *sceptro*, though what it is made of is not yet said. Ulysses strikes him with it, told with *percussit*. What was named *sceptro* is revealed as carved from ivory, closing the line as *eburno*.

#### Line 141

> 141 Tum uero ardescit conceptis litibus ira:

At that moment the reaction begins, marked by *Tum*. The feeling rises in earnest, emphasized with *uero*. It blazes up fiercely, told with *ardescit*. Disputes break out across the gathering, named *conceptis litibus*. The passion behind these quarrels is rage, closing the line as *ira*.

#### Lines 142–143

> 142 uix telis caruere manus, ad sidera clamor
>
> 143 tollitur et cunctos pugnandi corripit ardor.

Restraint is almost lost, described with *uix*. Men reach toward their weapons, named *telis*. They barely keep themselves from using them, told with *caruere*. The hands that hold back close the clause as *manus*. A sound rises toward the heavens, directed *ad sidera*. The roar that fills the air is an outcry, closing the line as *clamor*.

It is lifted high into the sky, reported with *tollitur*. The surge spreads to every soldier, joined by *et* and encompassing *cunctos*. The desire is for battle, defined by *pugnandi*. A sudden passion takes hold of them, expressed by *corripit*. The burning heat that grips them ends the scene as *ardor*.
