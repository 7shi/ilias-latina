### 152–153 (*Iliad* 2.322, 2.328–330)

#### Lines 152–153

> 152 in decimo labor est, Calchas quem dixerat, anno,
>
> 153 quo caderet Danaum uictricibus Ilion armis."

It is in the tenth, given as *in decimo*, though the tenth of what is not yet said. The toil comes then, given as *labor est*. Calchas comes in, named *Calchas*. The toil returns, as *quem*. He had said it, told with *dixerat*. What was given as *in decimo* is the year, which closes the line as *anno*.

That year returns, as *quo*. Something would fall, told with *caderet*. The Danaans come in, named *Danaum*. Something is victorious, described as *uictricibus*, though what it is is not yet said. What would fall is Ilion, named *Ilion*. As the commentary says, Ilion is Troy. What was described as *uictricibus* is arms, which close the speech as *armis*.
