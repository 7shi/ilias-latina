### 144–146 (*Iliad* 2.278–284, 2.299–302)

#### Lines 144–146

> 144 Tandem sollertis prudentia Nestoris aeuo
>
> 145 compressam miti sedauit pectore turbam
>
> 146 admonuitque duces dictis responsa recordans

At last something changes, marked by *Tandem*. Skill and resource enter the scene, displayed as *sollertis*. It takes the form of wisdom, named *prudentia*. This wisdom belongs to Nestor, arriving as *Nestoris*. His judgment has been ripened by long years, closing the line as *aeuo*. The raging assembly is checked, described as *compressam*, though what is quieted is not yet said. His approach is gentle, characterized as *miti*. He calms the strife, told with *sedauit*. The calm comes from his gentle spirit, appearing as *pectore*. What was described as *compressam* is revealed as the turbulent crowd, closing the line as *turbam*.

He also warns the men who lead, instructed with *admonuitque*. Those he counsels are the chiefs, named *duces*. He addresses them with spoken words, delivered *dictis*. He recalls divine oracles, spoken of as *responsa*. His mind brings them back to memory, closing the scene as *recordans*.
