### 223–225 (*Iliad* 2.786–787, 2.796–801, 2.807–808)

#### Lines 223–224

> 223 cum pater ad Priamum mittit Saturnius Irim,
>
> 224 quae doceat fortes uenisse ad bella Pelasgos.

Then a moment comes, marked by *cum*. A father comes in, named *pater*, though who he is is not yet said. The way leads to Priam, given as *ad Priamum*. The father sends, told with *mittit*. He is Saturn's son, described as *Saturnius*. As the commentary says, Saturn's son is Jupiter. The one sent is Iris, who closes the line as *Irim*.

Iris comes back, as *quae*. She is to tell him, told with *doceat*. Someone is brave, described as *fortes*, though who it is is not yet said. They have come, told with *uenisse*. It is for war, given as *ad bella*. The brave ones are the Pelasgians, who close the line as *Pelasgos*. As the commentary says, the Pelasgians are the Greeks.

#### Line 225

> 225 Nec mora: continuo iussu capit arma parentis

There is no delay, told as *Nec mora*. It is at once, marked by *continuo*. A command comes in, named *iussu*, though whose it is is not yet said. Someone takes something, told with *capit*; who he is comes only in the next section. What he takes is arms, named *arma*. The command was his father's, which closes the line as *parentis*.
