### 147–151 (*Iliad* 2.303–308, 2.311–317)

#### Lines 147–150

> 147 temporis illius, quo uisus in Aulide serpens
>
> 148 consumpsit uolucrum bis quattuor arbore fetus
>
> 149 atque ipsam inualido pugnantem corpore contra
>
> 150 addidit extremo natorum funere matrem.

The prophecies are of a time, named *temporis illius*. Something happened in it, given as *quo*. Something was seen, told with *uisus*. It was at Aulis, given as *in Aulide*. As the commentary says, Aulis is the Greek port where the fleet gathered before sailing against Troy. What was seen is a serpent, which closes the line as *serpens*.

It devoured, told with *consumpsit*. Birds come in, named *uolucrum*. There were twice four, counted as *bis quattuor*. It was in a tree, named *arbore*. Their young are what it devoured, which close the line as *fetus*.

More follows, joined by *atque*. Someone comes in herself, as *ipsam*, though who she is is not yet said. Something is feeble, described as *inualido*. She is fighting, described as *pugnantem*. The feeble thing is her body, named *corpore*. Her fight goes against the serpent, given as *contra*.

The serpent added her, told with *addidit*. It comes last, described as *extremo*. Her young come in, named *natorum*. Their death is the last, named *funere*. The one who came in as *ipsam* is the mother, who closes the omen as *matrem*.

#### Line 151

> 151 Tunc "sic deinde" senex "moneo remoneboque, Achiui:

Then comes a moment, marked by *Tunc*. Words begin in this way, marked by *sic*. They lead on, marked by *deinde*. The speaker is the old man, named *senex*. He counsels them, told with *moneo*. He will counsel them again, told with *remoneboque*. Those he counsels are the Achaeans, addressed as *Achiui*. As the commentary says, the Achaeans are the Greeks.
