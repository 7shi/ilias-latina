### 147–151 (*Iliad* 2.303–308, 2.311–317)

#### Lines 147–150

> 147 temporis illius, quo uisus in Aulide serpens
>
> 148 consumpsit uolucrum bis quattuor arbore fetus
>
> 149 atque ipsam inualido pugnantem corpore contra
>
> 150 addidit extremo natorum funere matrem.

He looks back to a particular moment, named *temporis illius*. The time is marked when the sign occurred, opened with *quo*. A marvel was seen, told with *uisus*. It appeared at the port of departure, situated *in Aulide*. The creature that showed itself was a snake, closing the line as *serpens*.

It devoured its prey, recorded with *consumpsit*. The victims were birds, spoken of as *uolucrum*, though which ones were taken is not yet said. Their number was eight, reckoned as *bis quattuor*. The nest rested high above, set in an *arbore*. What was spoken of as *uolucrum* is revealed as the nestlings, closing the line as *fetus*.

The mother herself is joined to their fate, connected by *atque* and pointed to as *ipsam*, though who she is is not yet said. Her resistance was frail, described as *inualido*. She struggled to defend them, portrayed as *pugnantem*. She fought with her weak frame, named *corpore*. She threw herself against the attacker, closing the line with *contra*.

The serpent joined her to the slaughter, reported with *addidit*. Her death came last of all, presented as *extremo*. She shared the doom of her brood, spoken of as *natorum*. She perished in the same ruin, arriving as *funere*. What was pointed to as *ipsam* is revealed as the mother bird, closing the omen as *matrem*.

#### Line 151

> 151 Tunc "sic deinde" senex "moneo remoneboque, Achiui:

The story returns to the speaker, marked by *Tunc*. He begins his counsel in this manner, declared as *sic*. He looks toward what must follow, urged with *deinde*. The speaker who addresses them is the elder, named *senex*. He offers his guidance now, delivered with *moneo*. He promises to repeat the warning hereafter, affirmed as *remoneboque*. His call is directed to the army, addressed as *Achiui*. As the commentary says, the Achaeans are the Greeks.
