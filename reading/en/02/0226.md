### 226–235 (*Iliad* 2.816–818, 2.809–810)

#### Lines 226–227

> 226 Priamides Hector totamque in proelia pubem
>
> 227 festinare iubet portisque agit agmen apertis.

The one who takes up arms is the son of Priam, named *Priamides*. His name is Hector, given at once as *Hector*. Something is whole, described as *totamque*, though what is whole is not yet said. It is for battle, given as *in proelia*. What was described as *totamque* is the young men, who close the line as *pubem*.

They are to hurry, told with *festinare*. He orders it, told with *iubet*. Gates come next, named *portisque*. He drives something, told with *agit*. What he drives is the column, named *agmen*. The gates are open, which closes the line as *apertis*.

#### Lines 228–232

> 228 Cui fulgens auro cassis iuuenile tegebat
>
> 229 omni parte caput, munibat pectora thorax
>
> 230 et clipeus laeuam, dextram decorauerat hasta
>
> 231 ornabatque latus mucro; simul alta nitentes
>
> 232 crura tegunt ocreae, quales decet Hectoris esse.

Now Hector comes back, as *Cui*. Something gleams, described as *fulgens*. It gleams with gold, named *auro*. It is a helmet, named *cassis*. Something is youthful, described as *iuuenile*, though what it is is not yet said. The helmet covered it, told with *tegebat*. It was on every side, given as *omni parte*. What was described as *iuuenile* is his head, named *caput*. Something guarded him, told with *munibat*. What it guarded was his chest, named *pectora*. The guard is a breastplate, which closes the line as *thorax*.

More follows, joined by *et*. A shield comes next, named *clipeus*. It guarded his left hand, named *laeuam*. His right hand comes next, named *dextram*. It had been graced, told with *decorauerat*. A spear graced it, which closes the line as *hasta*.

Something adorned him as well, told with *ornabatque*. It was at his side, named *latus*. A sword adorned it, named *mucro*. At the same time there is more, marked by *simul*. Something is long, described as *alta*, though what is long is not yet said. Something gleams, described as *nitentes*. What was described as *alta* is his legs, named *crura*. Something covers them, told with *tegunt*. What was described as *nitentes* is greaves, named *ocreae*. They are of a kind, given as *quales*. It is a kind that befits, told with *decet*. It befits Hector, named *Hectoris*. They are to be so, which closes the line as *esse*.

#### Lines 233–235

> 233 Hunc sequitur forma melior, tunc fortis in armis,
>
> 234 belli causa Paris, patriae funesta ruina,
>
> 235 Deiphobusque Helenusque simul fortisque Polites

Hector comes back, as *Hunc*. Someone follows him, told with *sequitur*. He is fairer in looks, given as *forma melior*, though who he is is not yet said. Then comes another side of him, marked by *tunc*. He is brave, described as *fortis*. It is in arms, given as *in armis*. He is the cause of the war, named *belli causa*. The fair one is Paris, named *Paris*. Something belongs to his country, named *patriae*, though what it is is not yet said. It is deadly ruin, which closes the line as *funesta ruina*.

Deiphobus comes next, as *Deiphobusque*. Then Helenus, as *Helenusque*. They come together, marked by *simul*. Someone brave is added, described as *fortisque*. He is Polites, who closes the line as *Polites*.
