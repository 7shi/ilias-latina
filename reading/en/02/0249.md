### 249–251 (*Iliad* 2.876)

#### Line 249

> 249 Sarpedon claraque satus tellure Coroebus.

The warrior celebrated as the son of Jupiter in the preceding verse is now revealed, entering as *Sarpedon*. Another ally is also praised for what is illustrious, joined as *claraque*, though what is renowned is not yet said. He was born of noble stock, spoken of as *satus*. What was described as *claraque* is revealed as his homeland, named *tellure*. The warrior born of that land comes forward, closing the line as *Coroebus*.

#### Lines 250–251

> 250 His se defendit ducibus Neptunia Troia
>
> 251 uicissetque dolos Danaum, ni fata fuissent.

The whole company of commanders is gathered together, referred to with *His*. The city acts to protect itself, pointing to itself as *se*. It wards off the enemy, told with *defendit*. It relies upon these chieftains, spoken of as *ducibus*. The citadel founded by Neptune is named, closing the line as *Neptunia Troia*.

She would also have prevailed over her enemies, declared with *uicissetque*. What she would have overcome was their deceit, named *dolos*. That cunning belonged to the Greeks, spoken of as *Danaum*. Yet an exception prevented this victory, marked by *ni*. What stood in the way was destiny, appearing as *fata*. Had those decrees not intervened, the verse concludes with *fuissent*. With this tribute to Troy's defense, the second book of the poem comes to an end.
