### 249–251 (*Iliad* 2.876)

#### Line 249

> 249 Sarpedon claraque satus tellure Coroebus.

The famous son of Jupiter is Sarpedon, named *Sarpedon*. Something is renowned, described as *claraque*, though what it is is not yet said. Someone is born of it, described as *satus*. What was described as *claraque* is a land, named *tellure*. The one born there is Coroebus, who closes the line as *Coroebus*.

#### Lines 250–251

> 250 His se defendit ducibus Neptunia Troia
>
> 251 uicissetque dolos Danaum, ni fata fuissent.

The ones just named come back, pointed to as *His*. Something defends itself, told with *se defendit*. The ones pointed to as *His* are leaders, named *ducibus*. What defends itself is Neptune's Troy, which closes the line as *Neptunia Troia*.

It would also have overcome something, told with *uicissetque*. What it would have overcome is deceit, named *dolos*. The deceit is the Danaans', named *Danaum*. But there is a condition, marked by *ni*. It concerns the fates, named *fata*. The condition is that they had not been there, and it ends the second book as *fuissent*.
