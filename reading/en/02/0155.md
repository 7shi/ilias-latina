### 155–160 (*Iliad* 2.398, 2.381–384, 2.442–444, 2.465)

#### Lines 155–156

> 155 conciliumque simul dimittitur. Arma parari
>
> 156 dux iubet atque animos aptare et pectora pugnae.

An assembly comes next, named *conciliumque*. It is all at once, marked by *simul*. The assembly is dismissed, told with *dimittitur*. Weapons follow, named *Arma*. They are to be prepared, told with *parari*. The leader comes in, named *dux*. He orders it, told with *iubet*. More follows, joined by *atque*. Minds come next, named *animos*. They are to be fitted, told with *aptare*. Another joins them, marked by *et*. It is hearts, named *pectora*. They are fitted for battle, which closes the line as *pugnae*.

#### Lines 157–160

> 157 Postera lux tacitas ut primum dispulit umbras
>
> 158 et nitidum Titan radiis caput extulit undis,
>
> 159 protinus armari socios iubet acer Atrides
>
> 160 et petere Iliacos instructo milite campos.

The next day's light comes, given as *Postera lux*. Something is silent, described as *tacitas*. It is the first moment, marked by *ut primum*. The light drove it away, told with *dispulit*. What was described as *tacitas* is the shadows, named *umbras*. More follows, joined by *et*. Something shines, described as *nitidum*, though what shines is not yet said. Titan comes in, named *Titan*. There are rays, named *radiis*. What was described as *nitidum* is his head, named *caput*. He lifted it, told with *extulit*. It came up from the waves, which close the line as *undis*.

It happens at once, marked by *protinus*. The men are to arm, told with *armari*. They are his comrades, called *socios*. Someone orders it, told with *iubet*. He is the fierce son of Atreus, appearing as *acer Atrides*. More follows, joined by *et*. They are to make for something, told with *petere*. Something is Ilian, described as *Iliacos*, though what it is is not yet said. The troops are drawn up, given as *instructo milite*. What was described as *Iliacos* is the plains, which close the line as *campos*.
