### 155–160 (*Iliad* 2.398, 2.381–384, 2.442–444, 2.465)

#### Lines 155–156

> 155 conciliumque simul dimittitur. Arma parari
>
> 156 dux iubet atque animos aptare et pectora pugnae.

With the speech accepted, an assembly comes next, named *conciliumque*. At that very moment something occurs, marked by *simul*. The gathering is dismissed, told with *dimittitur*. Weapons come next, spoken of as *Arma*. They are to be made ready, expressed by *parari*. The one behind this is the leader, appearing as *dux*. He lays down the order with *iubet*. His command reaches further, joined by *atque* to take in their spirits, which enter as *animos*. These they are to fit for what is coming, instructed with *aptare*. Their hearts too are added by *et*, standing as *pectora*. All this preparation is directed toward battle, closing the line as *pugnae*.

#### Lines 157–160

> 157 Postera lux tacitas ut primum dispulit umbras
>
> 158 et nitidum Titan radiis caput extulit undis,
>
> 159 protinus armari socios iubet acer Atrides
>
> 160 et petere Iliacos instructo milite campos.

The light of the next day arrives, appearing as *Postera lux*. Something silent comes into view, described as *tacitas*, though what is silent is not yet said. The moment of the action is marked by *ut primum*. The light scatters what lay before it, told with *dispulit*. What was described as *tacitas* is now revealed as the darkness of the shadows, named *umbras*. Something gleaming is joined by *et*, described as *nitidum*, though what gleams is not yet said. Titan comes into view under his own name, *Titan*. He shines with beams, spoken of as *radiis*. What was described as *nitidum* is his head, which appears as *caput*. He raises it up, told with *extulit*. He lifts it from the waves, closing the verse as *undis*.

Without delay, action follows at once, marked by *protinus*. Men are to be armed, expressed by *armari*. Those who must arm are his comrades, called *socios*. The command is given, instructed with *iubet*. The one commanding them is fierce, the son of Atreus, appearing as *acer Atrides*. With *et*, they are directed to advance, told with *petere*. The ground they are to head for is Ilian, named *Iliacos*. The troops march in battle order, designated as *instructo milite*. Their destination is the open plains, closing the line as *campos*.
