### 171–173 (*Iliad* 2.569, 2.579–580, 2.576)

#### Lines 171–173

> 171 Inde Mycenaeis Agamemnon moenibus ortus,
>
> 172 quem sibi bellatrix delegit Graecia regem,
>
> 173 centum egit plenas armato milite puppes;

The catalogue goes on, marked by *Inde*. Something belongs to Mycenae, described as *Mycenaeis*, though what it is is not yet said. Agamemnon comes in, named *Agamemnon*. What was of Mycenae is its walls, named *moenibus*. He was born there, told with *ortus*.

He comes back as *quem*. Someone acts for herself, given as *sibi*. She is warlike, described as *bellatrix*, though who she is is not yet said. She chose him, told with *delegit*. The warlike one is Greece, named *Graecia*. What she chose him as is king, which closes the line as *regem*.

The count is a hundred, given as *centum*. He brought them, told with *egit*. They were full, described as *plenas*. Armed troops come next, given as *armato milite*. The hundred are ships, named by their sterns as *puppes*.
