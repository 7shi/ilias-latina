### 176–178 (*Iliad* 2.601–602)

#### Lines 176–178

> 176 quos iuxta fidus sollerti pectore Nestor
>
> 177 consilioque potens gemina cum prole suorum
>
> 178 it ter tricenis munitus in arma carinis.

The ones already named come back, as *quos*. Someone is beside them, given as *iuxta*. He is trusty, described as *fidus*. His mind is keen, given as *sollerti pectore*. He is Nestor, named *Nestor*. He has counsel too, named *consilioque*. He is powerful in it, described as *potens*. Something is twofold, described as *gemina*, though what it is is not yet said. What was described as *gemina* is offspring, two sons who come with him as *cum prole*. They are of his own, which closes the line as *suorum*.

He goes, told with *it*. The count is three times thirty, given as *ter tricenis*, though thirty of what is not yet said. He is backed by them, described as *munitus*. It is for war, given as *in arma*. The ninety are ships, named by their keels as *carinis*.
