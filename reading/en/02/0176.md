### 176–178 (*Iliad* 2.601–602)

#### Lines 176–178

> 176 quos iuxta fidus sollerti pectore Nestor
>
> 177 consilioque potens gemina cum prole suorum
>
> 178 it ter tricenis munitus in arma carinis.

The leaders just named are referred to again as *quos*. Next to them stands another warrior, positioned with *iuxta*. He is known as dependable, characterized as *fidus*. His wisdom is keen, shown with *sollerti pectore*. The counsellor himself is Nestor, entering as *Nestor*. His strength lies also in guidance, joined as *consilioque*. He is powerful in persuasion, appearing as *potens*. He brings two companions, described as *gemina*, though who they are is not yet said. What was described as *gemina* is his offspring with him, told with *cum prole*. They belong to his own family, closing the line as *suorum*.

He marches forth, told with *it*. His contingent numbers ninety, counted as *ter tricenis*, though what is numbered is not yet said. He is backed by this strength, supported with *munitus*. His march is directed toward war, spoken of as *in arma*. What was counted as *ter tricenis* is revealed as the ships, shown by their keels as *carinis*.
