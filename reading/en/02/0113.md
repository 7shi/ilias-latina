### 113–119 (*Iliad* 2.3–9, 2.19, 2.10–13)

#### Line 113

> 113 cum pater omnipotens Somnum uocat atque ita fatur:

In this stillness a moment arrives, marked by *cum*. The almighty father appears, described as *pater omnipotens*. His purpose turns to sleep, named *Somnum*. He calls upon him, told with *uocat*. He proceeds in this manner, joined by *atque* and indicated by *ita*. He speaks, given as *fatur*.

#### Line 114

> 114 "Vade age per tenues auras, lenissime diuum,

The instruction opens with a command to go, delivered as *Vade*. He is urged forward without delay, pressed with *age*. His way is directed through something thin and fine, told as *per tenues*. This path lies through the breezes, appearing as *auras*. He is greeted as the gentlest of beings, addressed as *lenissime*. The company among whom he holds this place is that of the gods, closing the line as *diuum*.

#### Line 115

> 115 Argolicique ducis celeri pete castra uolatu

The destination belongs also to an Argive leader, spoken of as *Argolicique ducis*. As the commentary says, this Argive leader is Agamemnon. The journey is to be swift, described as *celeri*, though how he travels is not yet said. He is told to seek his goal, commanded with *pete*. That goal is an encampment, appearing as *castra*. What was described as *celeri* is revealed as flight through the air, closing the line as *uolatu*.

#### Lines 116–119

> 116 dumque tuo premitur sopitus pondere dulci,
>
> 117 haec illi mandata refer: cum crastina primum
>
> 118 extulerit Titana dies noctemque fugarit,
>
> 119 cogat in arma uiros incautumque occupet hostem."

A condition of time is also set, introduced by *dumque*. Something belonging to the god is invoked, described as *tuo*, though what it is is not yet said. The leader is weighed down, told with *premitur*. He lies overcome with sleep, portrayed as *sopitus*. What was described as *tuo* is the god's gentle burden, named *pondere dulci*.

The message to be delivered is pointed out as *haec*. It is directed to the sleeping leader, given to him as *illi*. The charge itself consists of commands, named *mandata*. Somnus is instructed to deliver them, commanded with *refer*. A new condition of time follows, opening with *cum*. The time belongs to the coming morning, described as *crastina*, though what brings it is not yet said. The very moment it begins is marked by *primum*.

Something is to raise the light, told with *extulerit*. The figure being raised is Titan, appearing as *Titana*. As the commentary says, Titan is the sun. What was described as *crastina* is revealed as the daylight itself, arriving as *dies*. Darkness is also driven back, named *noctemque*. Day scatters it, banished with *fugarit*.

The leader is to assemble his forces, instructed with *cogat*. He must summon them for battle, called *in arma*. The ones summoned to fight are his men, spoken of as *uiros*. The opposing force is also caught off guard, described as *incautumque*. He is to fall upon them by surprise, told with *occupet*. Those taken unawares are the enemy, closing the command as *hostem*.
