### 113–119 (*Iliad* 2.3–9, 2.19, 2.10–13)

#### Line 113

> 113 cum pater omnipotens Somnum uocat atque ita fatur:

Then a moment comes, marked by *cum*. A father appears, the almighty one, described as *pater omnipotens*. As the commentary says, the almighty father is Jupiter. He turns to the god of sleep, named *Somnum*. He calls him, told with *uocat*. One thing more follows, joined by *atque*. It is done in this way, marked by *ita*. He speaks, told with *fatur*.

#### Line 114

> 114 "Vade age per tenues auras, lenissime diuum,

The father's words begin with a command to go, given as *Vade*. He presses at once, adding *age*. The way is through something thin, given as *per tenues*, though what is thin is not yet said. It is the air, appearing as *auras*. Somnus is addressed as the gentlest, called *lenissime*. He is the gentlest of the gods, who close the line as *diuum*.

#### Line 115

> 115 Argolicique ducis celeri pete castra uolatu

Then comes an Argive leader, spoken of as *Argolicique ducis*. As the commentary says, the Argive leader is Agamemnon. Somnus is to be swift, described as *celeri*, though in what he is swift is not yet said. He is to seek something, told with *pete*. What he seeks is the leader's camp, appearing as *castra*. What was described as *celeri* is his flight, which closes the line as *uolatu*.

#### Lines 116–119

> 116 dumque tuo premitur sopitus pondere dulci,
>
> 117 haec illi mandata refer: cum crastina primum
>
> 118 extulerit Titana dies noctemque fugarit,
>
> 119 cogat in arma uiros incautumque occupet hostem."

A time is set, given as *dumque*. Something is Somnus' own, described as *tuo*, though what it is is not yet said. The leader is pressed down, told with *premitur*. He is asleep, described as *sopitus*. What was Somnus' own is his weight, a sweet one, named *pondere dulci*.

What follows is pointed to as *haec*. It is for the sleeper, spoken of as *illi*. These are orders, named *mandata*. Somnus is to carry them, told with *refer*. Then another time is set, marked by *cum*. It belongs to tomorrow, described as *crastina*, though what of tomorrow is not yet said. It is to be the first moment, marked by *primum*.

Something will have been lifted up, told with *extulerit*. What is lifted is Titan, appearing as *Titana*. As the commentary says, Titan is the sun. What was described as *crastina* is the day that lifts him, which comes as *dies*. Night comes next, named *noctemque*. The day will have driven it away, told with *fugarit*.

Then the leader is to gather, told with *cogat*. The gathering is to arms, given as *in arma*. Those he gathers are men, named *uiros*. Someone is unwary, described as *incautumque*. The leader is to take him by surprise, told with *occupet*. The unwary one is the enemy, who closes the command as *hostem*.
