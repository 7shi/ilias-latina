### 179–181 (*Iliad* 2.517, 2.524)

#### Lines 179–181

> 179 At Schedius uirtute potens et Epistrophus ingens,
>
> 180 gloria Myrmidonum, saeui duo robora belli,
>
> 181 longa quaterdenis pulsarunt aequora proris

A contrast is drawn with *At*. The first warrior to enter is Schedius, recorded as *Schedius*. He is formidable in courage, celebrated as *uirtute potens*. His brother is joined by *et*, named *Epistrophus*. He is a warrior of immense size, characterized as *ingens*. They stand as a source of pride, spoken of as *gloria*. They bring renown to their people, appearing as *Myrmidonum*. As the commentary says, the Myrmidons are the Greeks. They are matched against something fierce, described as *saeui*, though what is fierce is not yet said. Together they stand as two bulwarks, designated as *duo robora*. What was described as *saeui* is revealed as the fighting, closing the line as *belli*.

The expanse before them stretches wide, described as *longa*, though what is wide is not yet said. The count reaches forty, given as *quaterdenis*. They strike against the waters, told with *pulsarunt*. What was described as *longa* is the open sea, entering as *aequora*. They cross it with their prows, closing the line as *proris*.
