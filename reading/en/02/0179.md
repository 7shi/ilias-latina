### 179–181 (*Iliad* 2.517, 2.524)

#### Lines 179–181

> 179 At Schedius uirtute potens et Epistrophus ingens,
>
> 180 gloria Myrmidonum, saeui duo robora belli,
>
> 181 longa quaterdenis pulsarunt aequora proris

The catalogue turns, marked by *At*. Schedius comes in, named *Schedius*. He is strong in valor, given as *uirtute potens*. More follows, joined by *et*. Epistrophus comes next, named *Epistrophus*. He is huge, described as *ingens*.

The two are a glory, named *gloria*. It belongs to the Myrmidons, named *Myrmidonum*. As the commentary says, Homer makes these two commanders of the Phocians, not of Achilles' people, the Myrmidons. Something is savage, described as *saeui*, though what is savage is not yet said. They are two bulwarks, named *duo robora*. What was described as *saeui* is war, which closes the line as *belli*.

Something is long, described as *longa*, though what is long is not yet said. The count is forty, given as *quaterdenis*. They beat at the long thing, told with *pulsarunt*. What was described as *longa* is the sea, named *aequora*. They beat it with prows, which close the line as *proris*.
