### 185–186 (*Iliad* 2.567–568)

#### Lines 185–186

> 185 Tydides ualido pulsarunt remige pontum:
>
> 186 bis quadragenas onerarunt milite puppes;

The one brave in battle is the son of Tydeus, named *Tydides*. As the commentary says, Tydides is Diomedes. Something is strong, described as *ualido*. They beat at something, told with *pulsarunt*. The strong thing is the rowing, named *remige*. What they beat is the sea, which closes the line as *pontum*.

The count is twice forty, given as *bis quadragenas*, though eighty of what is not yet said. They loaded them, told with *onerarunt*. It was with soldiers, named *milite*. The eighty are ships, named by their sterns as *puppes*.
