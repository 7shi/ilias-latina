### 130–131 (*Iliad* 2.48–53)

#### Line 130

> 130 Interea lucem terris dedit ignea lampas.

Time moves forward in the camp, marked by *Interea*. A glow spreads across the scene, appearing as *lucem*. It is shed upon the earth below, named *terris*. The morning bestowed this gift, told with *dedit*. The source that grants it is the fiery lamp, closing the line as *ignea lampas*.

#### Line 131

> 131 Conuocat attonitus iussis Pelopeius heros

A summons is made, told with *Conuocat*, though whom he calls together is not yet said. The leader is struck with awe, described as *attonitus*. The cause of this wonder is the orders he received, spoken of as *iussis*. The one who acts is the hero sprung from Pelops, closing the line as *Pelopeius heros*. As the commentary says, this Pelopeian hero is Agamemnon.
