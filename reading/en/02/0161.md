### 161–166 (*Iliad* 2.484, 2.487, 2.485, 2.493)

#### Lines 161–163

> 161 Vos mihi nunc, Musae - quid enim non ordine nostis? -,
>
> 162 nomina clara ducum clarosque referte parentes
>
> 163 et dulces patrias: nam sunt haec munera uestra.

The poet turns to others, addressing them as *Vos*. It is for himself, spoken of as *mihi*. It is now, marked by *nunc*. Those addressed are the Muses, named *Musae*. Something is asked, beginning with *quid*. It gives the reason, marked by *enim*. A denial follows, marked by *non*. It is about order, named *ordine*. The Muses know it, told with *nostis*.

Names come next, famous ones, named *nomina clara*. They belong to leaders, named *ducum*. Something is renowned as well, described as *clarosque*, though what it is is not yet said. The Muses are to recount them, told with *referte*. What was described as *clarosque* is the parents, named *parentes*. More follows, joined by *et*. Dear homelands come next, named *dulces patrias*. A reason follows, marked by *nam*. Something is said of these, given as *sunt haec*. They are tasks, named *munera*. The tasks are the Muses' own, which closes the line as *uestra*.

#### Lines 164–166

> 164 Dicamus quot quisque rates ad Pergama duxit
>
> 165 et coeptum peragamus opus, sitque auctor Apollo
>
> 166 aspiretque libens operi per singula nostro.

The poet would tell it with them, told with *Dicamus*. It is a count, marked by *quot*, though a count of what is not yet said. Each man comes in, named *quisque*. What is counted is ships, named *rates*. They go to Pergama, given as *ad Pergama*. As the commentary says, Pergama is another name for Troy, especially its citadel. Each brought them, told with *duxit*.

More follows, joined by *et*. Something has been begun, described as *coeptum*, though what it is is not yet said. The poet would finish it with them, told with *peragamus*. What was described as *coeptum* is the work, named *opus*. A wish comes next, given as *sitque*. Someone is to inspire it, called *auctor*. That one is the god who closes the line, *Apollo*.

He is to favor it, told with *aspiretque*. He is to be willing, described as *libens*. It is for the work, named *operi*. It is in every detail, given as *per singula*. The work is the poet's own, which closes the line as *nostro*.
