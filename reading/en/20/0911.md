### 911–914 (*Iliad* 21.233–240)

#### Lines 911–914

> 911 At Venus et Phrygiae gentis tutator Apollo
>
> 912 cogunt in Danaos Xanthi consurgere fluctus,
>
> 913 ut fera terribili miscentem proelia dextra
>
> 914 obruat Aeaciden; qui protinus undique totis

Something is set against this, marked by *At*. Venus comes in, called *Venus*. More is added, marked by *et*. The Phrygian people come in, given as *Phrygiae gentis*. As the commentary says, the Phrygian people are the Trojans. A protector comes in, named *tutator*. Apollo comes in, who closes the line as *Apollo*.

They force something, told with *cogunt*. The Danaans come in, given as *in Danaos*. As the commentary says, the Danaans are the Greeks. Xanthus comes in, called *Xanthi*. As the commentary says, Xanthus is the river of the Trojan plain. Rising comes next, told with *consurgere*. What they force to rise is waves, which close the line as *fluctus*.

A purpose is set, marked by *ut*. Something is savage, described as *fera*, though what it is is not yet said. Something is fearsome, described as *terribili*, though what it is is not yet said. Someone is stirring up something, described as *miscentem*. What is savage is battles, named *proelia*. What is fearsome is his right hand, which closes the line as *dextra*.

It may overwhelm someone, told with *obruat*. Aeacides comes in, called *Aeaciden*. As the commentary says, Aeacides is Achilles, grandson of Aeacus. Xanthus comes back, as *qui*. A time is set, marked by *protinus*. Every side comes in, marked by *undique*. Something is whole, described as *totis*, though what it is is not yet said. What is whole comes only in the next section.
