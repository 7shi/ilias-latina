### 915–919 (*Iliad* 21.240–242, 21.268–271)

#### Lines 915–919

> 915 exspatiatur aquis et uasto gurgite praeceps
>
> 916 uoluitur atque uirum torrentibus impedit undis
>
> 917 praetardatque gradus. Ille omni corpore saeuas
>
> 918 contra pugnat aquas aduersaque flumina rumpit
>
> 919 et modo disiectos umeris modo pectore uasto

It spreads abroad, told with *exspatiatur*. What is whole is waters, named *aquis*. More is added, marked by *et*. A vast whirl comes in, given as *uasto gurgite*. It is headlong, which closes the line as *praeceps*.

It rolls on, told with *uoluitur*. More is added, marked by *atque*. The man comes in, named *uirum*. Something is torrential, described as *torrentibus*, though what it is is not yet said. It hampers him, told with *impedit*. What is torrential is waves, which close the line as *undis*.

It slows something as well, told with *praetardatque*. What it slows is steps, named *gradus*. Someone comes in, as *Ille*. His whole body comes in, given as *omni corpore*. Something is savage, described as *saeuas*, though what it is is not yet said.

Opposition comes in, marked by *contra*. Achilles fights, told with *pugnat*. What is savage is waters, named *aquas*. Opposing currents come in as well, given as *aduersaque flumina*. He breaks through them, which closes the line as *rumpit*.

More is added, marked by *et*. A time is set, marked by *modo*. Some are scattered, described as *disiectos*, though what they are is not yet said. Shoulders come in, named *umeris*. Another time is set, marked by *modo*. A broad chest comes in, which closes the line as *pectore uasto*. What is scattered comes only in the next section.
