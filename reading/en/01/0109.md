### 109–110 (*Iliad* 1.601–604, 1.606–611)

#### Lines 109–110

> 109 et dapibus diui curant sua corpora largis;
>
> 110 inde petunt thalamos iucundaque dona quietis.

More follows, joined by *et*. A feast comes in, appearing as *dapibus*, though what kind of feast it is is not yet said. Those who feast are the gods, spoken of as *diui*. They refresh something, told with *curant*. What they refresh is their own bodies, designated as *sua corpora*. The feast is lavish, closing the line as *largis*.

Then they go on, marked by *inde*. They seek something, given as *petunt*. What they seek are their chambers, named *thalamos*. They also seek pleasant gifts, described as *iucundaque dona*. These are the gifts of rest, which close the line as *quietis*. With this rest, the first book of the poem comes to an end.
