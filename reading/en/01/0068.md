### 68–69 (*Iliad* 1.457, 1.472–474, 1.456)

#### Line 68

> 68 Protinus infesti placantur numina Phoebi

The response of the god follows without delay, marked by *Protinus*. Someone was angry, described as *infesti*, though who it is is not yet said. Now that anger is appeased, told with *placantur*. What is soothed is divine power, returning as *numina*. What was described as *infesti* turns out to be Phoebus, who closes the line as *Phoebi*.

#### Line 69

> 69 et prope consumptae uires redduntur Achiuis.

Relief also reaches the army, joined by *et*. Their loss was almost complete, marked by *prope*. Their physical strength had been worn down, spoken of as *consumptae uires*. That strength is given back to them, told with *redduntur*. Those who receive it back close the line as *Achiuis*. As the commentary says, the Achaeans are the Greeks.
