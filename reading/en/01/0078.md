### 78–80 (*Iliad* 1.194–200)

#### Lines 78–80

> 78 Quod nisi casta manu Pallas tenuisset Achillem,
>
> 79 turpem caecus amor famam liquisset in aeuum
>
> 80 gentibus Argolicis. Contempta uoce minisque

A turn comes, marked by *Quod*. It is a condition, marked by *nisi*. Someone is chaste, described as *casta*, though who it is is not yet said. A hand comes in, appearing as *manu*. What was described as *casta* is revealed as the goddess, named *Pallas*. As the commentary says, Pallas is Minerva. She held someone back, told with *tenuisset*. The one held back is Achilles, who closes the line as *Achillem*.

Something would have been shameful, described as *turpem*, though what it is is not yet said. A love is blind, spoken of as *caecus amor*. What was described as *turpem* is revealed as a name, appearing as *famam*. That shame would have been left behind, told with *liquisset*. It would have lasted for all time, given as *in aeuum*.

It would have been left to the Argive peoples, described as *gentibus Argolicis*. As the commentary says, the Argive peoples are the Greeks. Then words have been held in contempt, spoken of as *Contempta uoce*. Threats too have gone unheeded, closing the section as *minisque*.
