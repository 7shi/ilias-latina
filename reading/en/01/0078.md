### 78–80 (*Iliad* 1.194–200)

#### Lines 78–80

> 78 Quod nisi casta manu Pallas tenuisset Achillem,
>
> 79 turpem caecus amor famam liquisset in aeuum
>
> 80 gentibus Argolicis. Contempta uoce minisque

The poem turns to what averted the clash, beginning with *Quod*. It states what would have happened otherwise, marked by *nisi*. Someone is chaste, described as *casta*, though who it is is not yet said. She acts with her hand, appearing as *manu*. What was described as *casta* is revealed as the goddess, named *Pallas*. As the commentary says, Pallas is Minerva. She restrains the hero, told with *tenuisset*. The one she holds back is Achilles, who closes the line as *Achillem*.

The outcome would have been shameful, described as *turpem*, though what would be disgraceful is not yet said. The passion driving the quarrel was blind, spoken of as *caecus amor*. What was described as *turpem* is revealed as the lasting report, appearing as *famam*. That shame would have been left behind, told with *liquisset*. It would have endured through the ages, given as *in aeuum*.

The disgrace would have fallen on the Greeks, described as *gentibus Argolicis*. As the commentary says, the Argive peoples are the Greeks. His words had been set aside, spoken of as *Contempta uoce*. His threats too had been spurned, closing the section as *minisque*.
