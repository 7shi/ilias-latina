### 70–73 (*Iliad* 1.318–325, 1.345–348)

#### Lines 70–71

> 70 Non tamen Atridae Chryseidis excidit ardor:
>
> 71 maeret et amissos deceptus luget amores.

The god is appeased, but something does not change, given as *Non*. It holds in spite of all, marked by *tamen*. It concerns the king, returning as *Atridae*. It concerns as well the woman he had to give up, named *Chryseidis*. Something has not left him, told with *excidit*. What has not left him is his passion, closing the line as *ardor*.

He grieves, told with *maeret*. More follows, joined by *et*. Something is lost, described as *amissos*, though what it is is not yet said. He feels cheated, appearing as *deceptus*. He mourns, given as *luget*. What was described as *amissos* is revealed as his beloved, who closes the line as *amores*.

#### Lines 72–73

> 72 Mox rapta magnum Briseide priuat Achillem
>
> 73 solaturque suos alienis ignibus ignes.

Soon he acts, marked by *Mox*. Someone is carried off, described as *rapta*, though who it is is not yet named. Someone else is great, spoken of as *magnum*, though who he is is not yet said. The one carried off is Briseis, named *Briseide*. The king deprives the great one of her, told with *priuat*. The one described as *magnum* is Achilles, who closes the line as *Achillem*.

He also soothes something, told with *solaturque*. What he soothes is his own, given as *suos*. He soothes it with what belongs to another, described as *alienis ignibus*. What is his own are flames as well, which close the line as *ignes*.
