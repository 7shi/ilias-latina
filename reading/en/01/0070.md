### 70–73 (*Iliad* 1.318–325, 1.345–348)

#### Lines 70–71

> 70 Non tamen Atridae Chryseidis excidit ardor:
>
> 71 maeret et amissos deceptus luget amores.

The god is appeased, but something does not change, given as *Non*. The story turns on what remains, marked by *tamen*. This refusal to change concerns the king, returning as *Atridae*. His feeling is for the woman he had to surrender, named *Chryseidis*. That feeling has not left him, told with *excidit*. What still burns within him is his desire, closing the line as *ardor*.

Agamemnon feels his loss, told with *maeret*. His distress goes further, joined by *et*. He broods over something he has lost, described as *amissos*, though what was lost is not yet said. He feels cheated of his prize, appearing as *deceptus*. He laments his deprivation, given as *luget*. What was described as *amissos* is revealed as his beloved, who closes the line as *amores*.

#### Lines 72–73

> 72 Mox rapta magnum Briseide priuat Achillem
>
> 73 solaturque suos alienis ignibus ignes.

The king takes action without delay, marked by *Mox*. Someone is carried off, described as *rapta*, though who it is is not yet named. The one from whom she is taken is great, spoken of as *magnum*, though who he is is not yet said. What was described as *rapta* is revealed as Briseis, named *Briseide*. Agamemnon robs him of her, told with *priuat*. The hero who was described as *magnum* is Achilles, who closes the line as *Achillem*.

He also seeks consolation for himself, told with *solaturque*. What he seeks to soothe is his own, given as *suos*. He tries to satisfy it with another man's love, described as *alienis ignibus*. The feeling of his own that he eases is his flames, which close the line as *ignes*.
