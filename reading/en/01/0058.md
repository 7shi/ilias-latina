### 58–64 (*Iliad* 1.101–108, 1.131–147, 1.173–187, 1.149–171, 1.225–244, 1.304–305, 1.116–117, 1.308–311, 1.440–441)

#### Line 58

> 58 Dixerat. Exarsit subito uiolentia regis:

Calchas has finished speaking, marked by *Dixerat*. Something blazes up, told with *Exarsit*. It breaks out all at once, given as *subito*. What blazes is a surge of fury, named *uiolentia*. The one whose fury it is is the king, who closes the line as *regis*.

#### Lines 59–61

> 59 Thestoriden dictis primum compellat amaris
>
> 60 mendacemque uocat. Tum magnum incusat Achillem
>
> 61 inque uicem ducis inuicti conuicia suffert.

The king's first target is the son of Thestor, who appears again as *Thestoriden*. He goes at him with words, spoken of as *dictis*, though what kind of words they are is not yet said. This comes first, marked by *primum*. He addresses the seer, told with *compellat*. What was spoken of as *dictis* turns out to be bitter words, appearing as *amaris*.

He has a name for him as well, a liar, given as *mendacemque*. He calls him so, told with *uocat*. Then the king turns to another, marked by *Tum*. This man is great, described as *magnum*, though who he is is not yet named. The king brings charges against him, told with *incusat*. The one described as *magnum* is revealed as Achilles, closing the line as *Achillem*.

Then it goes the other way, given as *inque uicem*. Now something comes from an undefeated leader, described as *ducis inuicti*. What comes are insults, named *conuicia*. The king has to bear them, told with *suffert*.

#### Lines 62–64

> 62 Confremuere omnes. Tandem clamore represso
>
> 63 cogitur inuitos aeger dimittere amores
>
> 64 intactamque pio reddit Chryseida patri

Then there is an uproar, told with *Confremuere*. Everyone joins in, appearing as *omnes*. At last something changes, marked by *Tandem*. The shouting is held down, given as *clamore represso*.

The king is forced, told with *cogitur*. Someone is unwilling, described as *inuitos*, though who it is is not yet said. He himself is sick at heart, described as *aeger*. He is made to let someone go, given as *dimittere*. What was described as *inuitos* is revealed as his beloved, closing the line as *amores*.

She is untouched, spoken of as *intactamque*. Someone is devout, described as *pio*, though who it is is not yet said. The king gives her back, told with *reddit*. She is Chryseis, returning as *Chryseida*. What was described as *pio* is her father, who closes the line as *patri*.
