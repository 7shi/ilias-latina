### 58–64 (*Iliad* 1.101–108, 1.131–147, 1.173–187, 1.149–171, 1.225–244, 1.304–305, 1.116–117, 1.308–311, 1.440–441)

#### Line 58

> 58 Dixerat. Exarsit subito uiolentia regis:

Calchas has finished speaking, marked by *Dixerat*. Something blazes up, told with *Exarsit*. It breaks out all at once, given as *subito*. What blazes is a surge of fury, named *uiolentia*. The one whose fury it is is the king, who closes the line as *regis*.

#### Lines 59–61

> 59 Thestoriden dictis primum compellat amaris
>
> 60 mendacemque uocat. Tum magnum incusat Achillem
>
> 61 inque uicem ducis inuicti conuicia suffert.

The king turns first on the seer, calling him the son of Thestor as *Thestoriden*. He attacks him in speech, spoken of as *dictis*, though what kind of words they are is not yet said. This is his first attack, marked by *primum*. He addresses the seer, told with *compellat*. What was spoken of as *dictis* turns out to be bitter words, appearing as *amaris*.

He also denounces him as a liar, given as *mendacemque*. He names him so, appearing as *uocat*. Then the quarrel moves to another, indicated by *Tum*. The man he attacks is great, described as *magnum*, though who it is is not yet named. Agamemnon brings charges against him, told with *incusat*. The one described as *magnum* is revealed as Achilles, closing the line as *Achillem*.

Achilles answers him in turn, given as *inque uicem*. The insults come from an undefeated leader, described as *ducis inuicti*. What he casts back at the king are insults, named *conuicia*. Agamemnon has to bear them, told with *suffert*.

#### Lines 62–64

> 62 Confremuere omnes. Tandem clamore represso
>
> 63 cogitur inuitos aeger dimittere amores
>
> 64 intactamque pio reddit Chryseida patri

The crowd of soldiers roars with anger, told with *Confremuere*. The shouting comes from the whole army, appearing as *omnes*. At length the noise subsides, marked by *Tandem*. The shouting is quieted, given as *clamore represso*.

The king cannot resist the army's pressure; he is compelled, told with *cogitur*. The captive does not welcome his affection, described as *inuitos*, though what is unwilling is not yet said. Agamemnon suffers bitter grief at the loss, appearing as *aeger*. He is made to let her go, given as *dimittere*. What was described as *inuitos* is revealed as the object of his passion, closing the line as *amores*.

She is also restored without harm, spoken of as *intactamque*. The one who receives her is devout, described as *pio*, though who is devout is not yet said. The king gives her up, told with *reddit*. The maiden restored is Chryseis, returning as *Chryseida*. What was described as *pio* is her father, who closes the line as *patri*.
