### 81–82 (*Iliad* 1.348–357)

#### Lines 81–82

> 81 inuocat aequoreae Pelides numina matris
>
> 82 ne se Plistheniden contra patiatur inultum.

Achilles calls upon divine help, told with *inuocat*. He calls on someone of the sea, described as *aequoreae*, though who it is is not yet said. The one who calls is the son of Peleus, appearing as *Pelides*. What he appeals to is divine power, returning as *numina*. What was described as *aequoreae* is revealed as his mother, who closes the line as *matris*.

What he asks her to prevent begins with *ne*. He points to himself as *se*. His enemy is named through another ancestor as *Plistheniden*. As the commentary says, this descendant of Plisthenes is Agamemnon. He seeks redress against him, marked by *contra*. He prays that she not allow this, told with *patiatur*. He refuses to remain without vengeance, closing the line as *inultum*.
