### 81–82 (*Iliad* 1.348–357)

#### Lines 81–82

> 81 inuocat aequoreae Pelides numina matris
>
> 82 ne se Plistheniden contra patiatur inultum.

Someone calls on a power, told with *inuocat*. He calls on someone of the sea, described as *aequoreae*, though who it is is not yet said. The one who calls is the son of Peleus, appearing as *Pelides*. What he calls on is divine power, returning as *numina*. What was described as *aequoreae* is revealed as his mother, who closes the line as *matris*.

What he asks is that something not happen, marked by *ne*. He points to himself as *se*. Against him stands the descendant of Plisthenes, named *Plistheniden*. As the commentary says, this descendant of Plisthenes is Agamemnon. It is against this man, marked by *contra*. She is not to allow it, told with *patiatur*. What she is not to allow is that he stay unavenged, which closes the line as *inultum*.
