### 86–87 (*Iliad* 1.497–501)

#### Lines 86–87

> 86 emicat aetherias et in aurea sidera fertur.
>
> 87 Tunc genibus regis sparsis affusa capillis:

She darts upward, told with *emicat*. Those *auras* belong to the heavens, described as *aetherias*. She continues upward, joined by *et* toward golden realms, marked by *in aurea*, though what is golden is not yet said. What was marked by *in aurea* is revealed as the stars, appearing as *sidera*. She is borne aloft to them, closing the line as *fertur*.

The moment of her arrival follows, marked by *Tunc*. She casts herself before his knees, named *genibus*. These knees belong to the king, designated as *regis*. As the commentary says, this king is Jupiter. Something about her is disheveled, described as *sparsis*, though what is scattered is not yet said. She throws herself down before him, told with *affusa*. What was described as *sparsis* is revealed as her hair, which closes the line as *capillis*.
