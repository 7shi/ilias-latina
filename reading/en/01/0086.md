### 86–87 (*Iliad* 1.497–501)

#### Lines 86–87

> 86 emicat aetherias et in aurea sidera fertur.
>
> 87 Tunc genibus regis sparsis affusa capillis:

She darts upward, told with *emicat*. Those *auras* belong to heaven, described as *aetherias*. More follows, joined by *et*. She goes toward something golden, given as *in aurea*. It is the stars, appearing as *sidera*. She is carried there, closing the line as *fertur*.

Then she arrives, marked by *Tunc*. Knees come next, named *genibus*. They are the knees of the king, designated as *regis*. As the commentary says, this king is Jupiter. Something of hers is scattered, described as *sparsis*, though what it is is not yet said. She throws herself down before him, told with *affusa*. What was described as *sparsis* is revealed as her hair, which closes the line as *capillis*.
