### 50–51 (*Iliad* 1.54, 1.62–64)

#### Lines 50–51

> 50 cum Danaum proceres in coetum clarus Achilles
>
> 51 conuocat et causas hortatur pestis iniquae

On the tenth morning the crisis reaches a turning point, marked by *cum*. Achilles turns to the Greek army, appearing as *Danaum*. Those he singles out are the chiefs, named *proceres*. He brings them together into a meeting, told as *in coetum*. The one who calls them together is renowned, described as *clarus*. He is the warrior who opened the story, returning as *Achilles*.

He calls the gathering, given as *conuocat*. He also speaks to the chiefs, joined by *et*. He urges them to search for the reasons, spoken of as *causas*, though what they are reasons for is not yet said. He urges them forward, told with *hortatur*. What was marked as *causas* turns out to be the source of the cruel plague, described as *pestis iniquae*.
