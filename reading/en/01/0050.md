### 50–51 (*Iliad* 1.54, 1.62–64)

#### Lines 50–51

> 50 cum Danaum proceres in coetum clarus Achilles
>
> 51 conuocat et causas hortatur pestis iniquae

The tenth day is when something is done, marked by *cum*. The Danaans come first, appearing as *Danaum*. Of them, it is the chiefs who are meant, named *proceres*. They are brought into a meeting, told as *in coetum*. The one who brings them is renowned, described as *clarus*. He is the warrior named at the start of the poem, returning as *Achilles*.

He calls them together, given as *conuocat*. He does more, joined by *et*. Causes come next, spoken of as *causas*, though what they are the causes of is not yet said. He urges someone on, told with *hortatur*, but whom he urges, and to what, is said only in the next section. What was spoken of as *causas* are the causes of a cruel plague, described as *pestis iniquae*.
