### 52–57 (*Iliad* 1.62–64, 1.68–72, 1.74–91, 1.93–100)

#### Lines 52–54

> 52 edere Thestoriden. Tunc Calchas numina diuum
>
> 53 consulit et causam pariter finemque malorum
>
> 54 inuenit effarique uerens ope tutus Achillis

What Achilles urges is that the *causas* be brought out, told with *edere*. The one he urges is the son of Thestor, appearing as *Thestoriden*. Then this man acts, marked by *Tunc*. Only now is the son of Thestor called by his own name, *Calchas*. He turns to the divine powers, spoken of as *numina*. Those powers belong to the gods, closing the line as *diuum*.

He seeks their counsel, told with *consulit*. His search goes further, joined by *et*. What he looks for is the cause, named *causam*. Something else comes with it, marked by *pariter*. That is an end, named *finemque*. Both are of their troubles, which close the line as *malorum*.

He finds them both, told with *inuenit*. Speaking out comes next, given as *effarique*. He is afraid to do it, described as *uerens*. Yet he has someone's aid, spoken of as *ope*, though whose aid it is is not yet said. In it he is safe, appearing as *tutus*. What was spoken of as *ope* is revealed as the power of Achilles, who closes the line as *Achillis*.

#### Lines 55–57

> 55 haec ait: "Infesti placemus numina Phoebi
>
> 56 reddamusque pio castam Chryseida patri,
>
> 57 si uolumus, Danai, portus intrare salutis."

Calchas points to what he is about to say as *haec*. And he says it, told with *ait*. Someone is angry, described as *Infesti*, though who it is is not yet said. He calls on all of them to appease, told with *placemus*. What they are to appease is the divine power, returning as *numina*. What was described as *Infesti* is Phoebus, named *Phoebi*.

They are also to give back, told with *reddamusque*. Someone is devout, described as *pio*, though who it is is not yet said. The one to be given back is chaste Chryseis, spoken of as *castam Chryseida*. What was described as *pio* is her father, who closes the line as *patri*.

This is on a condition, marked by *si*. The condition is their own wish, told with *uolumus*. He addresses them all, calling them *Danai*. What they wish to reach are harbors, spoken of as *portus*. They wish to enter them, told with *intrare*. The harbors are those of safety, which close the speech as *salutis*.
