### 52–57 (*Iliad* 1.62–64, 1.68–72, 1.74–91, 1.93–100)

#### Lines 52–54

> 52 edere Thestoriden. Tunc Calchas numina diuum
>
> 53 consulit et causam pariter finemque malorum
>
> 54 inuenit effarique uerens ope tutus Achillis

Achilles urges that the truth be brought to light, told with *edere*. The man called upon to speak is the son of Thestor, appearing as *Thestoriden*. As the commentary says, the son of Thestor is Calchas. Then the seer steps forward, marked by *Tunc*. He is named in his own right as *Calchas*. He turns to the divine powers, spoken of as *numina*. Those powers belong to the gods, closing the line as *diuum*.

He seeks their counsel, told with *consulit*. His search goes further, joined by *et*. He seeks the reason for the plague, named *causam*. Beside the cause he seeks the cure alike, marked by *pariter*. He also looks for an end to the disaster, described as *finemque*. That end is to be for their sufferings, appearing as *malorum*.

He finds both the cause and the remedy, told with *inuenit*. Yet he also shrinks from speaking out, given as *effarique*. He is afraid of the commander's anger, described as *uerens*. He finds safety in someone's aid, spoken of as *ope*, though whose aid it is is not yet said. Under that help he is made secure, appearing as *tutus*. What was spoken of as *ope* is revealed as the power of Achilles, who closes the line as *Achillis*.

#### Lines 55–57

> 55 haec ait: "Infesti placemus numina Phoebi
>
> 56 reddamusque pio castam Chryseida patri,
>
> 57 si uolumus, Danai, portus intrare salutis."

With Achilles' backing, Calchas delivers his answer, pointing to his words with *haec*. He opens his speech, marked by *ait*. The god who sent the plague is wrathful, described as *Infesti*, though who is enraged is not yet said. He urges the army to appease the god, told with *placemus*. What they must placate is the divine will, returning as *numina*. What was described as *Infesti* is revealed as Apollo, named as *Phoebi*.

They must also restore what was taken, told with *reddamusque*. The father who lost his daughter is devout, described as *pio*, though who is devout is not yet said. The daughter to be given back is pure and undeserving of dishonor, spoken of as *castam Chryseida*. What was described as *pio* is revealed as the grieving father, who closes the line as *patri*.

The condition for their deliverance begins with *si*. It depends on their own choice, told with *uolumus*. He addresses the whole assembly, calling them *Danai*. The safety they seek is pictured as a haven, spoken of as *portus*. They must make their way into it, given as *intrare*. That haven is their deliverance, which closes the speech as *salutis*.
