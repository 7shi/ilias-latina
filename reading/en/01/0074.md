### 74–77 (*Iliad* 1.188–194)

#### Lines 74–76

> 74 At ferus Aeacides nudato protinus ense
>
> 75 tendit in Atriden et, ni sibi reddat honestae
>
> 76 munera militiae, letum crudele minatur,

Against this comes a response, marked by *At*. A fierce man comes forward, the descendant of Aeacus, appearing as *ferus Aeacides*. As the commentary says, this descendant of Aeacus is Achilles. Something of his has been bared, described as *nudato*, though what it is is not yet said. It is done at once, marked by *protinus*. What was described as *nudato* is his sword, which closes the line as *ense*.

He advances, told with *tendit*. He advances on the king, given as *in Atriden*. More follows, joined by *et*. It comes with a condition, marked by *ni*. Something is to be given back to him, and he points to himself as *sibi*. The king is to restore it, told with *reddat*. Something is honorable, described as *honestae*, though what it is is not yet said.

What was described as *honestae* is revealed as rewards, appearing as *munera*. They are the rewards of service in war, named *militiae*. What he holds out is a cruel death, described as *letum crudele*. He threatens it, told with *minatur*.

#### Line 77

> 77 nec minus ille parat contra defendere se ense.

The other side does not hang back, given as *nec*. What it does is no less, marked by *minus*. The other is the king, referred to as *ille*. He makes ready, told with *parat*. He faces Achilles, marked by *contra*. What he makes ready to do is defend, told with *defendere*. Whom he defends is himself, appearing as *se*. He does it with a sword, which closes the verse as *ense*.
