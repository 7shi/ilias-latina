### 45–49 (*Iliad* 1.44–54)

#### Lines 45–46

> 45 luctibus infestat Danaos pestemque per omnes
>
> 46 immittit populos: uulgus ruit undique Graium

What was described as *acerbis* in the previous line is revealed as griefs, named *luctibus*. With these griefs Apollo harries someone, told with *infestat*. Those he harries are the Danaans, appearing as *Danaos*. Next comes a plague, named *pestemque*. It spreads through all of something, given as *per omnes*, though what they are is not yet said.

He lets it loose upon them, told with *immittit*. What was marked as *per omnes* turns out to be the peoples of the army, named *populos*. Then the throng of the army comes in, spoken of as *uulgus*. It falls, told with *ruit*. It falls on every side, marked by *undique*. The throng is that of the Greeks, who close the line as *Graium*.

#### Line 47

> 47 uixque rogis superest tellus, uix ignibus aer,

Something is then scarcely enough, marked by *uixque*. It is meant for the pyres, spoken of as *rogis*. It scarcely remains for them, told with *superest*. What remains is the earth, appearing as *tellus*. The same shortage comes again, marked by *uix*. This time it is for the flames, named *ignibus*. What is scarcely enough for them is the air, which closes the line as *aer*.

#### Lines 48–49

> 48 deerat ager tumulis. Iam noctis sidera nonae
>
> 49 transierant decimusque dies patefecerat orbem,

Then something was lacking, told with *deerat*. What was lacking was land, named *ager*. It was lacking for the burial mounds, designated as *tumulis*. Already time has passed, marked by *Iam*. A night comes into the count, spoken of as *noctis*, though which night it is is not yet said. What belongs to that night are its stars, appearing as *sidera*. What was named as *noctis* turns out to be the ninth night, revealed as *nonae*.

These stars had passed, told with *transierant*. After them comes the tenth day, described as *decimusque dies*. That day had laid something open, told with *patefecerat*. What it laid open was the world, which closes the line as *orbem*.
