### 45–49 (*Iliad* 1.44–54)

#### Lines 45–46

> 45 luctibus infestat Danaos pestemque per omnes
>
> 46 immittit populos: uulgus ruit undique Graium

What was described as *acerbis* in the previous line is revealed as griefs, named *luctibus*. With these griefs Apollo assails the camp, told with *infestat*. Those he attacks are the Greeks, appearing as *Danaos*. He also brings a deadly plague upon them, described as *pestemque*. It spreads through all of something, given as *per omnes*, though what they are is not yet said.

He lets it loose upon them, told with *immittit*. What was marked as *per omnes* turns out to be the peoples of the army, named *populos*. The common soldiers begin to fall, spoken of as *uulgus*. The men collapse in death, falling with *ruit*. The slaughter spreads from all directions, marked by *undique*. Those who fall are the Greeks, who close the line as *Graium*.

#### Line 47

> 47 uixque rogis superest tellus, uix ignibus aer,

The space for the dead is also scarcely enough, told with *uixque*. The pyres need more ground than can be found, spoken of as *rogis*. Little room remains to hold them, expressed with *superest*. What runs short is the earth itself, appearing as *tellus*. A second shortage is felt just as hardly, marked by *uix*. The flames rise beyond measure, named *ignibus*. What is scarcely enough for them is the air, which closes the line as *aer*.

#### Lines 48–49

> 48 deerat ager tumulis. Iam noctis sidera nonae
>
> 49 transierant decimusque dies patefecerat orbem,

Ground was lacking for them, told with *deerat*. What fell short was the open land, named *ager*. It was needed for the burial mounds, designated as *tumulis*. Time moves forward in the plague, marked by *Iam*. Night comes into the count, spoken of as *noctis*, though which night it is is not yet said. The passing time is watched by the stars, appearing as *sidera*. What was named as *noctis* turns out to be the ninth night, revealed as *nonae*.

These stars had run their course and passed away, marked by *transierant*. The next morning brings the tenth day, described as *decimusque dies*. Light opened the world to view, told with *patefecerat*. What the morning revealed was the earth, which closes the line as *orbem*.
