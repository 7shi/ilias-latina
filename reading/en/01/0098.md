### 98–105 (*Iliad* 1.536–539, 1.555–559, 4.60–61, 1.540–543, 1.560–569)

#### Lines 98–105

> 98 Offensa est Iuno: "Tantum"que ait, "optime coniunx,
>
> 99 Doride nata ualet, tantum debetur Achilli,
>
> 100 ut mihi quae coniunx dicor tua quaeque sororis
>
> 101 dulce fero nomen, dilectos fundere Achiuos
>
> 102 et Troum renouare uelis in proelia uires?
>
> 103 Haec ita dona refers nobis? sic diligor a te?"
>
> 104 Talibus incusat dictis irata Tonantem
>
> 105 inque uicem summi patitur conuicia regis.

Someone has taken offense, told as *Offensa est*. The one offended is Juno, named *Iuno*. She asks how much is granted, and cries: *Tantum*. Her speech follows on, joined by *-que*. She speaks, told with *ait*. She addresses her husband, calling him *optime coniunx*.

A daughter is meant, and her mother comes first, named *Doride*. The daughter herself is spoken of as *nata*. As the commentary says, this daughter of Doris is Thetis. She prevails so far, told with *ualet*. So much is owed as well, said again as *tantum*. It is owed to someone, told with *debetur*. The one it is owed to is Achilles, who closes the line as *Achilli*.

What this comes to follows, led in by *ut*. It touches Juno herself, who points to herself as *mihi*. She goes on to say who she is, beginning with *quae*. She is a wife, spoken of as *coniunx*. She is called so, told with *dicor*. She is his, claimed as *tua*. She is also the one who bears another name, joined on as *quaeque*. It is the name of a sister, spoken of as *sororis*, though what of a sister's is meant is not yet said.

Something of the sister's is sweet, described as *dulce*. She bears it, told with *fero*. What is sweet is a sister's name, appearing as *nomen*. Then some are loved, described as *dilectos*, though who they are is not yet said. They are to be slaughtered, told with *fundere*. Those loved are the Achaeans, who close the line as *Achiuos*.

More follows, joined by *et*. The Trojans come in next, named *Troum*. Something of theirs is to be renewed, told with *renouare*. He would wish it, asked with *uelis*. It is to be renewed for battle, given as *in proelia*. What is renewed is their strength, closing the question as *uires*.

She points to all this as *Haec*. She asks whether it is so, marked by *ita*. What it is she calls gifts, named *dona*. He pays them back, told with *refers*. He pays them to her, and she speaks of herself as *nobis*. She asks again whether it is thus, marked by *sic*. She asks whether she is loved, told with *diligor*. The love she asks about is his, which closes her protest as *a te*.

She reproaches him with such things, spoken of as *Talibus*, though what kind they are is not yet said. She blames him, told with *incusat*. What was indicated as *Talibus* is revealed as her words, appearing as *dictis*. She is furious as she speaks, described as *irata*. The god she reproaches is the Thunderer, who closes the line as *Tonantem*.

Then it goes the other way, told as *inque uicem*. Someone is highest, described as *summi*, though who it is is not yet said. She endures something, given as *patitur*. What she endures is abuse, appearing as *conuicia*. The highest one is the king, who closes the section as *regis*.
