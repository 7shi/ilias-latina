### 83–84 (*Iliad* 1.357–360, 1.421–422)

#### Lines 83–84

> 83 At Thetis audita nati prece deserit undas
>
> 84 castraque Myrmidonum iuxta petit et monet armis

The poem turns from his prayer to his mother, beginning with *At*. The goddess of the sea appears by name as *Thetis*. She has heard something, told with *audita*, though what was heard is not yet said. It came from her son, spoken of as *nati*. What she heard is revealed as his prayer, given as *prece*. She leaves her place, told with *deserit*. What she departs from is the sea waters, which close the line as *undas*.

Her path leads also toward the camp, reached as *castraque*. The camp belongs to his followers, known as *Myrmidonum*. Their station lies close at hand, marked by *iuxta*. She makes straight for them, seeking them with *petit*. She also offers her counsel, joined by *et* and warning him with *monet*. What she warns him away from is battle, closing the line as *armis*.
