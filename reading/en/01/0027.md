### 27–31 (*Iliad* 1.33–36)

#### Line 27

> 27 Contemptus repetit Phoebeia templa sacerdos

Turned away by the king, Chryses is a man rejected, described as *Contemptus*. He goes back again, told with *repetit*. What he goes back to are the temples of Phoebus, named the *Phoebeia templa*. As the commentary says, Phoebus is Apollo. The one who returns is the priest, who closes the line as *sacerdos*.

#### Line 28

> 28 squalidaque infestis maerens secat unguibus ora

As he goes, something of his is unkempt, described as *squalidaque*, though what it is is not yet said. Something he uses on it is savage, declared *infestis*, but what that is is not yet named either. He is grieving, pictured as *maerens*. He cuts at himself, told with *secat*. What was declared *infestis* turns out to be his nails, which appear as *unguibus*. At the end of the line what was called *squalidaque* is revealed as his face, designated as *ora*.

#### Line 29

> 29 dilaceratque comas annosaque tempora plangit.

He goes further and tears something apart, told with *dilaceratque*. What he tears is his hair, named *comas*. His temples are old, described as *annosaque tempora*. Last, he beats them, and that beating is *plangit*.

#### Lines 30–31

> 30 Mox ubi depositi gemitus lacrimaeque quierunt,
>
> 31 Fatidici his sacras compellat uocibus aures:

After this outburst a calmer moment comes soon, marked by *Mox*. It comes when his grief settles, which is told with *ubi*. His groans are laid aside, described as *depositi gemitus*. With them his tears are named too, as *lacrimaeque*. Both at last fall quiet, told with *quierunt*.

Then he turns to the god of prophecy, who is spoken of as *Fatidici*, but what of the god is meant is not yet said. He points to what he is about to say as *his*. What he turns to is holy, described as *sacras*. He addresses it, told with *compellat*. What he pointed to as *his* are words, appearing as *uocibus*. At the end of the section what was called *sacras* turns out to be the ears of the *Fatidici*, named *aures*.
