### 660–662 (*Iliad* 8.216, 8.213–215)

#### Lines 660–662

> 660 Priamides agit et totis grauis imminet armis
>
> 661 unum quippe decus Phrygiae. Turbantur Achiui
>
> 662 Doricaque ingenti complentur castra tumultu.

The warrior roused by anger is revealed here. The son of Priam comes in, named *Priamides*. As the commentary says, Priamides is Hector. He drives the Danaans, told with *agit*. More is added, marked by *et*. All are brought in, called *totis*, though what is meant is not yet said. He is heavy, described as *grauis*. He bears down, told with *imminet*. What was all is his arms, which close the line as *armis*.

He is sole, described as *unum*. Indeed a reason follows, marked by *quippe*. What he is alone is the glory, named *decus*. The glory belongs to Phrygia, called *Phrygiae*. They fall into confusion, told with *Turbantur*. Those in confusion are the Achaeans, who close the line as *Achiui*.

Something is Doric as well, described as *Doricaque*, though what it is is not yet said. Something is mighty, called *ingenti*. It is filled, told with *complentur*. What is Doric is the camp, named *castra*. As the commentary says, the Doric camp is the camp of the Greeks. What is mighty is uproar, which closes the line as *tumultu*.
