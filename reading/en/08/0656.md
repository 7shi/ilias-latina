### 656–659 (*Iliad* 8.69–74, 8.215)

#### Lines 656–658

> 656 sustinet auratas aequato pondere lances
>
> 657 fataque dura Phrygum casusque expendit Achiuum
>
> 658 et Graium clades grauibus praeponderat armis.

He holds something up, told with *sustinet*. Something is golden, described as *auratas*, though what it is is not yet said. Something is balanced, described as *aequato*. What is balanced is weight, named *pondere*. What is golden is scales, which close the line as *lances*.

Fates come in as well, named *fataque*. The fates are harsh, described as *dura*. The Phrygians come in, named *Phrygum*. As the commentary says, the Phrygians are the Trojans. A fall comes in as well, named *casusque*. He weighs them, told with *expendit*. The fall is of the Achaeans, which closes the line as *Achiuum*. As the commentary says, the Achaeans are the Greeks.

More is added, marked by *et*. The Greeks come in, named *Graium*. Their ruin comes in, named *clades*. Something is heavy, described as *grauibus*. The ruin weighs down, told with *praeponderat*. What is heavy is arms, which close the line as *armis*.

#### Line 659

> 659 Interea Danaos ingenti concitus ira

A time is set, marked by *Interea*. The Danaans come in, named *Danaos*. As the commentary says, the Danaans are the Greeks. Something is immense, described as *ingenti*, though what it is is not yet said. Someone is roused, described as *concitus*. What is immense is anger, which closes the line as *ira*. Who is roused and what he does comes only in the next section.
