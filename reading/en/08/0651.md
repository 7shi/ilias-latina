### 651–654 (*Iliad* 8.2–12, 8.41–51)

#### Lines 651–652

> 651 conuocat in coetum superos Iouis et monet, armis
>
> 652 ne contra sua dicta uelint contendere diui.

What happened when the world was revealed begins here. Someone calls them together, told with *conuocat*. An assembly comes in, given as *in coetum*. Those called together are the gods above, called *superos*. Jupiter comes in, named *Iouis*. More is added, marked by *et*. He warns them, told with *monet*. Arms come in, which close the line as *armis*.

A forbidding comes, marked by *ne*. Opposition comes, given as *contra*. What it opposes is his own, described as *sua*. What is his own is his words, named *dicta*. They should have the wish, as *uelint*. They should contend in those arms, told with *contendere*. The ones forbidden to contend are the gods, which close the line as *diui*.

#### Lines 653–654

> 653 Ipse per aetherias caeli delabitur auras
>
> 654 umbrosisque simul consedit montibus Idae.

Jupiter himself comes in, as *Ipse*. Something is through the upper air, given as *per aetherias*, though what it is is not yet said. Heaven comes in, named *caeli*. He glides down, told with *delabitur*. What is through the upper air is the air, which closes the line as *auras*.

Something is shaded as well, described as *umbrosisque*, though what it is is not yet said. At once the moment comes, marked by *simul*. He took his seat, told with *consedit*. What is shaded is the mountains, named *montibus*. The mountains are Ida's, which close the line as *Idae*.
