### 651–654 (*Iliad* 8.2–12, 8.41–51)

#### Lines 651–652

> 651 conuocat in coetum superos Iouis et monet, armis
>
> 652 ne contra sua dicta uelint contendere diui.

Someone calls others together, told with *conuocat*. An assembly comes in, given as *in coetum*. Those called together are the gods above, named *superos*. The one who calls them is Jupiter, named *Iouis*. More is added, marked by *et*. He warns them, told with *monet*. Arms come in, which close the line as *armis*.

A forbidding comes, marked by *ne*. Something is set against, given as *contra*. Something is his own, described as *sua*. What is his own is his words, named *dicta*. Some should wish, told with *uelint*. They should wish to contend, told with *contendere*. Those who should not wish it are the gods, who close the line as *diui*.

#### Lines 653–654

> 653 Ipse per aetherias caeli delabitur auras
>
> 654 umbrosisque simul consedit montibus Idae.

Jupiter himself comes in, as *Ipse*. Something upper comes in, given as *per aetherias*, though what it is is not yet said. Heaven comes in, named *caeli*. He glides down, told with *delabitur*. What is upper is the air, which closes the line as *auras*.

Something is shaded as well, described as *umbrosisque*, though what it is is not yet said. It is at once, marked by *simul*. He took his seat, told with *consedit*. What is shaded is the mountains, named *montibus*. Ida comes in, which closes the line as *Idae*.
