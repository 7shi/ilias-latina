### 880–884 (*Iliad* 18.593–594, 18.599–602, 18.569–571)

#### Lines 880–881

> 880 Parte alia castae resonant Paeana puellae
>
> 881 dantque choros molles et tympana dextera pulsat;

Another part comes in, given as *Parte alia*. Some are chaste, described as *castae*, though who they are is not yet said. They make something resound, told with *resonant*. What they make resound is the Paean, called *Paeana*. As the commentary says, a paean is a song of praise or celebration addressed above all to Apollo. Who are chaste is maidens, who close the line as *puellae*.

They lead something as well, told with *dantque*. Soft dances come in, given as *choros molles*. More is added, marked by *et*. Tambourines come in, named *tympana*. A right hand appears as *dextera*. It beats them, which closes the line as *pulsat*.

#### Lines 882–883

> 882 ille lyrae graciles extenso pollice chordas
>
> 883 percurrit septemque modos modulatur auenis:

Someone comes in, as *ille*. A lyre comes in, named *lyrae*. Something is slender, described as *graciles*, though what it is is not yet said. An outstretched thumb comes in, given as *extenso pollice*. What is slender is strings, which close the line as *chordas*.

He runs over them, told with *percurrit*. Seven measures come in as well, given as *septemque modos*. He plays them, told with *modulatur*. Reeds come in, which close the line as *auenis*.

#### Line 884

> 884 carmina componunt mundi resonantia motum.

Songs come in, named *carmina*. They compose them, told with *componunt*. The world comes back, named *mundi*. The songs are echoing, described as *resonantia*. What they echo is movement, which closes the line as *motum*.
