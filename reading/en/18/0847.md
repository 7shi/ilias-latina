### 847–855 (*Iliad* 18.316–318, 18.323, 18.334–335)

#### Lines 847–848

> 847 et super exstincti prostratus membra sodalis
>
> 848 crudeles fundit questus atque oscula figit.

More is added, marked by *et*. A place above comes in, marked by *super*. Someone is dead, described as *exstincti*, though who it is is not yet said. Achilles is stretched out, described as *prostratus*. The limbs come in, named *membra*. Who is dead is his companion, who closes the line as *sodalis*.

Something is cruel, described as *crudeles*, though what it is is not yet said. He pours something out, told with *fundit*. What is cruel is laments, named *questus*. More is added, marked by *atque*. Kisses come in, named *oscula*. He presses them, which closes the line as *figit*.

#### Line 849

> 849 Mox ubi depositi gemitus lacrimaeque quierunt:

A time is set, marked by *Mox*. Another time is set, marked by *ubi*. The laid-out dead man comes in, described as *depositi*. Groans come in, named *gemitus*. Tears come in as well, named *lacrimaeque*. They ceased, which closes the line as *quierunt*.

#### Lines 850–853

> 850 "Non impune mei laetabere caede sodalis,
>
> 851 Hector" - ait - "magnoque meo, uiolente, dolori
>
> 852 persolues poenas atque istis uictor in armis,
>
> 853 in quibus exsultas, fuso moriere cruore."

A denial comes, marked by *Non*. Something is unpunished, marked by *impune*. Someone is his own, described as *mei*, though who it is is not yet said. The one addressed will rejoice, told with *laetabere*. Slaughter comes in, named *caede*. Who is his own is his companion, who closes the line as *sodalis*.

Who is addressed is Hector, called *Hector*. Achilles speaks, told with *ait*. Something is great as well, described as *magnoque*. It is his own, described as *meo*. Someone is reckless, described as *uiolente*. Grief comes in, which closes the line as *dolori*.

Hector will pay, told with *persolues*. Penalties come in, named *poenas*. More is added, marked by *atque*. Something comes in, as *istis*, though what it is is not yet said. Hector is a victor, named *uictor*. What it is is the armor, which closes the line as *in armis*.

The armor comes back, given as *in quibus*. Hector exults, told with *exsultas*. Something is poured out, described as *fuso*. Hector will die, told with *moriere*. Blood comes in, which closes the line as *cruore*.

#### Lines 854–855

> 854 Post haec accensus furiis decurrit ad aequor
>
> 855 fortiaque arma Thetin supplex rogat: illa relictis

A time is set, given as *Post haec*. He is ablaze, described as *accensus*. Fury comes in, named *furiis*. He runs down, told with *decurrit*. The sea comes in, which closes the line as *ad aequor*.

Strong armor comes in as well, given as *fortiaque arma*. Thetis comes in, named *Thetin*. As the commentary says, Thetis is Achilles' mother, a sea goddess. He is a suppliant, described as *supplex*. He begs her, told with *rogat*. Thetis comes back, as *illa*. Something is left behind, which closes the line as *relictis*. What it is comes only in the next section.
