### 876–877 (*Iliad* 18.490, 18.497–500)

#### Lines 876–877

> 876 fluminaque et montes cumque altis oppida muris,
>
> 877 in quibus exercent leges annosaque iura

Rivers come in as well, named *fluminaque*. More is added, marked by *et*. Mountains come in, named *montes*. With something lofty as well, given as *cumque altis*, though what it is is not yet said. Towns come in, named *oppida*. What is lofty is walls, which close the line as *muris*.

The towns come back, given as *in quibus*. They practice something, told with *exercent*. Laws come in, named *leges*. Something is time-honored as well, described as *annosaque*, though what it is is not yet said. What is time-honored is rights, which close the line as *iura*. Who practices them comes only in the next section.
