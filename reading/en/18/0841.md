### 841–844 (*Iliad* 18.22, 18.352–353)

#### Lines 841–842

> 841 Tunc ut Pelidae aures diuerberat horror,
>
> 842 palluit infelix iuuenis, calor ossa reliquit;

A time comes in, given as *Tunc*. When it happens comes in, marked by *ut*. The son of Peleus comes in, called *Pelidae*. As the commentary says, Pelides is Achilles, son of Peleus. His ears come in, named *aures*. Something strikes them, told with *diuerberat*. Dread strikes them, which closes the line as *horror*.

He grew pale, told with *palluit*. The unhappy young man comes in, given as *infelix iuuenis*. Warmth comes in, named *calor*. His bones come in, as *ossa*. It left them, which closes the line as *reliquit*.

#### Lines 843–844

> 843 membra simul lacrimans materno innectit amictu,
>
> 844 deflens Aeacides tristi de caede sodalis;

The limbs come in, named *membra*. The same time comes in, marked by *simul*. He is weeping, described as *lacrimans*. Something is from his mother, described as *materno*, though what it is is not yet said. He wraps them, told with *innectit*. What is from his mother is a robe, which closes the line as *amictu*.

He is weeping bitterly, described as *deflens*. The mourner is called *Aeacides*. As the commentary says, Aeacides is Achilles, grandson of Aeacus. Something is sad, described as *tristi*, though what it is is not yet said. What is sad is slaughter, given as *de caede*. His companion comes in, which closes the line as *sodalis*.
