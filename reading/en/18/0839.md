### 839–840 (*Iliad* 18.2, 18.16, 18.231–233)

#### Lines 839–840

> 839 Interea iuuenis tristi cum pube suorum
>
> 840 Nestorides in castra ferunt miserabile corpus.

A time is set, marked by *Interea*. The young man comes in, named *iuuenis*. Someone is grieving, described as *tristi*, though who it is is not yet said. Who is grieving is the youth, given as *cum pube*. His own people come in, which close the line as *suorum*.

The young man is called *Nestorides*. As the commentary says, Nestorides is Antilochus, a young Greek warrior and son of Nestor. The camp comes in, given as *in castra*. They bear it, told with *ferunt*. The pitiful body comes in, which closes the line as *miserabile corpus*.
