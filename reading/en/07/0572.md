### 572–574 (*Iliad* 6.475–478)

#### Lines 572–574

> 572 attollensque manus: "Precor, o pater optime" - dixit -
>
> 573 "ut meus hic, pro quo tua numina, natus, adoro,
>
> 574 uirtutes patrias primis imitetur ab annis."

He is raising something as well, described as *attollensque*. It is his hands, named *manus*. He prays, told with *Precor*. He cries out, beginning with *o*. He calls on a father, named *pater*. The father is the best, called *optime*. Hector says so, told with *dixit*.

What he prays for comes next, marked by *ut*. Something is his own, described as *meus*. It is this one, pointed to as *hic*. It is on his behalf, given as *pro quo*. Something is the father's, described as *tua*. It is divine power, named *numina*. What was his own is his son, named *natus*. He appeals to that power, which closes the line as *adoro*.

Virtues come in, named *uirtutes*. They are his father's, described as *patrias*. Something is earliest, described as *primis*, though what it is is not yet said. The son is to imitate them, told with *imitetur*. What was earliest is his years, which close the line as *ab annis*.
