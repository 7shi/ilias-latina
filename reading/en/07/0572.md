### 572–574 (*Iliad* 6.475–478)

#### Lines 572–574

> 572 attollensque manus: "Precor, o pater optime" - dixit -
>
> 573 "ut meus hic, pro quo tua numina, natus, adoro,
>
> 574 uirtutes patrias primis imitetur ab annis."

He raises something, described as *attollensque*. What he raises are his hands, named *manus*. He prays: *Precor*. He begins his cry: *o*. He calls upon a father, called *pater*. That father is the best, spoken of as *optime*. Hector spoke, told with *dixit*.

What he asks for is marked by *ut*. The child is his own, described as *meus*. He is this one, as *hic*. It is on his behalf, given as *pro quo*. The power is the god's, called *tua*. It is divine power, spoken of as *numina*. What was his own is his son, named *natus*. He appeals to that power, told with *adoro*.

Virtues come in, named *uirtutes*. They are his father's, described as *patrias*. A time is the earliest, called *primis*, though what time it is is not yet said. The son should imitate them, told with *imitetur*. What was earliest is from his childhood years, which close the line as *ab annis*.
