### 641–643 (*Iliad* 7.381–386, 7.400–404)

#### Lines 641–642

> 641 pertulit Idaeus Troum mandata; neque ille
>
> 642 aut animum praedae aut dictis accommodat aures,

He delivered something, told with *pertulit*. The one sent is Idaeus, named *Idaeus*. The Trojans come in, named *Troum*. What he delivered is their message, named *mandata*. A denial comes, marked by *neque*. Atrides comes in, as *ille*.

One alternative comes, marked by *aut*. His mind comes in, named *animum*. The plunder comes in, named *praedae*. Another alternative comes, marked by *aut*. The words come in, named *dictis*. He lends neither, told with *accommodat*. What he lends to neither is his ears, which close the line as *aures*.

#### Line 643

> 643 ultro etiam castris Idaeum excedere iussit.

Something goes beyond this, marked by *ultro*. It is even so, marked by *etiam*. The camp comes in, named *castris*. Idaeus comes in, named *Idaeum*. Idaeus is to leave the camp, told with *excedere*. Atrides ordered it, which closes the line as *iussit*.
