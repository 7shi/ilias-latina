### 641–643 (*Iliad* 7.381–386, 7.400–404)

#### Lines 641–642

> 641 pertulit Idaeus Troum mandata; neque ille
>
> 642 aut animum praedae aut dictis accommodat aures,

He delivered it, told with *pertulit*. The messenger is Idaeus, named *Idaeus*. The message is from the Trojans, called *Troum*. What he delivered is their message, spoken of as *mandata*. A refusal follows, marked by *neque*. Atrides comes in, as *ille*.

One thing is refused, as *aut*. He gives no attention, named *animum*. That attention was for the plunder, called *praedae*. Another thing is refused, marked by *aut*. He refuses the words, given as *dictis*. He does not lend them, told with *accommodat*. What he does not lend is his ears, which close the line as *aures*.

#### Line 643

> 643 ultro etiam castris Idaeum excedere iussit.

He goes further, as *ultro*. Even more is added, marked by *etiam*. The camp comes in, given as *castris*. He turns on Idaeus, named *Idaeum*. He demands that he depart, told with *excedere*. He ordered it, which closes the line as *iussit*.
