### 620–627 (*Iliad* 7.287)

#### Lines 620–622

> 620 deponunt animos. Tunc bello maximus Hector:
>
> 621 "Quae te terra uirum, qui te genuere parentes?
>
> 622 Viribus es proles generosa atque inclita" - dixit.

They lay something aside, told with *deponunt*. What they lay aside is their passion, named *animos*. Then someone speaks, marked by *Tunc*. He was renowned in war, given as *bello*. He was greatest, described as *maximus*. The greatest warrior is Hector, who closes the line as *Hector*.

He begins his question, marked by *Quae*. He addresses his foe, as *te*. He asks about his land, named *terra*. He was proven a man, called *uirum*. He asks who they were, spoken of as *qui*. He speaks to him, given as *te*. He asks who begot him, told with *genuere*. Those who bore him are parents, which close the line as *parentes*.

His proof is in his strength, given as *Viribus*. He tells him what he is, told with *es*. He is a noble son, named *proles*. He is noble, described as *generosa*. More praise is added, marked by *atque*. He is illustrious, called *inclita*. Hector finished his words, told with *dixit*.

#### Lines 623–625

> 623 At contra se ferre parat Telamonius Aiax:
>
> 624 "Hesiona de matre uides Telamone creatum,
>
> 625 nobilis est domus et fama generosa propago."

The reply turns, marked by *At*. He answers in turn, as *contra*. He refers to himself, as *se*. He was to present himself, as *ferre*. He prepares to speak, told with *parat*. The warrior was Telamonian, described as *Telamonius*. The speaker is Ajax, who closes the line as *Aiax*.

His mother is Hesione, named *Hesiona*. He was born from a mother, given as *de matre*. He tells Hector what he sees, told with *uides*. His father was Telamon, called *Telamone*. He was begotten by him, which closes the line as *creatum*.

His house was noble, described as *nobilis*. It is so, told with *est*. What is noble is his house, named *domus*. More follows, as *et*. It is renowned in fame, given as *fama*. His lineage was noble, called *generosa*. What is noble is his lineage, which closes the line as *propago*.

#### Lines 626–627

> 626 Hector, ut Hesionae nomen casusque recordans:
>
> 627 "Absistamus" - ait - "sanguis communis utriquest",

Hector answers, named *Hector*. He speaks as one remembering, as *ut*. He hears of Hesione, called *Hesionae*. He recalls her name, spoken of as *nomen*. Her misfortunes too come to mind, described as *casusque*. He remembers them, which closes the line as *recordans*.

He cries: *Absistamus*. He speaks to Ajax, told with *ait*. Their shared blood comes in, named *sanguis*. That blood was shared, described as *communis*. It belongs to both of them, which closes the line as *utriquest*, though what he adds comes only in the next section.
