### 620–627 (*Iliad* 7.287)

#### Lines 620–622

> 620 deponunt animos. Tunc bello maximus Hector:
>
> 621 "Quae te terra uirum, qui te genuere parentes?
>
> 622 Viribus es proles generosa atque inclita" - dixit.

They lay something aside, told with *deponunt*. What they lay aside is their passion, named *animos*. A time is set, marked by *Tunc*. Someone is in war, given as *bello*. He is greatest, described as *maximus*. The one greatest in war is Hector, who closes the line as *Hector*.

A question comes, marked by *Quae*. Someone is addressed, as *te*. A land comes in, named *terra*. The one addressed is a man, named *uirum*. A second question comes, marked by *qui*. The same one is addressed, as *te*. Some gave him birth, told with *genuere*. Those who gave him birth are parents, which close the line as *parentes*.

Strength comes in, given as *Viribus*. The one addressed is something, told with *es*. He is a son, named *proles*. He is noble, described as *generosa*. More is added, marked by *atque*. He is illustrious, described as *inclita*. Hector has spoken, told with *dixit*.

#### Lines 623–625

> 623 At contra se ferre parat Telamonius Aiax:
>
> 624 "Hesiona de matre uides Telamone creatum,
>
> 625 nobilis est domus et fama generosa propago."

Something stands against this, marked by *At*. It is in turn, marked by *contra*. Someone himself comes in, as *se*. He is to present himself, told with *ferre*. He prepares, told with *parat*. Someone is Telamonian, described as *Telamonius*. The Telamonian one is Ajax, who closes the line as *Aiax*.

Hesione comes in, named *Hesiona*. She is given as a mother, with *de matre*. Hector sees someone, told with *uides*. Telamon comes in, named *Telamone*. Someone is born of him, which closes the line as *creatum*.

Something is noble, described as *nobilis*. It is so, told with *est*. What is noble is his house, named *domus*. More is added, marked by *et*. Fame comes in, named *fama*. Something is noble, described as *generosa*. What is noble in fame is his lineage, which closes the line as *propago*.

#### Lines 626–627

> 626 Hector, ut Hesionae nomen casusque recordans:
>
> 627 "Absistamus" - ait - "sanguis communis utriquest",

Hector comes in, named *Hector*. Something is set as a comparison, marked by *ut*. Hesione comes in, named *Hesionae*. Her name comes in, named *nomen*. Her misfortunes as well, named *casusque*. Hector recalls them, which closes the line as *recordans*.

He calls on them to desist, told with *Absistamus*. He says so, told with *ait*. Blood comes in, named *sanguis*. It is common, described as *communis*. It is to both, which closes the line as *utriquest*. What follows comes only in the next section.
