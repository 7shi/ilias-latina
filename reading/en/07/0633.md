### 633–635 (*Iliad* 7.313–320, 7.482)

#### Lines 633–634

> 633 Implentur dapibus largis Bacchique liquore
>
> 634 atque auidi placido tradunt sua corpora somno.

The men take their fill, told with *Implentur*. They feast on food, named *dapibus*. That food was plentiful, described as *largis*. Bacchus too is joined to it, called *Bacchique*. As the commentary says, Bacchus is the god of wine. They drink his liquor, which closes the line as *liquore*.

More follows, as *atque*. They are eager for rest, described as *auidi*. Something was peaceful, called *placido*, though what it is is not yet said. They surrender themselves, told with *tradunt*. They give their own, spoken of as *sua*. What they give are their bodies, named *corpora*. What was peaceful is sleep, which closes the line as *somno*.

#### Line 635

> 635 Postera cum primum stellas Aurora fugarat,

Someone was of the next day, described as *Postera*, though who it is is not yet said. A new time begins as soon as possible, given as *cum primum*. The stars come in, named *stellas*. The goddess of the next day is Aurora, called *Aurora*. As the commentary says, Aurora is the goddess of dawn. She had driven them away, which closes the line as *fugarat*, though what follows comes only in the next section.
