### 633–635 (*Iliad* 7.313–320, 7.482)

#### Lines 633–634

> 633 Implentur dapibus largis Bacchique liquore
>
> 634 atque auidi placido tradunt sua corpora somno.

Some take their fill, told with *Implentur*. They take their fill of food, named *dapibus*. The food is plentiful, described as *largis*. Bacchus comes in as well, named *Bacchique*. As the commentary says, Bacchus is the god of wine. They take their fill of his liquid, which closes the line as *liquore*.

More is added, marked by *atque*. They are eager, described as *auidi*. Something is peaceful, described as *placido*, though what it is is not yet said. They surrender something, told with *tradunt*. It is their own, described as *sua*. What they surrender is their bodies, named *corpora*. What is peaceful is sleep, which closes the line as *somno*.

#### Line 635

> 635 Postera cum primum stellas Aurora fugarat,

Someone is of the next day, described as *Postera*, though who it is is not yet said. A time is set, given as *cum primum*. The stars come in, named *stellas*. The one of the next day is Aurora, named *Aurora*. As the commentary says, Aurora is the goddess of dawn. She had driven the stars away, which closes the line as *fugarat*. What happens then comes only in the next section.
