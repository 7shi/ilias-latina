### 646–648 (*Iliad* 7.430–431, 7.434, 7.432)

#### Lines 646–648

> 646 Interea Danai confusi caede suorum
>
> 647 ingentes struxere pyras collectaque passim
>
> 648 fortia tradiderunt sociorum corpora flammis.

A time is set, marked by *Interea*. The Danaans come in, named *Danai*. As the commentary says, the Danaans are the Greeks. They are grieving, described as *confusi*. They grieve over slaughter, named *caede*. The slaughter is of their own, which closes the line as *suorum*.

Something is great, described as *ingentes*, though what it is is not yet said. They built something, told with *struxere*. What is great is pyres, named *pyras*. Something is gathered as well, described as *collectaque*, though what it is is not yet said. It is gathered from all around, which closes the line as *passim*.

Something is brave, described as *fortia*. They consigned something, told with *tradiderunt*. Their comrades come in, named *sociorum*. What is gathered and brave is their bodies, named *corpora*. They consigned them to the flames, which close the line as *flammis*.
