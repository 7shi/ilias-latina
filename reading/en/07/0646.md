### 646–648 (*Iliad* 7.430–431, 7.434, 7.432)

#### Lines 646–648

> 646 Interea Danai confusi caede suorum
>
> 647 ingentes struxere pyras collectaque passim
>
> 648 fortia tradiderunt sociorum corpora flammis.

Meanwhile time passes, marked by *Interea*. The Danaans come in, named *Danai*. As the commentary says, the Danaans are the Greeks. They are grieving, described as *confusi*. They grieve over slaughter, given as *caede*. The slaughter is of their own men, which closes the line as *suorum*.

Something is great, called *ingentes*. They built them, told with *struxere*. What is great is pyres, named *pyras*. Things were gathered as well, described as *collectaque*, though what was gathered is not yet said. They were gathered from all around, which closes the line as *passim*.

Something is brave, described as *fortia*. They consigned them, told with *tradiderunt*. They belong to their comrades, named *sociorum*. What was gathered and brave is their bodies, given as *corpora*. The bodies go to the flames, which close the line as *flammis*.
