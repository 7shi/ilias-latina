### 595–604 (*Iliad* 7.256–257, 7.260–262)

#### Lines 595–597

> 595 Non sic saetigeri exacuunt feruoribus iras
>
> 596 pectoribusque petunt uastis, modo dentibus uncis
>
> 597 alterni librant gladios et uulnera miscent.

A comparison denies something, marked by *Non*. Not so do others fight, as *sic*. Bristling creatures come in, named *saetigeri*. They stoke something, told with *exacuunt*. It is in their frenzy, given as *feruoribus*. What they stoke is rage, which closes the line as *iras*.

Chests also come in, named *pectoribusque*. They attack with them, told with *petunt*. Those chests are massive, described as *uastis*. Now it is another way, marked by *modo*. They fight with tusks, called *dentibus*. Those tusks are hooked, which close the line as *uncis*.

They take turns, as *alterni*. They swing them, told with *librant*. The weapons are swords, named *gladios*. More follows, marked by *et*. Wounds come in, called *uulnera*. They exchange them, told with *miscent*.

#### Line 598

> 598 fortia terga premunt spumantque per ora uicissim;

Something was sturdy, described as *fortia*. The sturdy parts are backs, named *terga*. They press hard on them, told with *premunt*. They foam as well, told with *spumantque*. The foam is at their mouths, given as *per ora*. It happens in turn, which closes the line as *uicissim*.

#### Lines 599–600

> 599 fumiferae nubes concretaque fulgura et ignes
>
> 600 iactantur magnoque implentur murmure siluae.

Something was smoky, described as *fumiferae*. The smoky things were clouds, named *nubes*. Lightning bolts too were massed, called *concretaque*. Those massed flashes were lightning bolts, spoken of as *fulgura*. More follows, as *et*. Flames come in, which close the line as *ignes*.

They are hurled forth, told with *iactantur*. A sound was mighty, called *magnoque*, though what it is is not yet said. Places are filled with it, told with *implentur*. What was mighty was a rumbling, named *murmure*. What is filled are the woods, which close the line as *siluae*.

#### Line 601

> 601 Tales Priamides ardorque Aiacis in armis

Such are the warriors, as *Tales*. The son of Priam comes in, named *Priamides*. As the commentary says, this son of Priam is Hector. Ardor too is joined to him, called *ardorque*. That ardor belongs to Ajax, spoken of as *Aiacis*. They stand ready in arms, which close the line as *in armis*.

#### Lines 602–604

> 602 Tandem animis teloque furens Telamonius Aiax
>
> 603 insignem bello petit Hectora, quaque patebat
>
> 604 nuda uiri ceruix, fulgentem derigit ensem.

At last something changes, marked by *Tandem*. He was fierce in spirit, given as *animis*. He was fierce with a weapon too, called *teloque*. He was raging, described as *furens*. The hero was Telamonian, spoken of as *Telamonius*. The raging hero is Ajax, who closes the line as *Aiax*.

Someone was famed, described as *insignem*. He was famed in war, given as *bello*. Ajax attacks him, told with *petit*. The famed one is Hector, named *Hectora*. Where something lay open is marked by *quaque*. It lay open, told with *patebat*.

A part of his body was bare, described as *nuda*. It belonged to the man, named *uiri*. What was bare was his neck, called *ceruix*. A weapon was gleaming, spoken of as *fulgentem*. Ajax directs it, told with *derigit*. What was gleaming was his sword, which closes the line as *ensem*.
