### 628–632 (*Iliad* 7.303–307)

#### Line 628

> 628 et prior Aeaciden aurato munerat ense

A gift follows, marked by *et*. Hector is first to give, as *prior*. He gives to Aeacides, named *Aeaciden*. As the commentary says, this descendant of Aeacus is Ajax. The gift was gold-adorned, described as *aurato*, though what it is is not yet said. He presents it, told with *munerat*. What was gold-adorned was a sword, which closes the line as *ense*.

#### Lines 629–630

> 629 inque uicem, quo se bellator cinxerat Aiax,
>
> 630 accipit insignem uario caelamine balteum.

Another gift comes in return, given as *inque uicem*. Something is offered, as *quo*. He had worn it himself, spoken of as *se*. He was a warrior, called *bellator*. He had girded himself with it, told with *cinxerat*. That warrior was Ajax, who closes the line as *Aiax*.

Hector receives it, told with *accipit*. It was distinguished, described as *insignem*. Its work was varied, called *uario*. It was worked with engraving, given as *caelamine*. What was distinguished was a belt, which closes the line as *balteum*.

#### Lines 631–632

> 631 Post haec extemplo Graium Troumque cateruae
>
> 632 discedunt caelumque tegit nox atra tenebris.

The scene ends after this, given as *Post haec*. At once something follows, marked by *extemplo*. The Greeks come in, named *Graium*. The Trojans too are joined, called *Troumque*. Their troops close the line as *cateruae*.

The troops depart, told with *discedunt*. The sky also is covered, named *caelumque*. Something covers it, told with *tegit*. What covers it is night, spoken of as *nox*. That night was black, described as *atra*. It covers it with darkness, which closes the line as *tenebris*.
