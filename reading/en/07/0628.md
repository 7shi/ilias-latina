### 628–632 (*Iliad* 7.303–307)

#### Line 628

> 628 et prior Aeaciden aurato munerat ense

More is added, marked by *et*. Someone is first, described as *prior*. Aeacides comes in, named *Aeaciden*. As the commentary says, this descendant of Aeacus is Ajax. Something is gold-adorned, described as *aurato*, though what it is is not yet said. Someone presents Aeacides with something, told with *munerat*. What is gold-adorned is a sword, which closes the line as *ense*.

#### Lines 629–630

> 629 inque uicem, quo se bellator cinxerat Aiax,
>
> 630 accipit insignem uario caelamine balteum.

Something is in return, given as *inque uicem*. Something comes in, as *quo*. Someone himself comes in, as *se*. He is a warrior, named *bellator*. He had girded himself, told with *cinxerat*. The warrior is Ajax, who closes the line as *Aiax*.

Someone receives it, told with *accipit*. Something is distinguished, described as *insignem*. Something is varied, described as *uario*. It is engraving, named *caelamine*. What is distinguished is a belt, which closes the line as *balteum*.

#### Lines 631–632

> 631 Post haec extemplo Graium Troumque cateruae
>
> 632 discedunt caelumque tegit nox atra tenebris.

A time is set, given as *Post haec*. It is at once, marked by *extemplo*. The Greeks come in, named *Graium*. The Trojans as well, named *Troumque*. Their bands close the line as *cateruae*.

The bands depart, told with *discedunt*. The sky comes in as well, named *caelumque*. Something covers it, told with *tegit*. What covers it is night, named *nox*. The night is black, described as *atra*. It covers the sky with darkness, which closes the line as *tenebris*.
