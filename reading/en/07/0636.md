### 636–640 (*Iliad* 7.345–346, 7.350–351, 7.363–364, 7.379)

#### Lines 636–637

> 636 in coetum uenere Phryges. Tunc maximus Hector
>
> 637 cum sociis memorans hesternae funera caedis

An assembly comes in, given as *in coetum*. Some came together, told with *uenere*. Those who came together are the Phrygians, named *Phryges*. As the commentary says, the Phrygians are the Trojans. A time is set, marked by *Tunc*. Someone is mighty, described as *maximus*. The mighty one is Hector, who closes the line as *Hector*.

His companions come in, given as *cum sociis*. He is recalling something, described as *memorans*. Something is of yesterday, described as *hesternae*, though what it is is not yet said. He recalls deaths, named *funera*. What is of yesterday is slaughter, which closes the line as *caedis*.

#### Lines 638–639

> 638 suadet ut inuictis Helene reddatur Achiuis
>
> 639 praedaque quae duros Menelai mulceat ignes

Hector urges something, told with *suadet*. A purpose comes in, marked by *ut*. Some are unconquered, described as *inuictis*, though who they are is not yet said. Helen comes in, named *Helene*. She is to be returned, told with *reddatur*. The unconquered ones are the Achaeans, who close the line as *Achiuis*. As the commentary says, the Achaeans are the Greeks.

Plunder is to be returned as well, named *praedaque*. The plunder is to do something, as *quae*. Something is harsh, described as *duros*, though what it is is not yet said. It belongs to Menelaus, named *Menelai*. The plunder is to soothe it, told with *mulceat*. What is harsh is his fires, which close the line as *ignes*.

#### Line 640

> 640 idque placet cunctis. Tunc saeuo missus Atridae

Hector's proposal comes in, as *idque*. It pleases, told with *placet*. It pleases all, named *cunctis*. A time is set, marked by *Tunc*. Someone is fierce, described as *saeuo*. Someone is sent, described as *missus*. The fierce one, to whom he is sent, is Atrides, who closes the line as *Atridae*. Who is sent comes only in the next section.
