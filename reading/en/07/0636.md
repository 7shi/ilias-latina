### 636–640 (*Iliad* 7.345–346, 7.350–351, 7.363–364, 7.379)

#### Lines 636–637

> 636 in coetum uenere Phryges. Tunc maximus Hector
>
> 637 cum sociis memorans hesternae funera caedis

An assembly comes in, given as *in coetum*. They came together, told with *uenere*. Those who came together are the Phrygians, named *Phryges*. As the commentary says, the Phrygians are the Trojans. A time then is marked by *Tunc*. Someone is mighty, described as *maximus*. The mighty one is Hector, who closes the line as *Hector*.

His companions come in, given as *cum sociis*. He recalls something, as *memorans*. Something was of yesterday, described as *hesternae*, though what it is is not yet said. He recalls deaths, named *funera*. What was of yesterday was slaughter, which closes the line as *caedis*.

#### Lines 638–639

> 638 suadet ut inuictis Helene reddatur Achiuis
>
> 639 praedaque quae duros Menelai mulceat ignes

Hector urges a course, told with *suadet*. What he urges is marked by *ut*. Some were unconquered, described as *inuictis*, though who they are is not yet said. Helen comes in, named *Helene*. She is to be returned, told with *reddatur*. The unconquered ones are the Achaeans, who close the line as *Achiuis*. As the commentary says, the Achaeans are the Greeks.

Plunder too comes in, named *praedaque*. It is offered, as *quae*. Something was harsh, described as *duros*, though what was harsh is not yet said. It belongs to Menelaus, called *Menelai*. The plunder should soothe it, told with *mulceat*. What was harsh was his fires, which close the line as *ignes*.

#### Line 640

> 640 idque placet cunctis. Tunc saeuo missus Atridae

This proposal comes in, as *idque*. It pleases them, told with *placet*. All approve it, called *cunctis*. A time then is marked by *Tunc*. Someone was fierce, described as *saeuo*, though who it is is not yet said. Someone is sent, told with *missus*. The fierce one is Atrides, which closes the line as *Atridae*, though who is sent and what happens comes only in the next section.
