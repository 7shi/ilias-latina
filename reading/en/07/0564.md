### 564–568 (*Iliad* 6.394–395, 6.399–403, 6.466–468)

#### Lines 564–568

> 564 Colloquium petit interea fidissima coniunx
>
> 565 Hectoris Andromache paruumque ad pectora natum
>
> 566 Astyanacta tenet, cuius dum maximus heros
>
> 567 oscula parua petit, subito perterritus infans
>
> 568 conuertit timidos materna ad pectora uultus

Converse comes in, named *Colloquium*. Someone seeks it, told with *petit*. A time meanwhile is marked by *interea*. She is most faithful, described as *fidissima*. The faithful one is a wife, which closes the line as *coniunx*.

She is Hector's wife, called *Hectoris*. Her own name is Andromache, named *Andromache*. Her child too is little, described as *paruumque*. She holds him to her breast, given as *ad pectora*. The little one is her son, which closes the line as *natum*.

The son is Astyanax, named *Astyanacta*. She holds him, told with *tenet*. The child is referred to again, as *cuius*. A time while something happens is marked by *dum*. Someone is mighty, described as *maximus*. The mighty one is a hero, who closes the line as *heros*.

Kisses come in, named *oscula*. They are little, described as *parua*. The hero seeks them, told with *petit*. It happens suddenly, marked by *subito*. Someone is terrified, described as *perterritus*. The terrified one is the infant, who closes the line as *infans*.

He turns away, told with *conuertit*. His gaze was fearful, as *timidos*. It was toward his mother's, described as *materna*. It was to her breast, given as *ad pectora*. What was fearful is his face, which closes the line as *uultus*, though the cause of his terror comes only in the next section.
