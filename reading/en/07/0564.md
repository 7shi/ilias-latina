### 564–568 (*Iliad* 6.394–395, 6.399–403, 6.466–468)

#### Lines 564–568

> 564 Colloquium petit interea fidissima coniunx
>
> 565 Hectoris Andromache paruumque ad pectora natum
>
> 566 Astyanacta tenet, cuius dum maximus heros
>
> 567 oscula parua petit, subito perterritus infans
>
> 568 conuertit timidos materna ad pectora uultus

Converse comes in, named *Colloquium*. Someone seeks it, told with *petit*. It is meanwhile, marked by *interea*. Someone is most faithful, described as *fidissima*. The faithful one is a wife, which closes the line as *coniunx*.

She is Hector's, named *Hectoris*. Her name is Andromache, named *Andromache*. Someone is little as well, described as *paruumque*. It is to her breast, given as *ad pectora*. The little one is her son, which closes the line as *natum*.

The son is Astyanax, named *Astyanacta*. She holds him, told with *tenet*. The child comes back, as *cuius*, though what of his is meant is not yet said. A time is set, marked by *dum*. Someone is mighty, described as *maximus*. The mighty one is a hero, who closes the line as *heros*.

What was the child's is kisses, named *oscula*. They are little, described as *parua*. The hero seeks them, told with *petit*. It is sudden, marked by *subito*. Someone is terrified, described as *perterritus*. The terrified one is the infant, who closes the line as *infans*.

He turns something, told with *conuertit*. Something is fearful, described as *timidos*. Something is his mother's, described as *materna*. It is her breast, given as *ad pectora*. What was fearful is his face, which closes the line as *uultus*. What he fears comes only in the next section.
