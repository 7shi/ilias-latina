### 614–616 (*Iliad* 7.272–273)

#### Lines 614–616

> 614 Quem leuat exceptum Grais inimicus Apollo
>
> 615 integratque animum; iam rursus ad arma coibant
>
> 616 stringebantque iterum gladios, cum fessus in undas

Hector comes back, as *Quem*. Someone lifts him up, told with *leuat*. He was caught as he fell, described as *exceptum*. The Greeks come in, named *Grais*. A god was hostile to them, called *inimicus*. The hostile god is Apollo, who closes the line as *Apollo*.

He restores him as well, told with *integratque*. What he restores is his courage, named *animum*. Already a change comes, marked by *iam*. They move again, as *rursus*. They return to fight, given as *ad arma*. They were coming together, told with *coibant*.

They were drawing weapons too, told with *stringebantque*. They do so a second time, marked by *iterum*. Those weapons are swords, named *gladios*. An interruption arrives, as *cum*. Someone was weary, described as *fessus*, though who it is is not yet said. The waves come in, given as *in undas*, though what enters them comes only in the next section.
